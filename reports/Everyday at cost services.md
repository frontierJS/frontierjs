# Group tools lead the at-cost field

The best everyday service for a small web team to offer at cost is a **single "group kit" for adult organizers**: sign-up sheets, volunteer scheduling, event pages, paid tickets and dues, built so that money flows through each group's own Stripe account. It ranks first across all four directions because the people it serves pay in the most awkward way (fixed tiers of **$108 to $540 a year** for sign-up sheets, **$175 to $300 a year** for a Meetup group, and about **25% in fees on a $10 ticket**), because the build is ordinary CRUD plus email, and because adult-organizer-only design sidesteps most child-safety law. A volunteer-and-learner matching layer for adult ESL conversation partners and tutoring programs runs on the same scheduler and ranks second, since the documented gap there is human capacity (1,000+ people on waitlists in one Maine metro), not content. Per-maker newsletters, memberships and storefronts come next, because flat-fee tools already exist and the team's edge is a lower price and the maker's own payment account. Photo and file storage, which most people name first, ranks near the bottom: Apple and Google already sell about $5 per TB, wholesale costs are rising, and honest redundancy costs more than that. The whole ranking rests on mostly secondary figures (see the method note), and four owner decisions (minors, marketplace versus per-maker tools, the storage promise, a time bank) move specific rows but not the top of the list. The funding model that fits is a flat annual price per group or maker that covers real running costs, Stripe fees passed through with no markup, a sliding-scale option, and grants only for the build.

## Most figures are secondary, so read them as ranges

The four research notes lean on search-result summaries and on comparison blogs, many written by competitors. Vendor pages that did load (Apple, Fastmail, Bitwarden, Backblaze, Hetzner, Squarespace, Gumroad, Ghost, beehiiv, Stripe, Anthropic) are the firmest numbers; Etsy, Meetup, SignUpGenius, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Substack and Patreon fee pages were blocked, so those rates come from third-party guides. Sources conflict in places: SignUpGenius paid tiers appear as $8.99 to $44.99 a month depending on the guide, Meetup's organizer price ranges from $29.99 a month to $299.99 a year, and Google's 2026 storage lineup is unsettled between two reports. Several population figures predate 2022 (the 373,000-congregation count is a 2017 estimate; the Deloitte 51% data-loss figure dates to about 2019; the Linktree creator-earnings survey was fielded in 2021). Where this report does arithmetic or judgment of its own, it says so. Dollar amounts are therefore best read as "about this size", and I treat a number as decision-grade only when a vendor page or a primary filing backs it. The earlier legal, medical and benefits work is deliberately left out; the only borrowed material is the funding and cost-per-user lessons in the precedents note.

## Digital life: storage is priced near cost, the real gap is setup and ownership

Families pay **$5 per TB per month** at the 2 TB and 6 TB tiers from Apple ($9.99 and $29.99) ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108047)) and at Google (2 TB at $9.99) ([Google One](https://one.google.com/about/plans)), shared across five or six people, and Google One alone passed 150 million subscribers ([Wikipedia: Google One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_One)). Own-domain email costs **$60 a year** for one person on Fastmail and **$132** for a six-person family ([Fastmail](https://www.fastmail.com/pricing/)); a Squarespace site is **$19 a month billed annually** ([Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing)); a family password manager runs **$47.88 a year** on Bitwarden ([Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/pricing/)). Microsoft raised its Family plan 30% in January 2025 ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/microsoft-increases-price-of-microsoft-365-bundle-for-consumers-the-first-bump-in-12-years/)). The sums are real but modest, so the savings case is weak, and the better argument is ownership and lock-in: Google can delete an account inactive for two years ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546)), 63% of Americans cannot tell sync from backup in one vendor-run survey ([Handy Recovery](https://www.handyrecovery.com/data-backup-statistics/)), and a trade survey reports 48% have no instructions for their digital accounts ([AfterYou, citing Trust & Will](https://blog.afteryou.me/digital-legacy-planning-online-accounts-after-death/)).

The free and co-op alternatives are strong on software and weak on operations. Immich has **119,000 GitHub stars** and a full-time team funded by FUTO, yet users report breaking upgrades and one commenter says you "need to be a web dev or a sys admin" ([GitHub](https://github.com/immich-app/immich), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46172664)). Ente sells hosted, encrypted photo storage with three replicas at **$9.99 a month for 1 TB** ([Ente](https://ente.com/pricing)), which is above Apple's per-TB price and shows what redundancy costs. Volunteer-run hosts burn out: Mastodon instances close over moderation load ([GFSC](https://gfsc.community/why-we-discontinued-our-mastodon-server/)). The models that last pay at least one person, such as Framasoft (donations were 94% of 2025 income, 74% of spending is staff) ([Framasoft](https://framasoft.org/en/)) and May First (dues, three workers) ([May First](https://mayfirst.coop/membership-options/plans-pricing/)).

For a small team the cost picture splits. A Hetzner Storage Box is about **€2 per TB** (BX21: 5 TB at €10.90; BX41: 20 TB at €40.60) ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/storage/storage-box/)), but Backblaze B2 is **$6.95 per TB** ([Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)), and the notes' own arithmetic puts a three-copy scheme at roughly **$9 to $12 per TB**, above Apple and Google. Hetzner and Backblaze both raised prices in 2026 ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/hetzner-to-raise-prices-by-up-to-37-percent-from-april-1)), so an at-cost price must be built on current wholesale numbers. Email is harder than storage: Gmail requires SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for bulk senders, a spam-complaint rate under 0.30%, and a matching PTR record ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)), and the real cost is reputation and abuse handling rather than hardware. The sensible shape is resale of an existing host: Migadu sells accounts from **$19 a year** ([Migadu](https://www.migadu.com/pricing/)) and Purelymail charges about $10 a year plus $1 per mailbox (a [third-party comparison](https://markrelay.com/research/custom-domain-email-hosting-pricing-compared)), so the team's wedge is doing the DNS and setup, not running mail servers. A non-custodial "digital legacy and account kit" (a checklist and worksheet that never stores secrets) is cheap to build because Google's Inactive Account Manager and Apple's Legacy Contact already exist and the gap is setup. Support is the largest unmetered cost across this whole direction; the notes found no per-user figure, and data-loss liability and, for any hosted uploads, the provider duties around illegal child-abuse imagery were not researched and need review before a storage launch.

## Community: groups overpay in fixed tiers and fee drag

This direction has the clearest overcharge and the largest base. The notes' best count is about **373,000 US congregations** with a median of 70 regular participants (a 2017 estimate) ([Hartford Institute](https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/fast-facts-on-american-religion/)), **27.3 million children** in organized sports ([Project Play](https://projectplay.org/youth-sports/facts/participation-rates)), 300,000+ Meetup groups worldwide ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/blog/2025-meetup-progress-report/)), and 7.5 million Buy Nothing members ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project)). What they pay is what makes the case: SignUpGenius paid plans cost about $108, $270 and $540 a year and mostly buy ad removal and counts ([SignUpKit](https://www.signupkit.co/signupgenius-pricing), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135392/SignUpGenius/)); Meetup roughly tripled organizer prices after Bending Spoons bought it, to about **$175 a year** for a small group in the help-center summary ([Meetup help via HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40854275)); Eventbrite takes about **6.6% plus $1.79 per ticket**, about $5.09 on a $50 ticket ([Eventcloud](https://www.eventcloud.io/blog/how-much-does-eventbrite-charge-2026)), which the creators note works out to 24.5% on a $10 ticket; TeamSnap's club tier was last published at $199 a month ([Clubside](https://clubside.io/blog/teamsnap-alternative)); church software runs $864 a year at Breeze to roughly $1,680 for a small-church suite, and card processing on $100,000 of giving costs about $3,050 ([Church Stack](https://www.churchstack.io/blog/average-cost-church-management-software)).

Alternatives exist but fragment the job. Mobilizon has 97,210 accounts across 134 servers but less polish than Eventbrite ([Wbcom](https://wbcomdesigns.com/mobilizon-review/)); Hylo has a free tier with 1 GB storage and Pro at an estimated $60 a month "coming soon" ([Hylo](https://www.hylo.com/pricing)); Lend Engine's free tier covers 100 items and 100 members and its paid plans cost $150 to $600 a year by the notes' arithmetic ([Lend Engine](https://www.lend-engine.com/pricing)). No post-mortem explains why the open-source tools stall; the notes infer that a group must stitch together events, decisions and money, lacks someone to self-host, and follows its members to Facebook or group text. Buy Nothing is the trust warning: automated trademark takedowns removed a 116,000-member San Francisco group in October 2025, and its three admins spend about **3 hours a day** moderating ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/11/07/buy-nothing-group-facebook-taken-down-trademark-infringment/)).

What a small team carries here is dominated by payments. Stripe charges 2.9% plus 30 cents, plus $15 per dispute ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/pricing)); no operator removes that, so "at cost" means passing it through. The nonprofit discount (2.2% plus 30 cents) does not apply to tickets, registrations or memberships ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/fee-discount-for-nonprofit-organizations)). Charging in each group's own Stripe account keeps the sales-tax and 1099-K treatment with the group, and keeps the operator out of the facilitator rules (a design inference that a tax advisor should confirm). The adult-organizer design (parents register children, no accounts under 13, no child photos or messaging) gives the lightest legal footprint: the amended COPPA rule has been in full effect since **April 22, 2026**, with verifiable parental consent, retention limits and written security programs ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)), and the Safe Sport Act obliges amateur youth organizations to report suspected abuse within 24 hours, train adults and limit one-on-one contact ([Sadler Sports Law](https://www.sadlersports.com/new-safe-sport-act-applies-amateur-sports-organizations/)). That reach claim comes from law-firm summaries, not statute text. League registration (the biggest dollar pain, with family costs up 46% since 2019 per [Project Play](https://projectplay.org/state-of-play-2025/participation-trends)) is therefore the highest-value and highest-exposure candidate, and the one most sensitive to the minors decision below. Congregation management and giving is a large market but competes with Planning Center's free People module and adds tax receipts and payments volume ([ChurchPosting](https://churchposting.com/resources/software/planning-center-vs-breeze-vs-churchtrac/)).

## Learning: the bottleneck is human hours, so build scheduling around volunteers

Cost gates some learning, but the evidence says capacity and uptake gate more. Tutoring runs **$25 to $80 an hour** and music lessons about **$170 a month** weekly at 30 minutes ([Wise](https://www.wise.live/blog/average-tutoring-rate-in-the-usa/), [Ensemble Schools](https://www.ensembleschools.com/blog/cost-of-private-music-lessons/)), yet one 2026 study finds the lowest-income families receive more tutoring overall once school-provided help is counted ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/richest-families-outspend-poorest-13-to-1-per-child-schools-equalizer/)). Adult ESL is the cleanest capacity gap: **51% of ProLiteracy programs keep waiting lists and under 10% of adults in need are served** ([ProLiteracy](https://www.proliteracy.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2023-PL-AdultBasicEducation-FactSheet-2467.pdf)), and Portland, Maine had about 1,300 enrolled and at least 1,000 more waiting ([Press Herald](https://www.pressherald.com/2024/11/22/demand-for-english-adult-education-courses-outpaces-class-space-in-portland-region/)). Free content is plentiful but uptake is thin: drop-in tutoring is used by 1% to 5% of eligible students ([KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/62572/schools-keep-buying-online-drop-in-tutoring-research-doesnt-support-it)), and in a two-year Tennessee trial Khanmigo added nothing over Khan practice because students barely engaged ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551)). Community music schools already subsidize heavily (about 70% of students aided at Springfield) ([CMS Springfield](https://www.communitymusicschool.com/impact/)), so software should serve them rather than compete.

The software-shaped pieces follow from that. A volunteer and learner scheduler with recurring slots, reminders and match check-ins attacks the retention problem (mentoring matches that end early last about 7.4 months against 25.1 for full matches) ([Am J Community Psychol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajcp.12585)), and the same engine can pair adult ESL conversation partners with waitlisted learners at the organization's request. A cheap AI add-on that assists human volunteers is documented at about **$20 per tutor per year** in API cost in the Tutor CoPilot trial ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017)), and Claude Haiku 5.5 now lists at $0.10 and $0.50 per million input and output tokens ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), so inference is a rounding error beside human costs. Guardrails matter: unguarded GPT-4 hurt later unaided scores by 17% in a Turkish high-school trial ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)). The expensive items are screening and moderation: background checks run roughly **$19 to $95 per volunteer** ([VCU](https://equity.vcu.edu/protection-of-minors/background-check-platform/), [Checkr](https://checkr.com/use-cases/volunteers)), chat and video reports need a human, and anything involving minors needs the consent flows above plus legal review the notes did not cover. An instrument and equipment lending ledger for music programs is a small, useful second piece, but the notes found no data on instrument-lending economics. Bootcamps are a poor candidate: tuition near $14,000, audited outcomes near 70%, and a contracting market with closures through 2025 ([Course Report](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/2025-year-in-review-coding-bootcamp-news)).

## Creators and makers: flat-fee tools beat margins, marketplaces keep failing

The overcharge is real at small ticket sizes. Stripe's 30 cents alone takes 6% of a $5 payment, and by the notes' arithmetic a $5 monthly supporter nets the creator about **81%** on Patreon (10% fee plus processing) ([Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/pricing)), Substack ([Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/blog/what-substack-is-and-how-you-can-use-it-for-subscriptions-or-email-marketing/)) and Buy Me a Coffee, and about 91% on Ghost or beehiiv. A $25 Etsy item costs about **11%** in fees and about **26%** when an Offsite Ad drove the sale ([FeeProofed](https://www.feeproofed.com/guides/etsy-fee-increase-history-2018-2026/), notes' arithmetic from [checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees)); Gumroad takes 10% plus 50 cents direct and 30% through Discover ([Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/pricing)). The population is large but low-earning: Etsy's marketplace had **5.6 million active sellers** in 2025 ([Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)), while a 2021 Linktree survey found 68% of part-time creators earn under $1,000 a year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/linktree-creator-economy-report-research/)). That favors per-sale or low-flat pricing over $15 to $30 monthly plans.

The at-cost pattern already works for tools and has failed for destinations. Ghost ($18 to $29 a month, 0% on subscriptions) ([Ghost](https://ghost.org/pricing/)), beehiiv Lite, Buttondown (about $9 at 1,000 subscribers) and Micro.blog ($1 to $20) persist on plain subscriptions, whereas the artist co-op Ampled closed in 2023 citing burnout and no money to hire ([Billboard Canada](https://ca.billboard.com/ampled-music-platform)), Resonate closed after 2017 to 2024 ([Resonate](https://resonate.coop/)), and Open Collective Foundation dissolved in 2024, reportedly because a 5% host fee could not cover compliance ([Open Collective](https://blog.opencollective.com/open-collective-official-statement-ocf-dissolution/)). The lesson is that infrastructure is cheap (AWS SES sends 1,000 emails for $0.10, so a 10,000-subscriber weekly newsletter costs about $4 a month in sends) ([AWS SES](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)) and that running a marketplace is the expensive part. Marketplace-facilitator sales-tax laws exist in 45 states and DC, mostly with $100,000 thresholds and $500,000 in Texas and California ([Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/blog/marketplace-facilitator-laws-by-state)), and 1099-K reporting reverted to $20,000 and 200 transactions ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/form-1099-k-faqs)). A per-maker newsletter, membership page or storefront in the maker's own Stripe account sidesteps both, though the notes found no source on whether a facilitator duty attaches to a software host that never holds funds. Honest limits: makers who lose Etsy's search traffic can lose more than 11% in revenue, so owning the audience pays only for makers who already have one, and podcast hosting is unpriced because the notes found no audio storage or bandwidth figures.

## One ranking: value times feasibility, with four shared builds

I scored each candidate 1 to 5 on value (how many people, how much they overpay or are shut out) and 1 to 5 on feasibility for a few developers who build and self-host fullstack apps (running cost, support, moderation, payments and tax, child-safety exposure). These are judgments built on the figures above, not measurements, and they assume the adult-organizer and per-maker choices in the lean column of the next section.

| 3 | Small-congregation management and giving | Community | 4 | 3 | 12 | $864 to $1,680 a year plus processing; free Planning Center People competes; receipts and payment volume |

The ranking reconciles across directions because the candidates share infrastructure, so effort adds up rather than splitting. One **group build** (organizations, roles, slots, reminders, event pages, per-group Stripe, email) delivers ranks 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and, with a ledger table, the time bank; that is the single highest-leverage investment. A thin **ledger build** (items or hours, check-out, balances) covers lending, instrument loans, the give-away board and the time bank. A **maker build** (per-maker Stripe, a sending domain, static pages) delivers newsletters, memberships, storefronts, and, together with the group build's event pages, tickets. The **home build** (storage, mail, vault) shares nothing with the rest and carries the heaviest operations, so it ranks last despite being what people picture first. The practical order is the group build first, the maker build second, and the home build only after a funded operating base exists. Three things would flip rows: opening to minors lifts league registration and tutoring (and their costs), a firm tax opinion could lift the marketplace, and a cheap two-copy storage design could lift storage to the middle of the table.

## Four owner decisions that move the ranking

These four forks are the owner's, not mine. Each lists the options and the researchers' lean; the leans are not rulings.

**Admitting minors (COPPA and SafeSport exposure) or adult-organizer-only tools.** Option A is adult organizers only: parents register children, no accounts under 13, no child photos or messaging. Option B admits minors through organizations or schools, which needs verifiable parental consent under the amended COPPA rule, Safe Sport training and 24-hour reporting duties, volunteer screening at roughly $19 to $95 each, and legal review of consent flows. Option C serves minors directly, which no source supports for a small team. The community and learning researchers both lean to **A for the first release**, which keeps league registration at a modest score and leaves tutoring to adult learners and org-run programs. The notes flag that A still needs legal review (parent-account data on children is still children's data), and that neither researcher fetched state mandated-reporter or online-tutoring statutes.

**A per-maker storefront or newsletter, or a shared marketplace (marketplace-facilitator sales tax and 1099-K duties).** Option A runs each maker on their own Stripe account with their own tax and 1099-K treatment. Option B operates a shared marketplace and takes on facilitator collection in 45 states and DC, thresholds from $100,000 to $500,000, and 1099-K reporting at $20,000 and 200 transactions. Option C is per-maker tools plus an optional directory that never touches money. The creators researcher leans to **A**, ranking marketplaces last on the strength of Ampled's and Resonate's closures, and states that it is a judgment for the owner and that a tax advisor must confirm whether a facilitator duty reaches a software host that does not hold funds.

**The storage redundancy promise, since three copies may cost more per TB than Apple's or Google's roughly $5.** Option A sells a single Hetzner copy at about €2 per TB, labeled clearly as not a backup, with an open file layout and the 3-2-1 message; it undercuts Apple but loses data on a failure. Option B holds two copies (Hetzner plus Backblaze), which approaches the notes' $9 to $12 per TB for three copies. Option C promises three copies at an honest price above $5, sold on ownership, with no price advantage. Option D skips hosting and offers a backup-and-handover guide. The digital-life researcher's reading points to **limited replication stated plainly, plus an exportable layout**, because a three-copy promise cannot beat $5 per TB, and it flags that no source measures support burden or data-loss liability for hobby hosts.

**Whether to build a time bank, given that hOurworld exists.** Option A builds a standalone ledger, competing with a free incumbent that counts 27,541 members in 303 communities ([hOurworld](https://www.hourworld.org/index.htm)) after reporting 44,000 members in 2019 ([Salon](https://www.salon.com/2019/04/06/time-really-is-money-with-increasingly-popular-cooperative-time-banks_partner/)). Option B skips it and points groups to hOurworld. Option C offers hours as a feature of the matching and lending builds, so a volunteer's hours are credited where they are worked. The learning researcher leans to **C only**, since time banks have modest scale and a free incumbent.

## A flat price per group, a small paid floor and grants for the build

The funding precedents point one way: pay the people and do not rely on one donor. Disroot ran on about **€25,000 in 2023 from an average 197 monthly supporters** ([Disroot](https://disroot.org/annual_reports/AnnualReport2023.pdf)); Social.coop budgets about £12,600 a year on 915 contributors giving £1 to £10 a month ([Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/socialcoop)); May First charges **$50 a year** for basic membership and employs three workers ([May First](https://mayfirst.coop/membership-options/plans-pricing/)). Concentration kills: Dollar For lost about a third of its budget when two donors left in early 2025 ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)), and Benefits Data Trust's $20 million gift did not cure a structural deficit ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)). Fixed costs divided by few users dominate cost per person: Direct File's reported **$138 per accepted return** against an earlier $9 to $16 projection is the warning for any small service ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-ends-direct-file-program)). Upsolve shows the blend that holds: about 70% grants and donations and about 30% earned revenue ([Upsolve](https://upsolve.org/learn/how-upsolve-is-free/)). Fiscal hosting at a thin 5% failed at Open Collective Foundation, so any entity must price in the compliance load; entity choice (nonprofit, benefit corporation, co-op) was not researched and is open.

For this team and these candidates, the fitting model is a **flat annual price per group or maker, set to cover real costs**, with Stripe fees passed through at the published rate and no markup, a free tier sized like Lend Engine's (100 items, 100 members), a sliding scale and voting seat on the May First pattern for tiny groups, and grants or one-time gifts only for the build, with a cap on any single funder's share. My arithmetic for scale: a **$60 annual plan**, about half the cheapest paid SignUpGenius tier, needs roughly 1,700 paying groups per $100,000 of annual budget, about 0.5% of US congregations alone; the team knows its own payroll and can rerun that sum. Running cost per group is mostly email and support rather than compute (SES is $0.10 per 1,000 messages), but the notes gave no measured per-user support figure, so a pilot with a few dozen groups should measure support hours before any price is promised. Cyber and liability insurance is a real line item, about $1,200 to $5,000 a year for a small data-holding nonprofit in insurer estimates ([Hotaling Insurance](https://hotalinginsurance.com/hotaling-insurance-blog/nonprofit-cyber-liability-insurance)). Ghost, which sells flat subscriptions over open code, and Immich under FUTO are the two precedents closest to this shape.

## Conclusion

The sharpest insight is that the lighter-feeling services are not the cheapest to run. The things people name first (photos, email, a Dropbox replacement) are priced near cost already and carry the heaviest operations, while the unglamorous group tools carry the biggest overcharge and the least operational risk. Because the top candidates share one scheduler, one ledger and one per-account payment pattern, a team of a few developers gets six or seven products from roughly two builds, and the order of work matters more than the choice of any single product.

What would change this answer is mostly outside the notes: a measured support-hours figure from a pilot, a tax adviser's view on facilitator duties for a software host, a legal read on adult-organizer-only design for youth leagues, and a real wholesale quote for two-copy storage. The first release should test the group kit on 20 to 50 adult-run groups at a flat low price, because that experiment answers the support, payments and moderation questions that no source in the notes could.
