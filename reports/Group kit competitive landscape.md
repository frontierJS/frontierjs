# No rival bundles the at-cost group kit

**No source found a single US tool that bundles sign-ups, volunteer scheduling, event pages, paid tickets and dues, with payments landing in the group's own Stripe account at cost.** The pieces exist separately. Luma Plus already reaches 0% platform fee on tickets with funds going to the host's own Stripe, but it costs $59 a month and has no volunteer scheduling in any page retrieved. Heylo is the closest bundle (events, member directory, chat, payments, unlimited members on a free tier), yet it takes 5% + $0.59 per transaction on that tier and no source says where its payouts go. Zeffy is free with no card fee, but it is built for nonprofits, needs an EIN, holds the Stripe relationship, and is funded by donor tips that draw the most repeated complaints in the category. SignUpGenius and SignUp.com own sign-ups and volunteer sheets and show ads even on paid tiers; SignUpGenius adds 5% + $0.50 on payments. The open ground is the combination: a Stripe-direct kit with no platform cut and no tip prompt, usable by groups that are not 501(c)(3)s. An entrant must match raw Stripe pricing (2.9% + $0.30, or ACH at 0.8% capped at $5), ad-free pages with no member account, email reminders, slot caps with waitlists, multiple organizers on the free tier, and recurring events on day one. The evidence is thin in places, since most figures come from search summaries or competitor blogs, so the claim that the gap is open is a finding about what the researchers could see, not proof that no such tool exists.

## Method and evidence limits

Four researchers covered sign-ups and volunteers, events and ticketing, dues and money, and all-in-one apps in a single pass of about 40 tool calls each on 2026-11-15. Most figures come from search-engine summaries or from competitor-written blogs (Zelos, Zeffy, Givebutter, Hi.Events, Luma's own comparison page and similar), which have reason to slant. Reddit, G2, Capterra and Software Advice could not be reached directly, so user complaints come from Trustpilot, BBB, app stores and quotations relayed by other sites; the researchers flagged each as third-party or possibly stale and this report keeps those flags. Several official pricing pages failed to load, including SignUpGenius, Doodle, Meetup, ClubExpress and PayPal, so third-party figures stand in and are marked. The fee tables below are this report's arithmetic from the published rate cards, assuming Stripe's standard 2.9% + $0.30 card rate ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)), no refunds and no disputes. The earlier report that chose the group kit is background only and its ranking is not repeated here.

## The strongest incumbents are narrow, taxed or tip-funded

**Luma** is the strongest honest competitor on tickets. Its Plus tier gives 0% platform fee, funds into the host's own Stripe account on a rolling schedule, recurring events, waitlists, approval-required registration, coupons, check-in, CSV export and paid memberships ([Luma](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-vs-partiful)). It reportedly grew as the default event page for tech and startup communities with a team of about seven ([Inc. headline via search summary](https://www.inc.com/rebecca-deczynski/meet-the-seven-person-startup-powering-silicon-valleys-event-boom/91408964)). What it lacks for this kit is any volunteer scheduling or sign-up sheet in the pages retrieved, a free tier that is at cost (the free tier takes 5%), and a price below $59 a month for the 0% rate. No organizer or attendee complaint corpus for Luma could be retrieved, so its weak points are unknown from this evidence.

**Heylo** is the closest bundle and the researchers' biggest open question. It offers unlimited members, admins, events and RSVPs on every tier, member directories and group chats on the free tier, and a Meetup-switcher story; its own testimonials include a track club reporting paid members up 3x to over 600 ([Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up), vendor marketing). A homeschool organizer's case study says Meetup roughly doubled in price on about a month's notice and that moving Discord chat into Heylo raised participation ([Heylo blog](https://www.heylo.com/blog/meetup-move-homeschool-group), vendor-published). But **the researchers could not confirm whether Heylo pays out to the group's own Stripe account or to a Heylo-held balance**, found no volunteer scheduling feature on any Heylo page, and found no independent complaints, so Heylo is both the strongest rival and the least verified.

**Zeffy** is free on cost to the group, covers tickets, memberships, donations and receipts, and requires an EIN in the US ([Zeffy](https://www.zeffy.com/pricing)). It is funded by tips, and the complaints cluster there: a pre-selected 17% tip on $50 that the nonprofit cannot change, reports of funds held for weeks, and a Reddit-relayed parent report that a school nonprofit now tells every parent to set the tip to 0% ([Nonprofit Software Guide](https://nonprofitsoftwareguide.com/blog/zeffy-tip-model-explained/), [search summary](https://checkthat.ai/brands/zeffy/reviews)). Zeffy says holds occur when Stripe needs to verify details ([search summary](https://www.zeffy.com/compare/zeffy-vs-stripe)); the structural point is that the platform sets payout timing on an account created through it. Whether Zeffy or Givebutter accept unincorporated clubs at all was not confirmed.

**SignUpGenius and SignUp.com** are the sign-up incumbents and the clearest source of organizer irritation. Trustpilot reviewers complain of ads "every 10 secs," ads on a paid trial, price, and clunky mobile creation, though ratings remain high at 4.3 from 4,132 reviews ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/signupgenius.com)). SignUp.com shows an "ad-free upgrade available" line on paid tiers, so ads persist after paying ([vendor](https://signup.com/pricing)). SignUpGenius already routes payments through the organizer's own Stripe, so "own Stripe" is not new; the difference is the 5% + $0.50 on top. Ownership matters for price risk: SignUpGenius sits inside Lumaverse, a PE-backed roll-up that also lists Membership Toolkit and a 2020 "Group Management Platform" launch in its portfolio ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200625005173/en/SignUpGenius-Joins-Technology-Leaders-to-Launch-New-Group-Management-Platform)), and Bending Spoons' repricing of Meetup (organizer fees reportedly up from $24 to $45 a month, per Morning Brew as relayed by [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)) and ownership of Eventbrite show the pattern organizers fear. The researchers found no confirmed 2026 price hike at SignUpGenius, however.

**Church suites** are the counterexample to the claim of an empty field. Planning Center is modular and, per third-party sources, covers groups, registrations, calendar, giving, a member app and shift scheduling in its Services module at roughly $10 to $20 per module ([ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/tithely-vs-planning-center/)), with card processing at 2.15% + $0.30. They are shaped for congregations, not neutral adult groups, and were not researched in depth, but they prove that organizations pay for the bundle and set a price anchor.

## The open ground is a combination, not a feature

Each missing piece is documented, and the pattern across them is that the gap is a pricing-plus-bundle gap rather than a messaging gap. Free tools lack payments; tools with payments tax them (SignUpGenius 5% + $0.50, Meal Train 7.9% + $0.30, Cheddar Up 3.95% + $0.95 on its free plan, Heylo 5% + $0.59); tools without a platform cut either charge a subscription (Luma Plus at $59 a month, MembershipWorks at $35 and up) or are nonprofit-only and tip-funded (Zeffy). Ads persist on paid sign-up tiers at both incumbents. Volunteer scheduling did not appear as a feature on any events, ticketing, dues or bundled-app page retrieved, other than church suites and BAND's partial sign-ups, though the researchers did not verify this product by product.

Four specific holes follow from the notes, and the researchers state them as inference rather than finding. First, **groups that are not 501(c)(3)s**: the pages fetched are priced for "nonprofits" or "organizations" and none says how an unincorporated club passes onboarding. Stripe requires a verifiable tax ID and the SSN of associated individuals even when an EIN is used ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/signing-up-for-a-us-stripe-account-without-a-tax-id-or-employer-id-number)), so a volunteer's identity attaches to the account, and a Stripe nonprofit rate of 2.2% + $0.30 reportedly requires a registered 501(c)(3) with 80% of volume as donations, which dues and tickets may not meet ([Givebutter blog](https://givebutter.com/blog/stripe-for-nonprofits), a competitor). Second, **who holds the money**: the tip-funded tools hold the connected account and control payouts, and hold complaints cluster at platform-held payouts such as Zeffy and Humanitix's default. Third, **treasurer turnover**: no source addresses handing a Stripe account, a roster and a payment history to the next volunteer, and Venmo or Zelle leaves records in a personal account. Fourth, **tip-free, ad-free pricing** that stays honest at small ticket sizes, since the percentage-plus-flat structure of every tool above punishes $10 payments.

The evidence limits on the claim that nothing bundles all of this are specific. Heylo's payout model is unverified, and its volunteer scheduling was not found rather than confirmed absent. Who's In, TinyClub and Invyt were seen only through vendor marketing or third-party summaries. Planning Center, Breeze, TeamSnap, Konstella, Membership Toolkit, Eventeny, Posh and Splash were not researched. Reddit, G2 and Capterra could not be read, so demand-side validation of what organizers ask for most is missing, and no source tested whether organizers would accept an at-cost model. The legal exposure of a treasurer for chargebacks on a club with no legal entity was flagged for a lawyer and not researched. Treat the open ground as a well-supported hypothesis, not a settled fact.

## What an at-cost entrant must match on day one

## Five owner decisions, with options and the researchers' lean

These are the owner's calls. The report lists options and the lean the researchers gave, and decides nothing.

**1. The Stripe account model.** Option A is legacy Standard, where the group has a full Stripe Dashboard, direct charges, and fraud and dispute liability on its own account. Option B is Express, with Stripe-hosted onboarding, a lighter dashboard and platform liability, but Stripe says there is an additional cost for Express or Custom accounts. Option C is controller properties (or the Accounts v2 API) configured to behave like Standard. Stripe's documentation now labels Standard, Express and Custom as legacy account types and steers new platforms to controller properties or Accounts v2 ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts)). **The researchers' lean is C**, because it gives the cleanest "your account, your dashboard" story and the lowest platform cost, while following Stripe's current vocabulary. The cost is that the group completes its own verification and bears dispute liability, which matters for a volunteer treasurer; the researchers did not resolve whether a treasurer is personally liable for chargebacks on a club with no legal entity.

**2. Whether to require nonprofit verification for payments.** Option A requires proof of nonprofit status, as Zeffy requires an EIN. Option B requires none, as Cheddar Up does not. The notes give no lean. The tension, in this report's reading, is that the open ground sits with groups that are not 501(c)(3)s, so a gate would remove the main separation from Zeffy, while an open door brings identity checks and dispute exposure the platform does not control. Stripe's nonprofit rate likely does not apply to dues and tickets either way.

**3. SMS at cost passthrough versus deferring it.** Option A offers SMS with carrier and per-message cost passed through at cost. Option B defers it and uses email first, with a text link later. The sign-ups researcher marks SMS as cost-bearing, carrier-fee-bound and gated to paid tiers elsewhere (SignUp.com Starter at $99.99 a year includes unlimited SMS; SignUpGenius includes 150 to 500 texts a month on paid tiers), and the all-in-one researcher lists email first and text link second. **The notes lean toward deferral.** Counterweight: GroupMe ended two-way SMS in August 2026, so text-only members lose a path elsewhere ([RallyText](https://rallytext.app/groupme-sms-shutdown), a competitor, verify against GroupMe's own notice).

**4. Building polls versus linking out.** Option A links to Rallly (free for 3 open polls, no login for voters) or When2meet (free, no account, dated interface) ([Rallly](https://rallly.co/pricing)). Option B builds a bare date vote on the sheet. Option C builds full polls. The notes lean toward not chasing polls beyond a simple date vote, because Rallly shows how little a poll needs and is a thin competitor; the cost of linking out is a handoff off the kit and a dependency on Rallly's free-tier limits and its early-adopter pricing note.

**5. Deferring chat.** Option A defers chat, since members already sit in WhatsApp, GroupMe, Facebook or Discord and replacing it is the heaviest app-install ask. Option B builds a basic chat to match Heylo. **The lean is to defer.** The counter-evidence is Heylo's own case study, where moving Discord chat into the app raised participation ([Heylo blog](https://www.heylo.com/blog/meetup-move-homeschool-group), vendor-published), and the evidence that Discord is hard for older members is anecdotal ([Siit](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/discord-review)).

## Conclusion

The competitive field is not empty; it is fragmented along a single line. Every tool that does one job well taxes payments, locks the useful bits behind a subscription, or passes the cost to the payer as a tip, and the one product that reaches Stripe's cost on tickets (Luma Plus) does so for $59 a month and without shifts. That makes the entrant's edge a combination of price and scope, not a feature: Stripe-direct money, no platform cut, no tip, no ads, and the sign-up sheet and the dues roster living in the same product for groups that are not registered charities. The edge is narrower than it looks, because Luma and Heylo move fast and Heylo's real payout model is unknown.

Two things change what the owner should do next. The unverified Heylo payout model is the cheapest fact to check before committing, since it decides whether the "own Stripe" claim is a differentiator or a tie. And the unincorporated-club onboarding path, including treasurer handover and who bears disputes, is untested in every source and is where the open ground is most likely to be won or lost.
