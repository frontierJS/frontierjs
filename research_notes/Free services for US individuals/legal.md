# US Civil Justice Gap: Tasks Software Could Deliver Free or At Cost

Method note: about 38 tool calls. Primary pages I fetched and read: the LSC 2022 press release, the Stanford CLP 2025 piece, the ABA Journal Utah article, the LawNext LSC "Next Frontier" article, and the Supreme Court docket for Upsolve. Everything else comes from WebSearch result summaries and has NOT been checked against the underlying page. Where the underlying source is a commercial blog (law-firm marketing, comparison sites), I say so. Figures older than 2022 are marked **[PRE-2022]**.

## 1. How big is the civil justice gap? (LSC study, self-representation rates, default rates)

### Takeaway
The latest LSC Justice Gap study is still the April 2022 edition (92% of low-income Americans' substantial civil legal problems got no or insufficient help); I found no newer edition as of Nov 2026. Self-representation is the norm in the high-volume case types: roughly three-quarters of civil cases in the best multi-court sample (2015 data) had a self-represented party, and tenants and debtors are almost never represented. Default judgments are the typical outcome in debt collection and a large minority of evictions.

### Cited Findings
- LSC's 2022 Justice Gap study (NORC at U. Chicago; fourth edition): 92% of civil legal problems reported by low-income Americans that substantially affected them got no or not enough legal help. 2017 edition said 86% with slightly different methodology. — [LSC press release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/low-income-americans-face-immense-justice-gap-according-new-legal-services-corporation-report); [LSC exec summary](https://justicegap.lsc.gov/resource/executive-summary-2022/)
- 74% of low-income households had at least one civil legal problem in the past year; 39% had five or more. — [LSC press release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/low-income-americans-face-immense-justice-gap-according-new-legal-services-corporation-report)
- LSC-funded organizations received ~1.9 million problems in 2021 from low-income individuals and could not give any or enough help for ~1.4 million; about half of eligible requests were turned away, unchanged since 2005. — [LSC press release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/low-income-americans-face-immense-justice-gap-according-new-legal-services-corporation-report)
- Only 28% of respondents agreed people like them are treated fairly by the civil justice system. — [LSC press release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/low-income-americans-face-immense-justice-gap-according-new-legal-services-corporation-report)
- Secondary summary (search result, not verified on the LSC page; the press-release fetch did NOT contain these): low-income Americans seek help for only 25% of problems; 46% of non-seekers cited cost; subgroups: rural 94%, disabled 91%, households with children 90%. — [LSC Justice Gap research page via search](https://www.lsc.gov/initiatives/justice-gap-research)
- No newer edition found. A Jan 2025 LawNext article reportedly said LSC was preparing an update for later in 2025; I could not confirm it was published. LSC's most recent related pieces: "The Next Frontier" technology report with 32 Technology Initiative Grants totaling $4.2M (2025), and a FY2027 budget request of $2.1B. — [LawNext on LSC tech blueprint](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/lsc-issues-blueprint-for-narrowing-the-justice-gap-through-technology-innovation-in-civil-legal-services.html); [LSC grants release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/lsc-awards-42m-technology-grants-and-releases-tech-summit-report); [LSC Annual Report 2025](https://www.lsc.gov/annual-report-2025)
- LSC Next Frontier: grantees turned away 1 in 2 requests; Technology Initiative Grants since 2000: 923 grants, >$92M; application-to-start takes 10-12 months; Recommendation 7 is to fund self-help tools incl. document assembly, chatbots and triage for self-represented litigants. Lone Star Legal Aid's guided online interviews have helped >100,000 people. — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/lsc-issues-blueprint-for-narrowing-the-justice-gap-through-technology-innovation-in-civil-legal-services.html)
- NCSC "Landscape of Civil Litigation in State Courts" **[PRE-2022; 2015 data, 925,344 cases, 10 urban counties]**: at least one party self-represented in >75% of cases (usually the defendant); debt collection, landlord/tenant and foreclosure were 39%/27%/17% of the sample; defendant representation in contract cases ~26% vs 94% in 1992 (per HLR Forum); default judgments ~20% of dispositions overall. — [Search summary citing NCSC/HLR](https://harvardlawreview.org/forum/no-volume/decreasing-supply-to-the-assembly-line-of-debt-collection-litigation/); [SJI summary](https://www.sji.gov/new-report-on-the-landscape-of-civil-litigation-in-state-courts/); [W&M copy](https://scholarship.law.wm.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3429&context=facpubs)
- State-reported ranges (older, mixed sources): 70-98% of family, DV, landlord-tenant and small-claims cases involve at least one unrepresented litigant; 95% of parents in child support matters unrepresented (2015 NCSC). — [GW Law faculty paper](https://scholarship.law.gwu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?httpsredir=1&article=2383&context=faculty_publications)
- Family law pro se estimates: 67% California, 73% large Florida counties, 70% some Wisconsin counties (undated Wikipedia aggregation, weak). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_se_legal_representation_in_the_United_States)
- Eviction: tenant representation about 4%, landlords about 83-84% (Nov 2024). Where right to counsel exists: NYC 42% of tenants represented FY2024; Cleveland 16% of eligible by end of 2024; Virginia median 0.25% (Q2 2024). — [Pro Bono Institute](https://www.probonoinst.org/2025/07/21/sustaining-tenants-right-to-counsel/); [NCCRC](https://civilrighttocounsel.org/resources/organizing_around_right_to_counsel/)
- Eviction volume: Eviction Tracking System (38 city areas + 10 states only) counted 1.23M filings in 2025 vs 1.26M in 2024. National filings ~3.6M/yr, ~900k court-ordered evictions, from 2018 data **[PRE-2022]**. — [Eviction Lab 2025 report](https://evictionlab.org/ets-report-2025/); [Eviction Lab national estimates](https://evictionlab.org/national-estimates/); [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2026/04/20/evictions-fell-slightly-in-2025-but-some-areas-saw-upticks-report-finds/)
- Eviction defaults: nationally 30-50% of tenants lose by default (New America summary); Philadelphia 40% lost by default in the study period (Hoffman & Strezhnev) vs ~31-33% in other Philadelphia sources; Washington State ~40% of cases analyzed (OCLA 2024); New York State, only 46% of filings are answered (Furman Center, text garbled). — [New America](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/us-eviction-court-data/what-court-eviction-data-could-tell-us-but-doesnt-always/); [Penn Carey Law](https://www.law.upenn.edu/live/news/14836-eviction-by-default); [OCLA WA](https://ocla.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/OCLA_EvictionsbyDefaultJudgementinWAState.pdf); [Furman Center](https://furmancenter.org/files/publications/Half_the_Battle_is_Just_Showing_Up_V3_(1).pdf); [Temple report](https://law.temple.edu/csj/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2020/07/Reducing-Default-Judgments-in-Philadelphias-Landlord-Tenant-Court.pdf)
- Debt collection **[PRE-2022; Pew 2020]**: Pew's May 2020 report is cited for >70% of debt-collection suits ending in default judgment for the plaintiff, ~90% of defendants unrepresented, and debt collection as ~1 in 4 civil cases; a New York testimony cites >96% unrepresented. Utah study highlighted by Pew: 53% of defendants with a lawyer won vs 19% self-represented. I could not open the Pew PDF (binary), so these are via secondary citations. — [Pew report](https://www.pew.org/-/media/assets/2020/06/debt-collectors-to-consumers.pdf); [Aspen Institute paper](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASP-FSP_DebtCollectionsPaper_092221.pdf); [Legal epidemiology deck](https://events.networkforphl.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Legal-Epidemiology.pdf); [NYS Senate testimony](https://www.nysenate.gov/sites/default/files/mfj_testimony-nys_senate_hearing_on_courts.pdf)

### Inferences
- The needed answer/response is overwhelmingly a defendant-side task in eviction and debt, which is why the defaults matter: the "product" is getting a short, correct response filed on time.
- Because representation rates are ~4% (tenants) and ~10% or less (debtors), software competes with nothing for most users, not with lawyers.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 LSC edition found; no per-problem-type breakdown from the 2022 study retrieved.
- No current national, multi-state default-judgment rate for debt collection newer than Pew 2020; no NCSC 2020s update on self-representation retrieved.
- Small claims self-representation rate: no number found (small claims is often lawyer-barred or lawyer-rare by design).

## 2. Task-by-task: demand, cost today, free tools

(Consolidated per-task entries. Costs from commercial sources are marked; treat as indicative.)

### Takeaway
Highest-volume, lowest-representation tasks are eviction response and debt-collection response; both are defendant-side, deadline-driven and have free tools in only some states. Record clearing, name change, wills and small-estate affidavits are individual-initiated, form-heavy and rarely need a hearing. Immigration forms have a federal single-form set (one jurisdiction), but fees and rules are volatile in 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Eviction answer and defense**
- Volume and representation: see section 1 (about 1.2M filings/yr in tracked areas; ~3.6M national in 2018 data **[PRE-2022]**; 4% tenant representation). — [Eviction Lab](https://evictionlab.org/ets-report-2025/)
- Massachusetts: eviction-record sealing law took effect May 2025; Suffolk LIT Clinic and the Trial Court built a guided interview; 300+ people completed the petition in the first week. — [SJI summary](https://www.sji.gov/national-document-assembly-hosting-and-e-filing-initiative/)
- JustFix (NYC): ~350,000 NYC-region tenants used its digital tools in 2021 (per JustFix claim via InfluenceWatch); Who Owns What ~33,000 monthly visitors; its HP-action e-filing tool launched April 2020, used by ~3,000 tenants before the court blocked it in June 2021. — [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/justfix/); [Georgetown Law Tech Review](https://georgetownlawtechreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/4.1-Clement_Housing_Formatted.pdf)
- I found no study of an online eviction-answer tool's effect on default rates.

**Debt-collection answer**
- See Pew figures in section 1 (70%+ default, ~90% unrepresented, **[PRE-2022]**).
- Upsolve's challenge (nonlawyer "Justice Advocates" to advise debt-collection defendants) was aimed at exactly this task; see section 4.

**Expungement and record sealing**
- Lawyer cost ~$1,500-$3,000 per record plus court fees (Stanford 2022 cited $2,000-$3,000; Rasa ~$500 in that report). — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50955517/clearing-your-record-just-got-easier-expungement-costs-cut); [Stanford 2022 report](https://clp.law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/SLS-CLP-Regulatory-Reform-REPORTExecSum-9.26.pdf)
- Rasa Legal (Utah/Arizona/Pennsylvania): eligibility check free to $15; flat $250 for eligible records plus government fees; generative-AI-drafted petition with lawyer review; NPR (Aug 2026) says 34,000 people cleared since 2022 launch. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50955517/clearing-your-record-just-got-easier-expungement-costs-cut); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5892484/ai-legal-tech-jobs-clean-slate); [Rasa FAQ](https://rasa-legal.com/faq/)
- Petition uptake is low: 6.5% of eligible Michigan residents got a set-aside within 5 years (Prescott & Starr, HLR 2020 **[PRE-2022]**); 3-9% for California Prop 47; <10% of eligible Americans overall (Checkr). — [NC Law Review 2025 audit](https://northcarolinalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2025/07/05-Chien_FinalForPrint.pdf); [Checkr](https://checkr.com/blog/criminal-record-clearance)
- Automation (Clean Slate): 13 states + DC passed as of the Initiative's wrap-up (Illinois 13th); a later blog says 14 (Missouri); NCSL says 26 states + PR have at least one automatic-clearing provision. >18M people on a path to sealing; Pennsylvania sealed 34M cases in first 10 months; Minnesota BCA identified >2.17M potentially eligible records (Oct 2025). Counts vary by definition. — [Clean Slate Initiative](https://www.cleanslateinitiative.org/updates/states-of-clean-slate-end-of-year-wrap-up); [NCSL database](https://www.ncsl.org/civil-and-criminal-justice/automatic-criminal-record-clearing-database); [NC Law Review](https://northcarolinalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2025/07/05-Chien_FinalForPrint.pdf)
- The NC Law Review 2025 article is titled "Clean Slate, Dirty Data: An Audit of..." which indicates automated clearing has data-quality problems (not read in full).

**Name and gender-marker changes**
- Court costs: filing fees from under $100 to >$400 (California $435; Michigan ~$175; Milwaukee $160 + $100-125 publication; fee waiver available), total $300-$800 including certified copies and ID updates; passport $130-$190; Social Security card free. These are from guides and a court page, not a primary study. — [Sacramento Superior Court](https://www.saccourt.ca.gov/civil/self-help-services/name-gender-change.aspx); [UWM LGBT Resource Center](https://uwm.edu/lgbtrc/support-resources/trans-at-uwm/legal-name-change/); [Legal Friend guide](https://legalfriend.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-to-change-your-name)
- Court self-help already provides packets in some courts (e.g., Sacramento, Sonoma). — [Sonoma packet](https://sonoma.courts.ca.gov/sites/all/assets/pdfs/forms-filing/NameGenderChangePacketCoversheet.pdf)
- I found no authoritative annual demand number.

**Simple wills**
- Share of adults with a will: 24% (Caring.com/YouGov, Jan 2025), down from 33% in 2022; 13% have a living trust; Gallup 2021 phone poll said 46% (methodology difference). — [Caring.com](https://www.caring.com/about/news/2025-will-and-estate-planning-survey); [US Legal Forms roundup](https://blog.uslegalforms.com/personal/estate-planning-statistics/)
- Paid DIY: LegalZoom basic will quoted at $89-$129 and premium $249-$299 across comparison blogs (conflicting; auto-renewal of $199/yr reported); Rocket Lawyer $39.99/month or $149-$240/yr. Blog-sourced; check vendor pages. — [law-trust LegalZoom guide](https://law-trust.com/blog/legalzoom-pricing-guide-2026); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/investing/estate-planning/do-your-own-will-review)

**Advance directives and powers of attorney**
- 36.7% of US adults have completed any advance directive (29.3% living will, 33.4% health care proxy; 150 studies, 2011-2016, Health Affairs 2017 **[PRE-2022]**). — [Penn PAIR summary](https://pair.upenn.edu/publication/approximately-one-in-three-us-adults-completes-any-type-of-advance-directive-for-end-of-life-care/)
- Free state-statutory forms exist widely; I did not retrieve a cost or coverage source for them.

**Small-estate affidavits and probate**
- Small-estate thresholds range ~$15,000-$400,000 by state (CA $208,850; TX $75,000; NY $50,000 personal property), waiting periods 30-90 days; affidavit itself needs a notary (~$15-20). Probate cost commonly cited at 2-8% of estate (law-firm sites, no primary study). — [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/free-books/avoid-probate-book/chapter8-4.html); [LegalMatch](https://www.legalmatch.com/law-library/article/the-cost-of-probate-a-state-comparison.html)

**Uncontested divorce**
- Lawyer: Martindale-Nolo survey average ~$4,100 total for uncontested; settled $10,600; tried $20,400-$23,300 (commercial summaries; survey likely **[PRE-2022]**, year not confirmed). Self-represented: filing fee $70-$435 by state; median ~$300. — [ailawyer.pro](https://ailawyer.pro/blog/average-cost-of-divorce); [divorcecostcalculators](https://divorcecostcalculators.com/blog/divorce-cost-without-a-lawyer-2026-price-guide)
- Hello Divorce (tiered fixed-fee, Arizona/Utah entity) cited by Stanford as example. — [Stanford 2022 report](https://clp.law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/SLS-CLP-Regulatory-Reform-REPORTExecSum-9.26.pdf)

**Custody and child-support modification**
- 95% of parents unrepresented in child support (NCSC 2015 **[PRE-2022]**; via GW paper above). No cost or tool data retrieved.

**Protective orders**
- Ohio's online civil protection order tool (Ohio Legal Help + Supreme Court of Ohio) helps >50,000 Ohioans annually (tool users, not filings). LawHelp Interactive completed >68,800 DV document packages in 2023. Other states with guided tools: Alaska, Washington, Texas, Idaho, South Dakota. — [Ohio Legal Help via search](https://www.lakecountyohio.gov/coc/orders-of-protection/); [Connecting Justice Communities](https://www.connectingjusticecommunities.com/lawhelp-interactive-empowering-domestic-violence-survivors/2024/10/); [Alaska courts](https://courts.alaska.gov/shc/dv/index.htm); [Washington LawHelp](https://www.washingtonlawhelp.org/en/file-protection-order); [Texas State Law Library](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/legal-forms/protective-orders)
- Protective-order forms are already free everywhere via courts; safety and confidentiality risk is the key issue (see section 4).

**Immigration forms**
- USCIS fee schedule G-1055: N-400 $760 paper / $710 online; reduced $380 for income <=400% FPG (paper only); full waiver via I-912; DACA I-821D $85 + I-765 $520 paper/$470 online (one source says no DACA fee waivers; unresolved). A June 23, 2026 proposed rule would remove the reduced N-400 fee and N-400 waivers; some fees rise Oct 16, 2026. Treat as proposals. — [USCIS G-1055](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-1055.pdf); [Immigration Direct](https://www.immigrationdirect.com/blog/understanding-uscis-filing-fees/); [Peter Chu DACA fees](https://peterchu.com/blogs/medium-feed/daca-government-filing-fees)
- Naturalization applications: 827,610 in 2023 (up from 781,080 in 2022). — [DHS OHSS](https://ohss.dhs.gov/system/files/2025-07/2025_0725_ohss_naturalizations_fy2023_0.pdf)
- DACA: 533,280 active recipients Dec 31, 2024; ~455,000 active at March 31, 2026; USCIS approved 53,855 renewals in first half of FY2026 vs ~145,000 in first half FY2025; 125,282 renewals pending June 2026. Two-year renewal cycle. — [CRS DACA by the numbers](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R46764); [KCRA](https://www.kcra.com/article/american-dreams-broken-system-daca-renewal-delays/73619047)
- Citizenshipworks: >60,000 people started or completed applications since 2011 via 200+ partner orgs; DIY visitors ~25-30% of traffic (about half during COVID). — [Citizenshipworks for organizations](https://www.citizenshipworks.org/en/for-organizations); [Connecting Justice Communities](https://www.connectingjusticecommunities.com/protect-the-path-to-citizenship-and-opportunity-with-citizenshipworks/2025/09/)
- Share of N-400 filers who are pro se: no hard data found (one law-firm blog asserts a majority; unsourced).

### Inferences
- Priority by (volume x low representation x deadline pain): eviction answer, debt-collection answer, then record clearing, protective orders (already well served), uncontested divorce.
- Gender-marker/name change and small estates are smaller populations but have very high cost-to-benefit per person and few free tools reaching the long tail.
- Immigration fee and rule volatility in 2026 makes DACA/N-400 tools a poor first build for a small team, unless built as thin wrappers around USCIS's own online filing.

### Gaps
- Annual counts for: name changes, small estates, uncontested divorces, custody modifications, protective-order petitions, DACA renewals as a clean annual number, debt collection suits post-2020.
- Free-tool coverage by state per task (no single source; would need LawHelp, state court self-help site audits).
- No primary source for lawyer fees in wills, POAs, name change, protective orders.

## 3. Existing tools and why they fall short

### Takeaway
Free tools exist but are fragmented by state, funded by short grants (LSC TIG takes 10-12 months to start), and maintained by few people; the most scalable open-source stack is the Suffolk LIT Lab's Assembly Line on Docassemble. Paid players (LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer) sell blank forms and subscriptions in the will/LLC market and largely skip defendant-side court tasks.

### Cited Findings
- LawHelp Interactive (Pro Bono Net): 2020: 1,069,826 interviews, 710,378 assemblies **[PRE-2022]**; 2022: >1 million free sessions, >825,000 document packages; used in 47 states/territories/provinces; built on HotDocs with optional A2J Author. 2023 DV packages: >68,800. — [Pro Bono Net](https://www.probono.net/programs/lhi/); [PA LawHelp](https://www.palawhelp.org/resource/about-lawhelp-interactive); [Connecting Justice Communities 2024](https://www.connectingjusticecommunities.com/lawhelp-interactive-empowering-domestic-violence-survivors/2024/10/)
- Suffolk LIT Lab / Assembly Line / Court Forms Online: Docassemble-based; MA Appeals Court standing order approved the site in 2020; Assembly Line community includes IL, LA, ME, MI, MN, TX, VT; no annual user total found. National Document Assembly Hosting and E-filing Initiative page exists (SSL certificate failed on fetch). — [Suffolk LIT Lab](https://suffolklitlab.org/new-court-forms-online-website-launched/); [Assembly Line](https://assemblyline.suffolklitlab.org/); [SJI](https://www.sji.gov/national-document-assembly-hosting-and-e-filing-initiative/)
- Upsolve (free bankruptcy filing tool): 200,000 accounts created (2022 report; survey of 784 users, 46% had filed); student loan discharge tool launched April 2024, 102 cases filed, 9 approved; self-claims of 23,000 families and >$1B debt erased (Trustpilot-quoted; self-reported). — [Upsolve 2022 report](https://finhealthnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FSL-Upsolve-Report-2022-FINAL.pdf); [LawNext 2024](https://www.lawnext.com/2024/04/upsolve-launches-tool-to-help-pro-se-bankruptcy-filers-get-relief-from-student-loans.html); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/upsolve.org)
- JustFix: see section 2; court blocked its HP e-filing tool in 2021, an example of court/e-filing gatekeeping killing a tool.
- Clean Slate: see section 2 (automation beats petitions on uptake but has data-quality issues).
- Citizenshipworks: see section 2; most users come through partner organizations.
- State court self-help: examples listed in section 2 (Ohio tool 50,000+/yr; Lone Star Legal Aid >100,000 people). — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/lsc-issues-blueprint-for-narrowing-the-justice-gap-through-technology-innovation-in-civil-legal-services.html)
- Maintenance: Stanford Filing Fairness Toolkit says document assembly needs a named maintainer; forms vary widely by state so vendors lack scale; Washington State spent almost a decade simplifying its forms; a docassemble vendor suggests 15-20% of annual build cost for maintenance and warns a year without upkeep may require a full audit (commercial source). — [Stanford Filing Fairness Toolkit](https://filingfairnessproject.law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/SLS_FilingFairnessProject_FF-1.pdf); [Stanford press](https://law.stanford.edu/press/stanfords-rhode-center-and-legal-design-lab-team-with-six-states-to-improve-court-access-using-readily-available-technology/); [RateMyPDF paper](https://assemblyline.suffolklitlab.org/assets/files/Beyond%20Readability%20with%20RateMyPDF-306d8c3ae5c5cb2ff33d436a859d1581.pdf); [docassembledevelopment.com](https://docassembledevelopment.com/use-cases/judicial-council-form-updates/)
- LSC survey: 45% of nonprofits say they spend too little on technology; 70% of AI Peer Learning Lab participants cite lack of AI knowledge. — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/lsc-issues-blueprint-for-narrowing-the-justice-gap-through-technology-innovation-in-civil-legal-services.html)

### Inferences
- The opportunity gap is not the form-filling engine (Docassemble/Assembly Line exist, open source) but state-by-state content, hosting, upkeep and distribution to users at the moment of need (e.g., at service of an eviction notice).
- Coverage by state is uneven and I have no per-state matrix.

### Gaps
- No per-state coverage matrix for LawHelp Interactive or Court Forms Online; no funding amount for Suffolk LIT Lab or Pro Bono Net; no JustFix or Upsolve funding or current user numbers beyond above; no source on LegalZoom/Rocket Lawyer revenue or defendant-side offerings; Rocket Lawyer withdrew from Utah and moved to Arizona (ABA Journal).

## 4. Barriers: UPL, sandboxes, Upsolve v. James, form changes, e-filing, liability

### Takeaway
UPL law was upheld against a First Amendment challenge for nonlawyer advice in the Second Circuit and the Supreme Court declined review (March 30, 2026), so no federal constitutional route to nonlawyer advice exists in the near term. Utah's sandbox has shrunk and sunsets in 2027; Arizona's ABS program has grown but is dominated by personal injury. Software that supplies information and forms (not individualized advice) remains the safe lane.

### Cited Findings
- Upsolve v. James (2d Cir., No. 22-1345, Sept 9, 2025): vacated the preliminary injunction; held NY UPL statutes regulate speech but are content-neutral (intermediate scrutiny). Cert petition No. 25-948 filed Feb 6, 2026, **denied March 30, 2026** (docket). — [CourtListener opinion](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10668452/upsolve-inc-v-james/); [Public Citizen CLP blog](https://clpblog.citizen.org/second-circuit-holds-unlawful-practice-law-can-apply-to-debt-collection-advice/); [Supreme Court docket 25-948](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=%2Fdocket%2Fdocketfiles%2Fhtml%2Fpublic%2F25-948.html)
- Utah sandbox: participation 39 entrants in 2022 to 11 as of April 30, 2025 (Stanford); new rule requires showing reach to underserved Utahns; at least 27 left or may leave; Rocket Lawyer withdrew to Arizona; sunset reported as Aug 14, 2027 and closed to new applications (search summary, not verified on the official page). Stanford: no high-innovation, software-without-lawyer entities; only 4 free low-income entities; 20 consumer complaints total (secondary report of the Stanford data). — [Stanford Law 2025](https://law.stanford.edu/2025/06/02/regulatory-innovation-at-the-crossroads-five-years-of-data-on-entity-regulation-reform-in-arizona-and-utah/); [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/nearly-30-legal-entities-may-leave-utahs-regulatory-sandbox-after-state-tightens-rules); [Utah Innovation Office](https://utahinnovationoffice.org/authorized-entities/); [LawNext on Stanford](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/06/five-years-after-reform-stanford-study-offers-comprehensive-look-at-legal-innovation-in-arizona-and-utah.html)
- Arizona ABS: 19 approved in 2022 to 136 by April 2025; concentrated in personal injury and mass tort; no practice-area limit; private-equity ownership growing. — [Stanford Law 2025](https://law.stanford.edu/2025/06/02/regulatory-innovation-at-the-crossroads-five-years-of-data-on-entity-regulation-reform-in-arizona-and-utah/)

Texas HB 2386 (2025) introduced; outcome not confirmed. — [Stanford CLP](https://clp.law.stanford.edu/licensing-paraprofessionals/); [Colorado Legal Regulation](https://www.coloradolegalregulation.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Understanding-Practice-of-Law-Issue-September-8-2026.pdf); [LegiScan HB2386](https://legiscan.com/TX/text/HB2386/id/3097671/Texas-2025-HB2386-Introduced.html); [NCSC UPL modernization](https://www.ncsc.org/resources-courts/modernizing-unauthorized-practice-law-regulations-embrace-technology-improve)
- Form changes and e-filing: forms vary widely by state (Stanford); court forms are reissued on a schedule and field names change silently (commercial docassemble vendor); NYC court blocked JustFix's HP e-filing tool in June 2021. Court e-filing access often runs through court-approved vendors (not researched).
- Liability: I found no authoritative source on liability for form-assembly tools. Rasa's model keeps a lawyer reviewing AI-drafted petitions. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50955517/clearing-your-record-just-got-easier-expungement-costs-cut)
- LSC Next Frontier does not mention UPL or maintenance as topics. — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/lsc-issues-blueprint-for-narrowing-the-justice-gap-through-technology-innovation-in-civil-legal-services.html)

### Inferences
- A small team should plan on (a) legal information plus form assembly, with courts' own forms, and (b) partnership with a legal aid org or a lawyer-of-record for anything resembling advice. Upsolve v. James removes the "nonlawyer advocate" path.
- Court-approved status (as Court Forms Online has in Massachusetts) is the strongest UPL and e-filing shield found, so working with a state court or its self-help center is likely the best first step.

## 5. Which tasks are form-driven and rules-stable enough for a small team to serve a state or many states?

### Takeaway
Best candidates, on the evidence found: (1) eviction answer/response, (2) debt-collection answer, (3) record sealing/expungement eligibility plus petition, and (4) name change, because each is a single-court, short-form, deadline- or petition-driven task with high self-representation. Immigration and divorce/custody are higher-risk, more variable, and less suited to a first build. This ranking is my inference; the evidence does not include revision-frequency data.

### Cited Findings
- High self-representation and low lawyer cost-benefit in eviction and debt (section 1).
- Expungement: eligibility check plus petition is productized at $250 plus fees and under $15 for the check by Rasa; automation (Clean Slate) reduces need over time but data quality is an issue. — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50955517/clearing-your-record-just-got-easier-expungement-costs-cut); [NC Law Review](https://northcarolinalawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2025/07/05-Chien_FinalForPrint.pdf)
- Massachusetts' May 2025 eviction-sealing petition went from law to a live interview within weeks and had 300+ completions in week one (partnership with the Trial Court). — [SJI](https://www.sji.gov/national-document-assembly-hosting-and-e-filing-initiative/)
- Statewide-form states (e.g., California Judicial Council forms) are easier to cover because one form set applies to every county; Stanford says fragmentation of forms by state deters vendors. — [Stanford press](https://law.stanford.edu/press/stanfords-rhode-center-and-legal-design-lab-team-with-six-states-to-improve-court-access-using-readily-available-technology/)
- Immigration: fee rules in flux (June 2026 proposed rule, Oct 16, 2026 fee changes); DACA renewal processing has slowed sharply (53,855 approved in H1 FY2026 vs ~145,000 in H1 FY2025). — [Immigration Direct](https://www.immigrationdirect.com/blog/understanding-uscis-filing-fees/); [KCRA](https://www.kcra.com/article/american-dreams-broken-system-daca-renewal-delays/73619047)

### Inferences
- Rules-stability ranking (my judgment, not sourced): small estate affidavit, advance directive and basic will (statute-based, rarely revised, no court form in most states) are the most stable and cheapest to maintain, but have the weakest impact per user (documents are already available as free state statutory forms and cheap at $40-$130 paid).
- Eviction/debt answers are impactful but require per-state and often per-county procedure (answer deadlines, service rules), so a statewide build in a uniform-form state (e.g., statewide Judicial Council forms) is the realistic unit.
- Expungement eligibility logic is the hardest rules engine (statutes change annually; Clean Slate data quality), but has the best-demonstrated at-cost economics (Rasa).
- Divorce and custody: long forms, judge discretion, many state variants, service rules; higher liability risk. Protective orders: forms free and widely available; confidentiality and safety risk argue against a small team owning it.
- Distribution via existing court self-help centers and legal aid partners is more important to reach than the software itself (Citizenshipworks: most users via partners).

### Gaps
- No quantitative form-revision-frequency, per-task maintenance cost or per-state coverage data; I therefore could not test the ranking against numbers.
