# Precedents and Operating Models for Free or At-Cost Public-Interest Software Services for US Individuals

Research date: 2026-11-15. Figures from before 2022 are marked **[pre-2022]**. Many search-result summaries came from aggregators; where I read a primary or near-primary page (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Code for America's own report, Nava's case study, Technical.ly) the finding says so. Where a figure is self-reported by the organization, it says so.

## 1. Case studies: what worked, what failed, and why (team size, budget, people served, cost per person)

### Takeaway
The durable cases share one trait: a payer other than the individual who sees the service as cheaper than the alternative (a state agency, a court, a funder buying a measurable outcome). The failures are structural (Benefits Data Trust's cost per application against a revenue base it could not raise; Direct File's dependence on one political patron; DoNotPay's unsubstantiated claims) rather than technical. Reported cost per person varies by about two orders of magnitude, from roughly $2 per application for lean online tools to $138 per return for Direct File to "a few hundred dollars" per application for Benefits Data Trust's human-assisted model.

### Cited Findings

**Code for America (CfA) and GetCalFresh**
- GetCalFresh helped 6.2 million people get $12.8 billion in SNAP benefits (2017–2025), at its peak about 70% of online SNAP applications and 50% of all SNAP applications in California. It was sunset in July 2025. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)
- The sunset was a planned handoff, not a failure. California now runs a state-owned system, BenefitsCal. GetCalFresh had been a stopgap built before a statewide system existed. The assister tool, CBO portal and document uploader ended June 30, 2025, and the site and customer service were to end by September 30, 2025. CDSS is executing a new agreement with CfA for a distinct set of digital outreach services. CfA says plain language, mobile-first design and streamlined uploads are now built into BenefitsCal. — [CDSS GetCalFresh transition page](https://cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/cdss-programs/calfresh-outreach/getcalfresh-transition); [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)
- Origin **[pre-2022, 2017]**: CfA won a $3.6 million, two-year California contract to build GetCalFresh. In its first two years it helped over 30,000 people apply and cut average application time from 45 minutes to eight. — [StateScoop, 2017](https://statescoop.com/with-california-food-stamp-contract-code-for-america-shifts-funding-strategy/)
- The 2017 contract was explicitly a funding strategy shift. CfA's money had come from institutional funders, individual donors and government partners, with government money usually tied to the fellowship program. A 2016 funding shortfall had already forced CfA to cut support for its Brigade Network. — [StateScoop, 2017](https://statescoop.com/with-california-food-stamp-contract-code-for-america-shifts-funding-strategy/)
- Organization-wide, InfluenceWatch reports CfA's 2024 filing at revenue of $29.85M, expenses of $38.41M and assets of $77.75M, with government grants of about $6.0M (roughly 20% of revenue). The expenses-over-revenue gap in the filing year is from a secondary aggregator, so check the 990. — [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/code-for-america/)
- CfA laid off 35 staff (17%) in 2023 for financial sustainability, and earlier stopped acting as fiscal sponsor for its local brigades, citing less funding for a decentralized network. — [CfA Workers United, 2023](https://cfaworkersunited.com/stories/2023/08/31/code-for-america-lays-off-35-colleagues.html); [StateScoop on brigade split](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-local-brigades-ending/)
- 2025 Impact Report: 7 million people helped and $22 billion in benefits across 27 states and D.C. (these are CfA's self-reported figures, not audited). Tax programs reached 725,000 people with $1.5 billion in cash. Named funders (no amounts) include Ballmer Group, JPMorganChase, Google.org and Blue Meridian Partners. The report does not disclose revenue or staff count. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)

**Clear My Record (CfA)**
- California DAs used it to identify about 144,000 marijuana convictions to reduce or dismiss, about two-thirds of all eligible ones in the state. Earlier partnerships reduced or dismissed about 85,000 convictions from 70,000 people's records. The application is free and open source and can identify up to 10,000 eligible convictions per minute from bulk records. — [Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/news/record-clearance-at-scale-how-clear-my-record-helped-reduce-or-dismiss-144-000-convictions-in-california/); [Clear My Record program page](https://www.codeforamerica.org/programs/criminal-legal-system/clear-my-record/)
- The model sells to the government that holds the records: CfA's position is that automatic clearance should be a free government service. Cost to individuals is the comparison point: a petition typically costs a flat attorney fee of $500–$2,000 plus a $50–$400 court fee, according to a secondary source. I found no per-record cost figure. — [CfA Clear My Record page](https://www.codeforamerica.org/programs/criminal-legal-system/clear-my-record/); [localattorneyfees.com (low-authority)](https://localattorneyfees.com/expungement)

**Upsolve (501(c)(3), bankruptcy)**
- Form 990 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, EIN 82-1736267), fiscal years ending December: 2024 revenue $2.10M, expenses $1.97M, net assets $3.40M. 2023: revenue $1.16M, expenses $1.93M. 2022: revenue $2.35M, expenses $2.07M, net assets $4.04M. 2021: revenue $3.65M, expenses $1.81M. 2020: revenue $1.56M, expenses $1.22M. 2019: revenue $2.09M, expenses $0.89M. 2018: revenue $0.45M, expenses $0.23M. — [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/821736267)
- Self-reported funding split: about 70% government funding and charitable donations (Legal Services Corporation, Robin Hood, Hewlett, New York Bar Foundation, individuals) and about 30% program service revenue. It earns a small referral fee when users sign up with partners such as credit counselors. Biggest expense is salaries, then rent, travel, insurance, software and marketing. — [Upsolve: How Upsolve Is Free](https://upsolve.org/learn/how-upsolve-is-free/)
- The user still owes the court filing fee ($335 per Upsolve) and the app helps request a waiver. Lawyers charge $1,500–$2,500 for Chapter 7. — [Marketplace, 2021](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2021/12/27/whats-the-best-way-to-navigate-personal-bankruptcy) and [Upsolve: How Upsolve Works](https://upsolve.org/learn/how-upsolve-works/) (fee figures came via search summaries of founder statements; not read directly) **[pre-2022]**
- Scale: a Trustpilot profile quoted in search says over 23,000 families and more than $1 billion in debt erased (self-reported, undated). A September 2024 press release said over 14,000 families and over $600M in debt, and that Upsolve educates about 2.9 million people a year. — [Upsolve Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/upsolve.org); [PR Newswire, Sept 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/upsolve-receives-4-2-million-grant-for-the-development-of-upsolve-assist-302237285.html)
- Legal aid distribution: LSC encourages legal aid organizations to refer people they cannot otherwise serve to Upsolve, and also promotes it as a way for legal aid to do bankruptcies faster. — [LSC](https://www.lsc.gov/i-am-grantee/model-practices-innovations/technology/legal-aid-organizations-upsolve-offers-way-do-bankruptcies-10-times-faster-and-help-more-people)
- Regulatory setback: Upsolve's pre-enforcement First Amendment challenge to New York's unauthorized practice of law (UPL) rules (to let trained non-lawyer "Justice Advocates" help with debt-collection suits) won a preliminary injunction in May 2022. The Second Circuit vacated it on September 9, 2025, and on remand the district court (Judge Kaplan) ruled against Upsolve in March 2026. A cert petition appears pending (Docket 25-948, status not confirmed). — [Second Circuit docket 22-1345 opinion](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Upsolve-CA2-Decision.pdf) (the URL path is dated 2023, so confirm which opinion it is; the September 2025 date comes from a search summary); [Volokh Conspiracy, March 2026](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/03/08/court-upholds-new-yorks-limits-on-unauthorized-practice-of-law/); [Supreme Court docket 25-948 appendix](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-948/395644/20260206111635719_2%20-%20Appendix.pdf)

**Dollar For (501(c)(3), hospital charity care)**
- Founded by Jared Walker in Portland, Oregon; site launched summer 2021. Built a database of charity-care criteria for more than 2,000 hospitals and maps a patient's answers onto each hospital's form, then follows up with the hospital. — [KFF Health News / An Arm and a Leg, Jan 2026](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)
- Helped erase over $55M in bills in 2025, up from about $32M in 2024. Estimates that less than a third of qualified patients get their bills forgiven. — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)
- Fragility: two big donors pulled out in early 2025 (one a $300K gift; total about $500K, almost a third of budget). It replaced about $200K, made no layoffs but moved some staff to half-time. A planned AI document-checking tool (about $250K including a first full-time CTO hire) is not yet built. — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)
- Staff grew from essentially one person to 15 and it reports no government funding (founder statement via a Houston Public Media report, October 2026, summarized by search). — [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/)

**IRS Direct File**
- 2024 pilot (12 states): 140,803 successful returns, 423,450 accounts created or signed into, more than 3.3 million started the Eligibility Checker. GSA Touchpoints survey of over 11,000 users: 90% rated the experience Excellent or Above Average. Users claimed over $90M in refunds and saved an estimated $5.6M in prep fees. — [IRS release IR-2024-122](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/398eb5a); [IRS after-action report](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5969.pdf)
- Team: 32 full-time IRS employees plus 29 US Digital Service employees (January 2024), with about 400 IRS employees identified for customer support as needed. — [TIGTA interim report, 2024](https://www.tigta.gov/sites/default/files/reports/2024-06/2024408031fr.pdf); [GAO-24-107236](https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24107236.pdf)
- Pilot cost: IRS spent $24.6M through the end of the pilot, excluding USDS, whose annualized team cost was $7.2M. GAO reports a pilot cost of about $20.2M, which I could not reconcile with the IRS figure. — [GAO-24-107236](https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24107236.pdf); [IRS after-action report](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5969.pdf)
- 2025 season (25 states): registrations rose 78% from 423,000 to 751,000, but 59% of registrants did not submit a return. TIGTA found the IRS overestimated costs: projected $61.2M for FY2025 against $16.2M actual as of May 2025 (still excluding support functions). — [Accounting Today on TIGTA](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-overestimated-direct-file-costs-before-ending-program)
- Treasury's report to Congress (dated October 2, released November 5, 2025) put the cost at at least $41M for tax year 2024, or at least $138 per accepted return (tax year 2023: at least $31.8M for 140,803 returns, about $225.85 each, per one account). Earlier IRS projections were about $9–$16 per return. Treasury said the $41M understates the true cost. — [Tax Notes via search summary (page blocked, 403)](https://www.taxnotes.com/featured-news/irs-shutters-direct-file-citing-cost-and-low-uptake/2025/11/05/7t7q0); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-ends-direct-file-program); [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/11/irs-direct-file-will-not-be-available-in-2026-agency-tells-states/) (I could not open the Tax Notes or FNN pages directly; these come from search summaries).
- The IRS told states Direct File will not be available in 2026, and Treasury said it would instead improve Free File (the partnership with private software companies). — [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/11/irs-direct-file-will-not-be-available-in-2026-agency-tells-states/)
- The IRS published most of the code at github.com/IRS-Public/direct-file in 2025 under the SHARE IT Act; it carries a public-domain dedication, and excludes sensitive data and National Security Systems code. A note says not all source, documentation and metadata is included. CfA has forked it, and former Direct File staff in an Economic Security Project fellowship worked on helping states build free filing tools from it. Others would still need access to IRS and state portals. — [Free Software Foundation](https://www.fsf.org/blogs/community/irs-direct-file-released-as-free-software); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/irs-makes-direct-file-software-open-source-after-trump-tried-to-kill-it-2000611151); [Hacker News thread on license](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44182356)
- CfA's 2025 report notes the IRS discontinued the program in November and gives no usage figures. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)

**Benefits Data Trust (BDT), closure June–August 2024**
- Announced in June 2024 a 60-day wind-down, board vote unanimous, citing "a perfect storm of circumstances." Staff had been 340 two years earlier, with two layoff rounds since late 2023; the state notice listed 273 layoffs. A board document showed a $9.8M loss on track for 2023. No malfeasance or fraud, per WHYY. It did not hand operations to a successor. — [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/health/benefits-data-trust-bdt-shutting-down-20240625.html); [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/philadelphia-benefits-data-trust-closure-employees-laid-off/)
- Why (Technical.ly, staff accounts): budget over $30M, about two-thirds philanthropy and one-third state and local contracts; one insider said expenses were at one point twice revenue, another that it needed about $2.5M a month and was bringing in about $1M. A $20M MacKenzie Scott gift in 2022 did not fix the gap. Some states paid for one or two benefits while BDT staffed to serve 10 to 15. Each successful application reportedly cost "a few hundred dollars" against about $2 for some lower-cost online tools. It helped 120,000 people access $182M in benefits in 2023. — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)
- Its partners (PA Department of Human Services, City of Philadelphia, a health insurer, a children's hospital) were left trying to reach applicants it had been serving. — [Philadelphia Inquirer, Aug 2024](https://www.inquirer.com/health/benefits-data-trust-closing-august-23-20240823.html)

**DoNotPay (cautionary case)**
- FTC finalized an order in February 2025 (docket C-4812; Commission vote 5-0 on January 16, 2025): $193,000 in monetary relief, notice to 2021–2023 subscribers, and a ban on advertising that the service performs like a real lawyer without substantiation. ### Inferences
- Cost per person served is an artifact of what is counted. Direct File's $138 per accepted return is a federal cost-accounting figure with partial overhead and a pilot-scale denominator; the IRS's earlier $9–$16 projection assumed a 50-state scale (a former official said so, per search summary), so an unscaled service costs 10x more per person. The same denominator logic applies to a small team: fixed costs (security, support, accessibility, legal review) divided by few users dominate.
- Back-of-envelope, labeled my own arithmetic: GetCalFresh's first $3.6M two-year contract against about 30,000 people helped in those two years is about $120 per person, but the contract also covered build costs and the first-years denominator was small; do not treat it as a steady-state figure.
- Upsolve's costs ($1.8–2.1M a year since 2021) and JustFix's (under $1M in 2024) show that a product organization of roughly 8–15 people is fundable on philanthropy plus modest earned revenue, but both have lumpy revenue (Upsolve revenue ranged from $1.2M to $3.7M against steady spending; JustFix has run deficits since 2020).
- Dollar For loses a third of its budget when two donors leave, which is the clearest small-organization illustration of concentration risk in the sources found.
- BDT's closure fits a pattern: human-assisted enrollment priced at several hundred dollars per application cannot be sustained on contracts that pay for one or two benefits while the organization staffs for ten to fifteen. The software-only tools (about $2 per application per Technical.ly's source) survive on thinner margins.
- Direct File shows that a technically successful, well-rated public service (90% positive) can still be closed for political reasons; open-sourcing under the SHARE IT Act is the only lasting part. For a small team, that source code is a free, public-domain reference for tax logic, but taking it to production needs IRS e-file access (inference from sources saying others still need access to IRS and state portals).

## 2. Funding models

### Takeaway
No single model carries these services. The stable pattern is a blend: foundation money for build and iteration, a government or court contract for run, and a small earned stream. Pure philanthropy is fragile (Dollar For lost a third of its budget when two donors left; BDT's $20M gift did not fix a structural deficit). Venture-backed for-profit (Propel) can be free to users and governments but monetizes the users' attention and spending.

### Cited Findings
- Upsolve: about 70% government funding and donations, about 30% program service revenue, plus referral fees from partners; funders named include LSC, Robin Hood, Hewlett, Gates (a $4,249,901 seed grant for Upsolve Assist, September 2024), and individual donors including Eric Schmidt. — [Upsolve](https://upsolve.org/learn/how-upsolve-is-free/); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/upsolve-receives-4-2-million-grant-for-the-development-of-upsolve-assist-302237285.html)
- CfA: philanthropy plus government contracts; the 2017 California GetCalFresh contract was meant to reduce dependence on philanthropy and fellowship revenue. — [StateScoop, 2017](https://statescoop.com/with-california-food-stamp-contract-code-for-america-shifts-funding-strategy/) **[pre-2022]**
- BDT: about two-thirds philanthropy, one-third government contracts; contracts that paid for one or two benefits while it staffed for more. — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)
- Suffolk LIT Lab: federal grant (State Justice Institute), service contracts with courts and legal aid, foundation grant (Hewlett), LSC pass-through. — [Suffolk LIT Lab](https://suffolklitlab.org/reintroducing-the-document-assembly-line/)
- JustFix: foundation grants (Robin Hood, Open Society, RWJF, CZI) plus a small share from local elected officials' discretionary budgets **[2022]**. — [Shelterforce](https://shelterforce.org/2022/03/24/how-hidden-property-owners-and-bad-landlord-patterns-are-revealed-in-nyc/)
- Dollar For: philanthropy only per founder; a Fool Foundation page lists it as a grantee. — [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/); [Fool Foundation](https://foolfoundation.org/if-fund/dollar-for/)
- Foundation scale (funders, not grantees): Arnold Ventures FY2023 Form 990 shows revenue of $687.3M and total assets of $4.3B (Instrumentl lists $193.3M in 2024 grants); Schmidt Family Foundation FY2024 shows $227.7M in expenses and $2.0B in assets. I did not find an Arnold Ventures, Schmidt Futures or Ballmer grant to any of the case-study organizations except as noted below. — [philanthropy.org: Arnold](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/263241764/laura-and-john-arnold-foundation/2023); [philanthropy.org: Schmidt Family Foundation](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/204170342/the-schmidt-family-foundation)
- Ballmer Group, Gates and partners committed $8.5M in 2025 to AI tools for public benefits access (via a secondary summary; verify). — [Search summary only; source page not identified. Verify before use.](https://www.govtech.com/civic/civic-tech-partnership-to-help-govt-caseworkers-use-ai)
- CfA names Ballmer Group, JPMorganChase, Google.org and Blue Meridian Partners as funders, without amounts. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)
- Propel: venture capital (about $68M total) with revenue from coupons, ads, jobs and banking interchange; free to users and governments. — [Crain's New York](https://www.crainsnewyork.com/chasing-giants/fintech-startup-propel-eyes-growth-all-in-one-app-snap-recipients)
- Nava (public benefit corporation) is paid by government on contract, with 71% of federal obligations from CMS. — [getwab.com vendor profile](https://www.getwab.com/library/vendors/nava-pbc-cage-7m2l6)
- Free File precedent: Treasury's chosen alternative to Direct File is Free File, a partnership with private tax software companies; in January 2024 the FTC ordered Intuit to fix misleading "free" tax-prep ads for a product most filers could not use. — [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/11/irs-direct-file-will-not-be-available-in-2026-agency-tells-states/); Intuit order: from a search summary, no primary URL retrieved
- Cost of ID verification, if a service must buy it (GAO-25-107000): Login.gov charges agencies $3 per active user in a proofing year and $1 in a non-proofing year, with a $2,500 monthly minimum; both Login.gov and ID.me are free to individuals, so the agency bears the cost. A 2018 GAO study cited by ID.me put one agency's cost at $54 per user **[pre-2022]**. — [GAO-25-107000](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-107000.pdf); [ID.me comment to USDA, 2023](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/DownloadDocument?objectID=133739702)
- Insurance cost benchmark (insurer-agency estimates, 2026): a small nonprofit with limited data and a $500K limit pays about $1,200–$2,500 a year for cyber coverage; mid-size with a client database and $1M limit $2,500–$5,000; a social services nonprofit with protected health information and a $2M limit $4,000–$10,000. A bundled package with general liability, D&O and others runs $3,000–$15,000+. — [Hotaling Insurance](https://hotalinginsurance.com/hotaling-insurance-blog/nonprofit-cyber-liability-insurance); [Christensen Group](https://www.christensengroup.com/article/nonprofit-insurance-coverage)

### Inferences
- "At cost" realistically means three layers: (a) build and iterate, funded by grants; (b) run, which is the layer a government or court payer should cover but often does not (Suffolk LIT Lab's hosting engineer is being funded through an LSC grant, and Upsolve earns about 30% from services); (c) hidden costs not in a product budget: support staffing, ID verification at $1–$54 per user when it is required, cyber and professional liability insurance in the low five figures, and security and accessibility reviews. I found no source that totals these for a small service; the layers are my synthesis.
- The fiscal-sponsorship and B-corp questions did not turn up evidence in this pass. The sources do show examples on both sides of the entity choice: 501(c)(3) (Upsolve, JustFix, mRelief, Dollar For, CfA), a public benefit corporation (Nava), and a venture-backed company (Propel). Choice follows the revenue source: contract revenue fits Nava; ads, coupons and interchange fit Propel; grants fit the 501(c)(3)s.
- Earned revenue from the end user is rare in this set because users have low income by definition; the earned revenue that exists comes from referral fees (Upsolve), contracts (CfA, Suffolk LIT Lab, Nava) or third-party offers (Propel).

### Gaps
- No source found on fiscal sponsorship specifics for service-delivery software, or on sliding-scale fees in this category.
- No direct evidence of Arnold Ventures, Schmidt Futures or Ballmer Group grant amounts to the named organizations (CfA names Ballmer without amounts; Upsolve lists Schmidt as an individual donor).
- No total-cost-of-ownership figure for a small free service, including support and fraud handling.

## 3. Recurring barriers: liability, security, identity verification, accessibility, reaching users, trust, scams

### Takeaway
The recurring barriers are structural and mostly cost the operator, not the user: UPL rules limit what a tool can say, identity proofing fails for a large minority of low-income people, and trusted intermediaries (legal aid, 211, libraries, caseworkers) control access to users. Evidence on uptake through intermediaries is thin.

### Cited Findings
- UPL and liability: Upsolve's challenge shows how far the rules reach. In September 2025 the Second Circuit vacated the injunction that would have let trained non-lawyers advise on debt-collection suits; on remand in March 2026 the district court ruled against Upsolve. — [Volokh Conspiracy](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/03/08/court-upholds-new-yorks-limits-on-unauthorized-practice-of-law/)
- Regulatory sandboxes have produced little tech-driven innovation: as of April 30, 2025, Arizona had 136 authorized alternative business structure entities (134 with lawyers providing services directly) and Utah 11. Utah recorded 20 complaints across all sandbox entities through April 2025 (about 1 in 5,869 services). The Utah court has narrowed the sandbox and removed ABS-only entities, with existing UPL waivers allowed through 2027. The entities offering free legal services are in the Utah sandbox. — [LawNext on Stanford five-year study](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/06/five-years-after-reform-stanford-study-offers-comprehensive-look-at-legal-innovation-in-arizona-and-utah.html); [Stanford report](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/SLS_CLP_LegalInnovation_REPORT_v5.pdf); [NCSC white paper, August 2025](https://www.ncsc.org/sites/default/files/media/document/AI_UPL_WhitePaper.pdf)
- Identity verification: experts estimate proofing solutions work for about 80% of people (one quoted estimate). ID.me sent 10–15% of benefit applicants to video chats with staff (House Oversight). Community Legal Services of Philadelphia counted 12,908 failed ID.me attempts in Pennsylvania's unemployment system as of September 15, 2021. Credit-history-based questions burden people with thin credit files. — [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2022/01/no-easy-fix-id-verification-government-benefits/361120/); [House Oversight release](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairs-maloney-clyburn-release-evidence-facial-recognition-company-idme); [CLS Philadelphia brief, 2021](https://clsphila.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/IDme-issue-brief-final-11-2-2021.pdf); [NELP](https://www.nelp.org/insights-research/id-verification/) **[2021–2022]**
- Treasury has made ID.me mandatory for some uses even as OMB policy shifts toward Login.gov (October 2026 trade report). — [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202610/us-treasury-makes-id-me-mandatory-as-federal-policy-shifts-toward-login-gov)
- Direct File's identity step and no pre-submission error alerts were findings in TIGTA's review; the IRS said low usage came from confusion about availability and lack of outreach. — [Accounting Today on TIGTA](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-overestimated-direct-file-costs-before-ending-program)
- Reaching users: LSC encourages legal aid organizations to refer overflow to Upsolve; 211 acts as an intake referral to legal aid in King County, WA; legal aid text-messaging projects have shown response rates above 50% per LSC. The ABA Foundation's survey of US legal technologies says many free tools are unusable by many people in low-income communities and many nonprofit providers lack digital tools themselves. — [LSC](https://www.lsc.gov/i-am-grantee/model-practices-innovations/technology/legal-aid-organizations-upsolve-offers-way-do-bankruptcies-10-times-faster-and-help-more-people); [ABA Foundation survey, 2023](https://www.americanbarfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/report_us_digital_legal_tech_for_nonlawyers.pdf); [Washington OPD legal assistance page](https://opd.wa.gov/legal-assistance)
- Dollar For attributes growth in 2025 to press (90+ stories) rather than channel partnerships. — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)
- Accessibility and plain language: CfA says GetCalFresh's plain language, mobile-first design and streamlined document uploads were carried into BenefitsCal. CfA's AI Studio Accessibility Project piloted conversion of over 9,000 PDFs in Salt Lake City and over 55,000 in Georgia. Nava's caseworker chatbot responses averaged a 10th to 12th grade reading level, above the recommended 8th-grade standard. — [CDSS](https://cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/cdss-programs/calfresh-outreach/getcalfresh-transition); [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/); [Nava](https://www.navapbc.com/case-studies/evaluating-ai-assistive-chatbot-caseworkers)
- Scams that imitate free services: consumers reported losing $3.5 billion to impersonation fraud in 2025 and the FTC removed 19 websites impersonating the agency; in October 2025 the FTC and Nevada sued American Tax Service for allegedly impersonating government to sell tax-debt relief; texts about tax refunds link to fake IRS sites. Fake sites often use unusual domain extensions. I found no FTC action about copycats of a specific free service like Direct File or SNAP apps. — [FTC imposter page](https://www.ftc.gov/imposter); [FTC press release, October 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/10/ftc-nevada-sue-tax-debt-relief-scammers-falsely-impersonating-government); [NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/ftc-warns-of-identity-theft-scams-targeting-taxpayers-during-filing-season/3755638/)
- Trust and misleading "free": FTC's Intuit order (January 2024) and the DoNotPay order (February 2025) both address deceptive claims of free or lawyer-equivalent service. — [FTC DoNotPay release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)
- Data security: no breach case study found for these organizations. Cyber coverage typically pays for forensics, notification, credit monitoring, legal defense and regulatory fines; even small nonprofits face notification laws. — [Hotaling Insurance](https://hotalinginsurance.com/hotaling-insurance-blog/nonprofit-cyber-liability-insurance)

### Inferences
- A team that collects Social Security numbers, income and immigration or criminal-record data takes on a regulated-data operator's costs at a nonprofit budget; the cheaper design pattern in the sources is not to hold the data (CfA's Clear My Record runs on bulk records held by the DA; Dollar For maps answers onto hospital forms and submits them). Whether a given tool actually avoids retaining data was not checked.
- Identity verification is a cost for the agency or the operator and a failure point for the user; services that do not require government-grade proofing (Upsolve, Dollar For, mRelief screening) avoid that cost but cannot submit on the user's behalf into systems that require it.
- Distribution through legal aid and caseworkers is where the documented growth and the documented AI pilots are; direct-to-consumer reach in the sources came from press (Dollar For) or search and word of mouth (not evidenced).

### Gaps
- No rigorous uptake evaluation through 211, libraries or legal aid.
- No documented data breach at a benefits or legal self-help nonprofit; no insurer quote specific to this software category.
- No source on WCAG compliance cost or limited-English-proficiency translation cost for these services, beyond the CfA statements above.
- No evidence on scam copycats of a named free service.

## 4. Where AI changes the economics (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The evidence so far supports AI as a caseworker or lawyer assist with human review and curated policy sources, not an autonomous consumer-facing adviser. Measured accuracy gains exist for caseworkers (about 40% in one trial) but base-model error rates on legal and benefits questions are high, and regulators have sanctioned unsubstantiated claims.

### Cited Findings
- Nava Labs ran a randomized trial with 125 caseworkers and a 14-week pilot with 61 caseworkers across six Los Angeles County organizations using Amplifi's Benefit Navigator; estimated accuracy improvement of about 40%, largest on difficult questions. About 65% of caseworkers with access used it, averaging 14 prompts each; usage declined over time and varied by site; reading level of responses was grade 10–12. Guardrails: out-of-scope questions get "I don't know" or a link. No systematic harm analysis and no cost figure are reported. — [Nava case study](https://www.navapbc.com/case-studies/evaluating-ai-assistive-chatbot-caseworkers)
- Earlier Nava experiments found the chatbot's accuracy highly variable against program rules, so navigators vetted output. — [Nava: Experimenting with AI-powered tools](https://www.navapbc.com/case-studies/ai-tools-public-benefits)
- Responses are much more accurate when a reliable state-specific policy document is supplied to the model. — [Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/publications/ai-powered-rules-as-code-experiments-with-public-benefits-policy/)
- CfA and Anthropic announced a SNAP Policy Navigator for caseworkers (built on Model Context Protocol, grounded in federal, state and county policy), with plans to extend to document review and plain-language communications; no accuracy or harm results published, and funding and pilot states not stated in the article. — [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-anthropic-ai-snap-caseworkers/)
- CfA's DC error-prevention tool is estimated to cut caseworker time per case by nearly 30% and recover about 650 caseworker hours a month (estimates, not measured outcomes). — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)
- Maryland contracted with Anthropic for an assistant to help residents apply for SNAP, Medicaid, cash assistance and WIC and to help caseworkers verify documents (secondary summary; not verified at source). — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/civic/civic-tech-partnership-to-help-govt-caseworkers-use-ai) (search-summary level; Maryland detail not confirmed on the page)
- A secondary source says a September 2026 Vals AI report found general-purpose models get SNAP questions wrong nearly a quarter of the time; I did not check the original. — [Future Leaders in AI guide (low authority)](https://futureleadersinai.com/guides/ai-public-benefits-navigation/)
- Legal accuracy: general chatbots hallucinated on 58%–88% of legal questions and purpose-built legal research tools erred 17%–33% in a Stanford RegLab/HAI study (2024 preregistered study; legal research, not consumer self-help). — [Stanford "Free?" study PDF](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf) **[2024 study]**
- Stanford Legal Design Lab: GPT-4-based models predicted legal-aid acceptance decisions with about 84% accuracy, erring toward caution; its 2026 class report sets pilot-readiness gates including accuracy against expert-reviewed ground truth, and one team's eviction-notice detector topped out near 20% accuracy. — [Legal Aid Intake & Screening AI](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/legal-aid-intake-screening-ai/); [AI for Legal Help 2026 class report](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/ai-for-legal-help-2026-class-report-scoping-building-and-testing-new-legal-aid-tech-systems/)
- Upsolve's $4.25M Gates grant (September 2024) funds an AI financial counselor (Upsolve Assist, first version expected January 2025); the release does not address safeguards or UPL. Dollar For plans an AI document-checking tool (about $250K with a CTO hire), unbuilt. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/upsolve-receives-4-2-million-grant-for-the-development-of-upsolve-assist-302237285.html); [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/)
- Regulators: the FTC's DoNotPay order bars "robot lawyer" claims without substantiation ($193,000). NCSC's August 2025 white paper treats Utah's sandbox as a model for AI-enabled legal services while noting no entity has deployed advanced generative AI; a Utah Supreme Court committee says the sandbox's access-to-justice impact is unproven. A federal court in New York has now upheld UPL limits against a nonprofit's challenge. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires); [NCSC](https://www.ncsc.org/sites/default/files/media/document/AI_UPL_WhitePaper.pdf); [Utah ad hoc committee agenda, October 2026](https://www.utcourts.gov/content/dam/committees/regulatory_reform/agendas/2026.10.19.Reg%20Reform_10.19%20agenda%20and%20materials.pdf)
- CfA's AI Studio Accessibility Project pilots: over 9,000 PDFs in Salt Lake City and over 55,000 in Georgia. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)

### Inferences
- AI lowers the cost of two things in the sources (form and PDF handling, caseworker lookup) and raises the cost of one (evaluation and monitoring: expert-reviewed test sets, human-in-the-loop, adoption support). Nava's finding that tools need training and peer support to be used suggests deployment cost is not just inference cost.
- The documented safe pattern is retrieval over curated state-specific sources with a refusal path, aimed at staff rather than the public. A small team offering AI answers directly to individuals would be outside any tested pattern and, for legal topics, within reach of UPL and FTC substantiation rules (FTC order text is about advertising claims).
- No evidence of AI harm at scale was found in the sources, but also no published harm monitoring beyond Utah's 20 complaints, which are about sandbox entities generally rather than AI.

### Gaps
- Accuracy and harm data for consumer-facing (not caseworker-facing) AI benefits or legal tools; Upsolve Assist results; Maryland–Anthropic results; verification of the Vals AI report.
- State attorney general or CFPB positions on AI benefits or legal advice tools (not searched).
- Cost to run an AI feature per user.

## 5. Picking a niche: depth in one state vs. breadth; partnering vs. going direct

### Takeaway
The long-run winners went deep with a government partner that owns the benefit (GetCalFresh in California with a state contract; Clear My Record with DAs; Direct File built inside the agency). Breadth-first, high-touch models (BDT) struggled when paying partners funded only a slice. Direct-to-consumer nonprofits with a narrow, high-value task (Dollar For: charity care; Upsolve: Chapter 7) have survived on philanthropy with small teams but remain concentration-exposed.

### Cited Findings
- GetCalFresh reached 70% of online SNAP applications in a single state and was sunset into the state's own system, with CfA keeping a distinct outreach contract. — [CDSS](https://cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/cdss-programs/calfresh-outreach/getcalfresh-transition); [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/)
- BDT served many states and programs with staff sized for 10–15 benefits while states paid for one or two. — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)
- Direct File scaled from 12 to 25 states with state-system integration (four state tax systems in the pilot), and states became partners; after closure, former staff are helping states build their own tools from the open-source code. — [IRS release IR-2024-122](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/398eb5a); [Free Software Foundation](https://www.fsf.org/blogs/community/irs-direct-file-released-as-free-software)
- Dollar For built one database of 2,000+ hospitals' charity-care rules (a national, single-task niche), with 15 staff. — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/podcast/an-arm-and-a-leg-podcast-charity-care-nonprofit-dollar-for-medical-bills/); [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/)
- JustFix's depth is one city (New York) and one issue (housing), funded mostly by NYC-based and national foundations, with revenue lower in 2024 than in 2019. — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/813080695); [Shelterforce](https://shelterforce.org/2022/03/24/how-hidden-property-owners-and-bad-landlord-patterns-are-revealed-in-nyc/)
- Suffolk LIT Lab's open-source toolkit lets courts and legal aid build their own interviews; the stated aim is a sustainable project with ongoing funding from courts that use it. — [Suffolk LIT Lab](https://suffolklitlab.org/reintroducing-the-document-assembly-line/)
- CfA stopped fiscal sponsorship of its volunteer brigade network after 2016 funding pressure and again in 2023, citing less funding for a decentralized network. — [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-local-brigades-ending/); [CfA Workers United](https://cfaworkersunited.com/stories/2023/08/31/code-for-america-lays-off-35-colleagues.html)
- A 2025 view: CfA says states faced deep cuts and little guidance after the January 2025 federal workforce reductions and passage of H.R. 1 in July 2025, and states are working to modernize IT before new SNAP requirements. — [CfA 2025 Impact Report](https://codeforamerica.org/impact/2025/); [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/code-for-america-anthropic-ai-snap-caseworkers/)

### Inferences
- For a team of a few developers, the sources point to a single task where the user's goal is clear and the data is public or user-supplied (Dollar For's hospital rules, Upsolve's Chapter 7 forms, JustFix's building data), with a payer such as a court or funder who sees the savings. A single-state build is the pattern behind both of the largest outcomes (GetCalFresh, Clear My Record in California), but those had a state counterpart paying for it; without a counterpart, a single-state build has nobody buying the run costs.
- Partnering with an existing nonprofit means the intermediary supplies trust and referrals (as LSC does for Upsolve) but also controls access; going direct needs press or search reach (Dollar For) and carries the support and scam-imitation load alone.
- Direct File's open-source code plus 18F and USDS' dissolution leave a gap for state-level or nonprofit tax tools, but the sources do not show whether any state or nonprofit has productionized it.

### Gaps
- No post-mortem comparing depth and breadth head to head; the above is inferred from individual cases.
- No evidence on state adoption of the open-sourced Direct File code after November 2025.
- Number of people served and cost per person through nonprofit partnerships vs. direct channels was not available for any case.
