# US money and government-paperwork services individuals cannot reach, and which software could deliver free or at cost

Method note for the report writer: about 40 tool calls, web search plus a few page fetches. Most facts below come from search-result summaries or a fetch summarized by a small model, not from reading the primary PDF in full. Where a figure comes from a secondary or low-quality site, it is marked. Figures older than 2022 are flagged "(pre-2022)". Current date is 2026-11-15.

## Tax filing: Direct File status, Free File, VITA, who pays to file, unclaimed credits, and what it takes to build

### Takeaway
IRS Direct File is dead for 2026 with no relaunch date; the IRS told states on 2025-11-03. What remains is Free File (industry partnership, about 3% take-up), VITA/TCE (grant-funded, demand exceeds grants) and the open-sourced Direct File code and Fact Graph that community forks (OpenFile and others) build on. Any new federal e-file product requires an EFIN, MeF transmitter/software-developer status and ATS testing, with fingerprinting and a suitability check for principals.

### Cited Findings
**Direct File status**
- On Nov. 3, 2025 an IRS official emailed state tax offices: "IRS Direct File will not be available in Filing Season 2026. No launch date has been set for the future." The program launched in 2024 with 12 states and expanded to 25 in 2025. — [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/direct-file-is-gone-what-s-next-for-free-filing-and-tax-pros/); [Forbes, Nov 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kellyphillipserb/2025/11/05/irs-kills-off--popular-free-direct-file-option-for-the-2026-tax-filing-season/)
- 2024 pilot (12 states): more than 3 million taxpayers checked eligibility; about 140,803 submitted accepted federal returns; filers got over $90 million in refunds. — [Wikipedia, IRS Direct File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Direct_File) (secondary; cites IRS after-action report Pub. 5969)
- Direct File was estimated to save filers about $160 in fees and time on average, roughly $11 billion a year in fees and time if universal, and to help families claim about $12 billion in extra credits; before it people spent about 9 hours and about $160 per year filing. — [Wikipedia, IRS Direct File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Direct_File) (estimates are advocacy-era IRS projections, not measured outcomes)
- Wind-down timeline: Feb 2025 DOGE at IRS, technical teams cut; May 2025 IRS staff released most of the codebase on GitHub; July 2025 IRS Commissioner Bill Long: "I don't care about Direct File. I care about direct audit"; Nov 2025 suspended. — [Wikipedia, IRS Direct File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Direct_File)
- The 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA) section 70607 directs Treasury to study public-private replacements and gave Treasury $15 million for research; any new program needs more congressional action. Sources conflict on whether the Act itself mandated ending Direct File (Wikipedia says the final Act did not). — [Wikipedia, IRS Direct File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Direct_File); [Instead summary](https://www.instead.com/resources/blog/irs-direct-file-is-gone-for-2026-and-how-to-save-more)
- Treasury's Oct 2025 report to Congress states Direct File was suspended and recommended focusing on Free File. — [GAO product page](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-22-105236) (GAO notes the Treasury statement); [Money.com](https://money.com/irs-direct-file-end-2026/)
- One secondary source says TIGTA found 751,000 taxpayers registered for Direct File in 2025 and 59% never submitted a return. Not verified against TIGTA. — [Visaverge](https://www.visaverge.com/taxes/irs-free-file-program-sees-just-3-participation-as-direct-file-suspension-looms/)
- IRS code is public: `IRS-Public/direct-file` is archived (no maintenance, "may contain unpatched or unknown vulnerabilities", 4.7k stars, 1.4k forks); the Fact Graph (Scala, XML knowledge graph, runs on JVM and via Scala.js in the browser) is also published as `IRS-Public/fact-graph`. — [GitHub IRS-Public/direct-file](https://github.com/IRS-Public/direct-file); [HN thread on Fact Graph](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45599567)
- Community successors: OpenFile (`openfiletax` org, "free tax tool based on IRS Direct File", about 179 stars, last updated Jan 9, 2026) and a fork `jz4396/direct-file` aiming at full Tax Year 2026 coverage, not government-affiliated. Activity and tax-logic quality not verified. — [GitHub openfiletax](https://github.com/openfiletax); [GitHub jz4396/direct-file](https://github.com/jz4396/direct-file)
- California FTB's CalFile (free state-only filing) continues; the planned Direct File data import into CalFile (announced for Jan 2026) is moot; no launch found. — [FTB 2024 release](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/newsroom/news-releases/2024-19-ftb-partners-with-irs-to-provide-free-tax-filing-for-californians.html); [CalFile](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/ways-to-file/online/calfile/index.asp)

**Free File and VITA**
- Free File: 2026 season, eight software companies; AGI limit $89,000 or less (about 70% of filers per one summary), plus active military; open through Oct 15, 2026. Search summary only. — [Instead](https://www.instead.com/resources/blog/irs-direct-file-is-gone-for-2026-and-how-to-save-more); [Livenow/Fox](https://www.livenowfox.com/news/irs-direct-file-canceled-2026-tax-changes)
- Free File take-up: TIGTA found about 3% of eligible taxpayers used it on average across processing years 2020–2024; CRS says about 3.2 million used it in FY2025 (peak about 6%, 5.1 million, in 2004; low 2% in 2016–2018). — [CRS IF11808](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11808) (via search summary); [TIGTA report hosted by Sen. Hickenlooper](https://www.hickenlooper.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Treasury-Dept.-Free-Tax-Filer.pdf)
- Free File MOU (ninth) runs to Oct 31, 2029 and no longer bars the IRS from building its own free direct e-file system. — [CRS IF11808](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11808); [Forbes 2024](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kellyphillipserb/2024/05/23/irs-extends-free-file-program-through-2029-while-direct-file-future-remains-uncertain/)
- VITA/TCE: IRS put $53 million into 2026 grants; awards to 315 VITA and 48 TCE applicants from 479 applications requesting more than $79 million (shortfall about $26 million). VITA income ceiling is about $67,000–$69,000 (sources differ). The old VITA locator URL was reported dead. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/irs-2026-tax-advice-grants-11447268); [Finopartners summary](https://thefinopartners.com/blogs/irs-expands-free-tax-help-programs-with-2026-grant-funding) (secondary)

**Who pays to file, and how much**
- NerdWallet estimates people eligible for Free File overpay by as much as $13 billion a year (about 70% eligible, fewer than 3% use it); basis is a Harris Poll finding that people who paid to file 2021 returns typically spent $150; the same page gives a lower bound of $3.5 billion. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/taxes/studies/data-taxpayers-freefile) (assumption-driven estimate)
- Professional preparer fees: NSA 2020–21 survey adjusted to Feb 2025 dollars gives $273 for a standard-deduction Form 1040 and $382 itemized. Older NSA editions are pre-2022. — [NSA survey summary via Tax Notes](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/other-documents/washington-roundup/national-society-of-accountants-says-return-preparation-rates-are-economical/wngl); [Money/NSA](https://content.money.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nsa-tax-preparation-survey-results-release.pdf?sfvrsn=0) (2015, pre-2022)
- Senate "Tax Maze" report (Warren) says Americans spend billions on preparation, about $200 per person (garbled snippet; treat with caution; report predates 2022). — [Tax Maze Report](https://www.warren.senate.gov/files/documents/Tax_Maze_Report.pdf)

**Unclaimed EITC and CTC**
- EITC participation: IRS state-table footnote says about 19.2% of eligible taxpayers did not claim EITC in TY2022 (about 80.8% participation); the CPS-based national estimate is 78%. CRS: roughly 11%–15% of potential EITC dollars go unclaimed (lower than nonparticipation because non-claimers are eligible for smaller credits). — [IRS EITC participation page](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/eitc-participation-rate-by-state); [CRS R43805](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R43805)
- Claimed amounts: 23.5 million filers received $68.5 billion in EITC for 2024 returns; max EITC for TY2025 is $7,830 (3+ children) and up to $632 with no children. — [IRS IR-2025-46](https://www.irs.gov/node/145076) (via search summary)
- Dollar estimates of unclaimed EITC: Chicago Fed gives more than $4 billion for 2019 if non-claimers got a quarter of the average credit, and about $11 billion if the average unclaimed is $1,500 (2019 data, pre-2022 vintage). — [Chicago Fed](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/profitwise-news-and-views/2022/millions-tax-refunds-unclaimed). A low-quality site says $7.3 billion a year; unverified. — [savemoneyquick](https://savemoneyquick.com/eitc-millions-of-americans-leave-this-credit-unclaimed-every-year/)
- IRS SOI has a TY2022 "EITC Credits and Deductions Gap Estimate" with claimed vs unclaimed tables; I could not extract the numbers. — [IRS SOI 24rpeitcunderclaims](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-soi/24rpeitcunderclaims.pdf)
- CTC: Columbia CPSP estimates 19 million children under 17 get less than the full $2,200 CTC under OBBBA because family income is too low (17 million before); OBBBA also requires a parent SSN, which ITEP says may exclude up to 2.7 million children. These are eligibility-design exclusions, not take-up gaps. — [ITEP](https://itep.org/child-tax-credit-2026-obbba-trump-taxes/); [CPSP brief](https://povertycenter.columbia.edu/sites/povertycenter.columbia.edu/files/content/Publications/Larger-Child-Tax-Credit-Design-Matters-CPSP-2025.pdf)

**What it takes to be an e-file provider / build on engines**
- Steps: IRS e-Services account approved; submit application with firm information and each Principal/Responsible Official; non-licensed principals are fingerprinted via the IRS-authorized Livescan vendor; IRS says up to 45 days; suitability check can include credit, tax-compliance and criminal background and prior e-file noncompliance; approval gives an EFIN. — [IRS: become an authorized e-file provider](https://www.irs.gov/e-file-providers/become-an-authorized-e-file-provider) (fetched)
- Software developers and transmitters first need an EFIN, then request the Transmitter option to receive an ETIN and test credentials and must pass testing (ATS) before acceptance; Pub 1345 (rules, revised annually), Pub 3112 (application), Pub 1436 (ATS guidelines for MeF individual returns), Pub 4164 (MeF guide for software developers/transmitters). An intermediate service provider that only resells software still needs its own EFIN. — [Pub 3112](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p3112.pdf); [Pub 1345](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1345.pdf); [MeF publications](https://www.irs.gov/e-file-providers/modernized-e-file-mef-user-guides-and-publications) (search summary; annual ATS re-pass is stated by an industry summary and not confirmed on an IRS page — [Fintegration](https://www.fintegrationfs.com/post/tax-filing-app-development-irs-efin-mef))
- Disclosure and security duties for a tax-prep product: IRC 7216 makes unconsented use/disclosure of return information a misdemeanor (up to 1 year, $1,000 fine; section 6713 civil penalty $250 per disclosure); consent must be written, signed, specific as to recipient and purpose, no opt-out; software/contractor disclosure allowed as necessary with written notice that 6713/7216 apply; FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) requires a written information security program from tax-prep firms of any size. — [Rightworks](https://www.rightworks.com/blog/irs-section-7216-consent-rules/); [QueryNow](https://www.querynow.com/industries/accounting/guides/section-7216-ai) (secondary summaries of the regulation)

### Inferences
- The cheapest legitimate path for a small team is likely not to become a federal e-file transmitter but to prepare returns and either partner with an existing transmitter/Free File-style channel or print-and-mail, because EFIN, fingerprinting, 45-day suitability and MeF ATS are a fixed regulatory cost. A free-to-user product would still carry that cost. (Reasoned from the IRS steps; no source prices it.)
- The Fact Graph/Direct File code reduces the tax-logic cost but is archived and unpatched, covers a limited return scope (simple W-2-type returns plus a few credits), and was not updated for OBBBA law changes (inferred; not verified).
- The strongest unmet tax need is probably EITC/CTC claim help for non-filers and filers with children or SSN complexity, since eligibility rules just got stricter (parent SSN) and 3-year late-claim windows apply. Free File's 3% take-up suggests that free software alone does not change behavior.

### Gaps
- Did not verify Direct File operating cost or 2025 return count from IRS/TIGTA primary documents.
- No clean primary figure for total dollars of EITC or CTC unclaimed by eligible nonparticipants for TY2022+; SOI table not extracted.
- Did not find a current state-by-state list of states running their own free filing (only California CalFile); did not research Open Tax Solver, OpenFisca, PolicyEngine as engines beyond mention.
- Whether the ATS must be re-passed annually was not confirmed on an IRS page.
- No data on the number of people who "pay to file" among Free File-eligible beyond NerdWallet's assumption-based estimate.

## Benefits: take-up gaps, churn, work requirements, screeners, and data access

### Takeaway
Take-up is high for SNAP (88% in FY2022) but low for WIC (about 54–56%), SSI among the elderly (roughly 50–70%) and SNAP among higher-income eligibles (about half); the larger near-term problem is paperwork churn: 25 million Medicaid disenrollments in unwinding, 69% procedural, and new work-reporting rules (SNAP now, Medicaid from Jan 1, 2027 in 44 states) that will add verification burden. Screeners exist (MyFriendBen open source, Benefit Kitchen API), but no standard API exists for third parties to submit applications to state portals.

### Cited Findings
**Take-up**
- SNAP: 88% of eligible individuals participated in FY2022, the highest since measurement began; nearly all households below poverty reached vs. about half of eligible households above poverty; 95% for those with no earnings; nearly 34 million eligible individuals participated; FY2022 is the latest estimate I found. — [USDA FNS FY22 trends](https://www.fns.usda.gov/newsroom/subscriber-updates/snap/fy22trends); [FNS state participation rates 2022](https://www.fns.usda.gov/research/snap/state-participation-rates/2022)
- SNAP participants: 43.9 million in 2022, 42.2 million in 2023. — [FNS FY2023 Characteristics report](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/snap-FY23-Characteristics-Report.pdf)
- WIC: about 11.79 million eligible in an average month of 2022 and 53.5% served; infants 78.4%, pregnant people 45.6%; more than half of WIC-eligible SNAP and Medicaid recipients do not take WIC; 2023 coverage was 56.1%. — [USDA WIC eligibility and reach 2022](https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/wic/eer/2022); [2023](https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/wic/eer/2023)
- Medicaid: take-up among eligibles rose from 76.5% pre-ACA to 85.0% post-ACA and 86.5% in 2023. — [Milbank Quarterly 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0009.70020)
- SSI: older studies, not current. AHEAD study found 53.9% participation among eligible oldest-old (pre-2022); SSA estimates 65–70% for the aged; Hemmeter et al. (2021, borderline) say estimates range from under 60% to 72% of potentially eligible age 65+; a letter telling people they were likely eligible raised take-up by 500% relative to no letter, but take-up in letter arms stayed under 7%. — [SSA Bulletin AHEAD](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v64n3/v64n3p38.html); [OES/GSA field experiment](https://oes.gsa.gov/assets/publications/1723.pdf)
- Dollar value of benefits left unclaimed: no single credible total found. BDT reported helping 120,000 people access $182 million in benefits in 2023 (a throughput benchmark). — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)

**ACA marketplace**
- Enhanced premium tax credits expired after 2025. Effectuated enrollment in Feb 2026 was 19.2 million, down about 12–13% (2.6 million) from 21.8 million; KFF projects 2026 average monthly effectuated enrollment of about 17.5 million (as low as 16.5 million) vs. 22.3 million in 2025; net premiums rose 58% on average ($113 to $178 per month per one summary). Individual-market enrollment fell from 25.2 million to 20.5 million June 2025–June 2026. — [KFF quick insight](https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/aca-marketplace-enrollment-is-down-by-3-million-after-big-jump-in-premium-payments/); [KFF 2027 open enrollment](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/9-things-to-watch-for-the-2027-aca-open-enrollment-period/)

**Churn and paperwork**
- Medicaid unwinding: as of Sept 12, 2024 at least 25.2 million disenrolled (31% of completed renewals); 69% of disenrollments were procedural (about 17 million, my arithmetic); five states above 50% disenrollment (MT, UT, ID, OK, TX); Medicaid/CHIP still 10 million above pre-pandemic. — [KFF end-of-unwinding](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/as-medicaid-unwinding-concludes-in-most-states-kff-finds-25-million-lost-medicaid-coverage-but-enrollment-is-10-million-higher-than-pre-pandemic-levels/)

**Work requirements (OBBBA, enacted July 4, 2025)**
- Medicaid: expansion adults 19–64 must show 80 hours a month of work, education, work programs or community service; states must implement by Jan 1, 2027 in 44 states (excludes WY, TX, KS, MS, AL, FL, SC); CMS issued its interim final rule June 1, 2026 (published June 3, 91 FR 33348; effective July 31, 2026); states must check data sources first, verify twice a year, and review 12 months of claims/encounter data; states had to start notices July–Sept 2026. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/cms-issues-interim-final-rule-implementing-medicaid-community); [Morgan Lewis, Nov 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/medicaid-work-requirements-remain-on-track-despite-partial-vacatur)
- Oct 29, 2026, D. Mass. (*Massachusetts v. Oz*) vacated the rule language requiring a functional impairment for five medical-frailty subcategories; the Jan 1, 2027 date and self-attestation limits stand; a preliminary injunction was denied July 31, 2026. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/medicaid-work-requirements-remain-on-track-despite-partial-vacatur)
- Coverage loss projections: pre-ruling about 5.3 million could lose Medicaid by 2034 (attribution unclear); CBO projects about $326 billion in 10-year savings. A 2023 CBO report found over 92% of adult Medicaid enrollees already worked, studied or were exempt (so losses are paperwork, not employment). — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/medicaid-work-requirements-remain-on-track-despite-partial-vacatur) (no source named in the summary for the 5.3 million)
- Early implementers: Montana planned July 1, 2026, Iowa Dec 1, 2026, Arkansas July 2026 with enforcement delayed to Jan 2027 (secondary; conflicting with Morgan Lewis note that no early implementers were identified). — [AMCP](https://www.amcp.org/regulatory-newsbreak/regulatory-newsbreak-cms-issues-interim-final-rule-medicaid-work-requirements)
- Vendor market for the Medicaid reporting systems: see CBPP's assessment. Nava's open-source OSCER works alongside state systems. — [CBPP vendor landscape](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/assessing-the-medicaid-work-requirement-vendor-landscape); [Nava case study](https://www.navapbc.com/case-studies/observations-integrated-eligibility-enrollment)
- SNAP: ABAWD age range expanded from 18–54 to 18–64 effective Nov 1, 2025; exemptions for veterans, homeless people and former foster youth removed; dependent-child exemption now under 14; state waivers terminated Nov 2, 2025. State admin cost share rises to 75% in FY2027; from FY2028 states with error rates over 6% pay 5%–15% of benefit costs. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Implementation_of_Supplemental_Nutrition_Assistance_Program_provisions_from_the_One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act_(2025)); [BenefitsUSA](https://benefitsusa.org/en/blog/obbb-snap-changes-2026-every-rule-that-changed) (secondary; check FNS)

**Screeners and access**
- MyFriendBen (Gary Community Ventures): open-source (`MyFriendBen/benefits-api`), uses PolicyEngine's API to calculate benefits, live in Colorado, tested with over 40 benefits; Urban Institute evaluation: median completion 6.6 minutes, about two-thirds of users learned of at least one new benefit. — [GitHub](https://github.com/MyFriendBen/benefits-api); [Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/myfriendben-benefits-screener)
- Benefit Kitchen: API returns dollar estimates across 25 federal/state/local programs, live in all 50 states, white-label for 211/311; pricing not found. — [Benefit Kitchen](https://benefitkitchen.com/)
- GetCalFresh: 2021 testimony said over 2.5 million applications and 250,000 renewals assisted; application cut from about 45 minutes to about 8–10; now in all 58 CA counties and it routes people to BenefitsCal for case management; no public screener API found. — [Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/news/california-launches-code-for-americas-getcalfresh-in-all-58-counties/); [House Rules testimony 2021](https://democrats-rules.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-rules.house.gov/files/HRDT-117-RU00-WState-RenteriaA-20210616.pdf)
- Propel claims over 5 million users (monthly per LinkedIn); no 2026-specific count found. — [Propel](https://www.propel.app/); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/propel-inc)
- Data/API access: CMS interoperability rules require state Medicaid agencies to run a standards-based Patient Access API for third-party apps (retrieval of data with beneficiary approval, not application submission); states can deny an app's connection under certain conditions. No standard API for third-party application submission to SNAP/Medicaid portals was found; Code for America's Michigan and other pilots appear to rely on state-specific integrations. — [Federal Register 2019 interoperability rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/03/04/2019-02200/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-patient-protection-and-affordable-care-act-interoperability-and); [Code for America Integrated Benefits Initiative](https://medium.com/code-for-america/reshaping-the-safety-net-the-integrated-benefits-initiative-state-cohort-32ea621e40d5)

### Inferences
- The work-reporting timeline gives a concrete near-term job for software: Jan 1, 2027 Medicaid reporting in 44 states plus SNAP ABAWD already in force. The ex parte data-first design means individuals mostly need help assembling proof of hours and exemptions; state-contracted vendors serve agencies, not individuals, so an individual-facing "document and report my hours" tool is a gap (inferred; CBPP vendor page was not read in detail).
- SNAP take-up is already high for the poorest; the remaining gap is higher-income eligibles (working households), WIC (about 45% unserved), SSI, and renewals. Screeners alone have thin evidence of enrollment effect; the BDT cost-per-application ($100s) vs mRelief-type tools shows cost depends on whether a human follows up.
- Without sanctioned submission APIs, most application help must be fax/email/mail or state-specific; a free tool is more tractable as screening plus document packaging plus renewal reminders than as direct portal submission.

### Gaps
- No credible national dollar total for eligible-but-unenrolled across SNAP, Medicaid, WIC, LIHEAP, SSI.
- No LIHEAP participation data found (only an Urban Institute overlap figure I could not tie to a take-up rate).
- No current SSI take-up estimate after 2021.
- Did not research mRelief usage, application costs/fees to individuals, or state programs separately.
- Did not verify SNAP OBBBA details against FNS primary guidance or the exact Medicaid look-back, ex parte, or good-cause rules in the IFR.
- Did not fetch the Medicaid IFR (91 FR 33348).

## Financial services: unbanked, cash and payday costs, credit reports, medical debt, student loans, FAFSA

### Takeaway
Unbanked rates are low and still falling (4.2%, 5.6 million households, FDIC 2023), but 14.2% are underbanked. The large, addressable paperwork problems are credit-report disputes (CFPB got 5.8 million credit-reporting complaints in 2025), medical debt (federal credit-reporting rule vacated July 2025; 15 states have their own laws), and the student-loan system, where SAVE ended and the RAP launched July 2026 amid repeated backlogs. FAFSA completion is at a record 59.1% for the class of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Unbanked/underbanked (FDIC 2023, released Nov 2024): 4.2% unbanked (5.6 million households, lowest since 2009), 14.2% underbanked (19.0 million), 81.6% fully banked; unbanked rates 10.6% Black, 9.5% Hispanic, 12.2% AI/AN, 1.9% White; 66.2% of unbanked households relied entirely on cash. — [FDIC press release](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2024/fdic-survey-finds-96-percent-us-households-were-banked-2023); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2024/11/fdic-percentage-of-unbanked-households-dropped-slightly-in-2023/)
- The 2025 survey (now titled the FDIC Survey of Household Use of Banking and Financial Services) was fielded with an instrument dated May 2025; I found no published 2025 results. — [OMB 3064-0215 record](https://omb.report/icr/202505-3064-002); [Philadelphia Fed WP 25-02](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/working-papers/2025/wp25-02.pdf)
- Payday loans: typical fee $15 per $100 for two weeks, about 400% APR (CFPB); Pew (2015–2016, pre-2022): about 12 million borrowers a year, $7 billion in fees (one Pew passage says $9 billion), average $520 in fees for $375 credit, average loan comes due as $430, about 36% of gross paycheck; CFPB found more than 80% of payday loans are rolled over or followed by another within 14 days. — [Pew fact sheet 2016](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/fact-sheets/2016/01/payday-loan-facts-and-the-cfpbs-impact); [Pew 2015](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2015/12/03/reforming-payday-loans-begins-with-understanding-how-they-really-work)
- Check-cashing fee totals: no credible figure found.
- Credit reporting complaints: CFPB received about 5,806,800 credit-reporting complaints in 2025 out of about 6,635,400 total (about 88%), vs 3,187,900 total in 2024; most common issue is inaccurate information; one summary says only 0.02% closed with monetary relief and that credit repair firms driving bulk disputes explain part of the jump (secondary). — [CFPB 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2025-cr-annual-report_2026-03.pdf); [NewsBreak summary](https://www.newsbreak.com/the-money-overview-380442759/4885781477485-credit-report-errors-drove-5-8-million-complaints-last-year-and-almost-none-brought-money-back)
- Credit repair law: FCRA lets consumers dispute free directly with bureaus; CROA bars credit repair firms from charging before services are performed, requires written notice of the right to dispute directly and a 3-business-day cancellation right. — [Credit Professional Services summary](https://creditprofessionalservices.com/your-rights/) (secondary; verify at 15 U.S.C. 1679)
- Medical debt: CFPB's Jan 2025 rule (would have removed medical debt from credit reports of about 15 million people holding about $49 billion) was vacated July 11, 2025 by E.D. Tex.; the court said FCRA preempts state limits; CFPB issued an Oct 2025 interpretive rule saying the same; 15 states have laws restricting medical debt reporting (CA, CO, CT, DE, IL, ME, MD, MN, NJ, NY, OR, RI, VT, VA, WA); a federal court in Aug 2026 reportedly held FCRA overrides a Texas rule in *CDIA v. Paxton*. — [Brownstein Hyatt](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/federal-court-vacates-cfpbs-medical-debt-rule-finds-fcra-preempts-state-laws/); [getoutofdebt.org](https://getoutofdebt.org/230550/medical-debt-credit-reports-2026) (secondary)
- Debt validation letters (FDCPA/Regulation F 30-day dispute) were not researched; no source.
- Student loans: SAVE ended by court order March 10, 2026; Repayment Assistance Plan launched July 1, 2026 (counts for PSLF); about 7.2 million borrowers were still on SAVE with servicer notices from July 1 and a 90-day clock; about 576,000 IDR applications pending in late Feb 2026 (down from over 730,000 at end of 2025 and almost 2 million in April 2025); PSLF Buyback about 90,000 applications pending March 2026; wage garnishment for defaulted borrowers announced for early 2026 (current status unverified; up to 15% of disposable income). — [GetOutOfDebt](https://getoutofdebt.org/231092/student-loan-repayment-changes-2026); [Forbes, Mar 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamminsky/2026/03/16/ominous-student-loan-backlogs-persist-as-7-million-borrowers-are-soon-forced-to-change-plans/); [Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/student-loan-assistance) (mostly secondary)
- FAFSA: 59.1% of the class of 2026 completed by June 30, a record (class of 2025: 53.8%; class of 2024: 46%); top states TN 72.7%, IL 71.6%, TX 69.3%; credited to early launch Sept 24, 2025 and state universal-FAFSA policies. — [NCAN via College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/83916/class-of-2026-sets-fafsa-completion-record-at-59-1-ncan-reports/); [NASFAA](https://www.nasfaa.org/news-item/39384/NCAN_2026_High_School_Seniors_Set_New_FAFSA_Completion_Record)
- Bill negotiation: not researched.
- CFPB Section 1033 (open banking, personal financial data rights): final Oct 2024, enjoined in E.D. Ky. (Oct/Nov 2025), compliance dates suspended; CFPB submitted its reconsideration to OIRA Aug 4, 2026; no revised text published as of Aug 2026. Without it, third-party apps keep using aggregators on bank terms. — [Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/section-1033-compliance-date-open-banking-rule-enjoined-and-under-reconsideration); [Open Banking Tracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status)

### Inferences
- The unbanked segment is shrinking, so a "bank access" tool matters less than tools around the paperwork those households and the underbanked meet: disputes, collections, student-loan plan switching.
- The student-loan RAP transition (7.2 million borrowers on a 90-day clock) and backlogs are the most time-sensitive consumer-paperwork surge in late 2026; a plan-switch/IDR/PSLF form helper is plausible but servicer and federal portals are the only submission channels (no API found).
- Credit dispute letters are pure document generation with a free legal right behind them (FCRA), but complaint volume is already inflated by credit repair firms and CFPB enforcement is retreating, so the value of a free tool is mainly doing it correctly and tracking deadlines.

## Precedents and why they fall short

### Takeaway
Benefits Data Trust shut in Aug 2024 because costs ran far above revenue under a call-center model funded two-thirds by philanthropy; Code for America's digital assisters scaled (GetCalFresh) but depend on state cooperation; Propel survives as a consumer app; Upsolve is a free bankruptcy tool; DoNotPay paid $193,000 after the FTC found its AI lawyer claims unsubstantiated. The paid players (TurboTax) have been the subject of FTC action over "free" claims, with the order since vacated by the Fifth Circuit.

### Cited Findings
- Benefits Data Trust: announced closure in July 2024, closed Aug 24, 2024; laid off 273; budget over $30 million, about two-thirds philanthropy and one-third state/local contracts; one staffer said it needed about $2.5 million a month and brought in about $1 million; some states paid for one or two benefits while BDT staffed for 10 to 15; cost per successful application was a few hundred dollars due to call-center staffing and marketing; on track for a $9.8 million loss in 2023 despite a $20 million unrestricted MacKenzie Scott gift in 2022; board replaced CEO Trooper Sanders in June 2024; it served 120,000 people and $182 million in benefits in 2023. — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/); [Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/health/benefits-data-trust-bdt-failed-acquirer-closing-august-24-20240730.html); [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2024/07/nonprofits-abrupt-closure-puts-access-public-benefits-risk/397968/) (staff accounts, not audited financials)
- Code for America: GetCalFresh (see Benefits section) plus the Integrated Benefits Initiative with CBPP, Nava and five states, now a Code for America program. — [Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/explore/bringing-social-safety-net-benefits-online/); [IBI](https://medium.com/code-for-america/reshaping-the-safety-net-the-integrated-benefits-initiative-state-cohort-32ea621e40d5)
- Propel: over 5 million Americans per its own site (2018: more than 1.5 million monthly users; 2020: 2 million plus about a million COVID-era additions). — [Propel](https://www.propel.app/); [TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/12/14/propel-series-a); [SiliconANGLE 2020](https://siliconangle.com/2020/05/13/solving-the-problems-of-poverty-through-technology-propels-freshebt-app-simplifies-food-stamp-finance-awssummit/)
- Upsolve: says 29,000 Americans have eliminated $1.3 billion of debt with its free tool (own claim; no date). — [Upsolve](https://upsolve.org/)
- DoNotPay: FTC final order (vote 5-0 on Jan 16, 2025; announced Feb 11, 2025) requires $193,000, notice to 2021–2023 subscribers, and bars "AI lawyer"-style claims without evidence; complaint said it never tested against a human lawyer's work or hired attorneys to check quality. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)
- TurboTax/Intuit: ProPublica found Intuit steered Free File-eligible users to paid products; the FTC order was vacated by the Fifth Circuit in March 2026 and remanded, per Wikipedia only (not confirmed with the opinion). — [Wikipedia, Intuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit)

### Inferences
- BDT's failure pattern is unit economics: human-heavy service paid per outcome and subsidized by grants. A software-only, at-cost model avoids the call-center cost but concedes the higher success rate of human follow-up (Technical.ly framing: funders weighed BDT's success rate against cheaper tools with lower rates).
- The DoNotPay order is a specific design constraint: any "AI helps with legal/government paperwork" tool must avoid performance claims it cannot substantiate and probably needs a human-review or tested-output story.

### Gaps
- Did not research Code for America's finances or staffing changes, Propel's business model/investors, Upsolve unit economics, mRelief, or Free File Alliance 2024–2026 changes in detail.
- Did not confirm the Fifth Circuit Intuit decision from a primary source.

## Barriers: e-file rules, benefits portals, FCRA/CFPB, data security, consent and representation

### Takeaway
Tax is the most structured barrier (EFIN, fingerprinting, 45-day suitability, ATS, 7216 consent, FTC Safeguards); benefits has no sanctioned third-party submission API found and relies on state-specific integrations; credit and debt actions are open to individuals by statute but the federal data-access (1033) and medical-debt rules are in flux; consent and authorized-representative mechanics vary by program and were only partly researched.

### Cited Findings
- E-file provider requirements, 7216 consent, and Safeguards Rule: see the Tax filing section (IRS provider page, Pubs 3112/1345, Rightworks/QueryNow summaries).
- Medicaid Patient Access API: states must offer a standards-based API for third-party apps with beneficiary approval; states may deny or discontinue an app's connection under certain conditions; this covers data retrieval, not applications. — [Federal Register 2019](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/03/04/2019-02200/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-patient-protection-and-affordable-care-act-interoperability-and); [2026 CMS interoperability rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/14/2026-07205/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-patient-protection-and-affordable-care-act-interoperability-standards) (not read in detail)
- State portals: Nava's Vermont case study describes a portal passing documents and applications to a legacy system through the same API endpoint as the old portal (a state-side pattern); Michigan/Minnesota digital-assister pilots appear to use state-specific integrations. — [Nava](https://www.navapbc.com/case-studies/observations-integrated-eligibility-enrollment); [Code for America IBI](https://medium.com/code-for-america/reshaping-the-safety-net-the-integrated-benefits-initiative-state-cohort-32ea621e40d5)
- FCRA/CFPB: FCRA preempts state medical-debt reporting limits per the E.D. Tex. vacatur and Oct 2025 CFPB interpretive rule (NCLC disputes the reach); 1033 rule enjoined and being rewritten, with "representative" and data-security definitions reopened. — [Brownstein Hyatt](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/federal-court-vacates-cfpbs-medical-debt-rule-finds-fcra-preempts-state-laws/); [Cozen](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/section-1033-compliance-date-open-banking-rule-enjoined-and-under-reconsideration)
- Data security: FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) applies to tax preparers; direct-file code repo itself warns it is archived and may contain unpatched vulnerabilities. — [QueryNow](https://www.querynow.com/industries/accounting/guides/section-7216-ai); [GitHub IRS-Public/direct-file](https://github.com/IRS-Public/direct-file)

### Inferences
- Scraping a state portal on a user's behalf is the high-risk path (terms of use, credentials in a third-party hands); the lower-risk designs are prefill-and-print, submission through existing assister channels (fax/email/county-accepted forms), or becoming a sanctioned assister partner of a state. (Not tested by any source I read; flag as design hypothesis.)
- Tax product consent can be built once (7216-compliant, separate, specific) and reused; the unknown is how states treat authorized-representative designations in benefits.

- Assister certification (e.g., ACA navigator/certified application counselor rules, state SNAP assister rules) not researched.
- Authorized-representative rules for SNAP/Medicaid, IRS Form 8821/2848 and Reg F consent not researched.
- State data-breach statutes and state tax-preparer registration not covered.
- 2026 CMS interoperability rule details not read.
- Tool budget (about 40 calls) used: topics left uncovered are bill negotiation, check-cashing costs, LIHEAP, mRelief, state programs, cost of applications/renewals to individuals, and CFPB/FCRA dispute rule specifics beyond the above.
