# US health, housing and life-admin tasks individuals cannot reach: free or at-cost software candidates

Method note for the writer: research was run through web search summaries plus six page fetches (KFF 2024 denials brief, KFF Health News presumptive-eligibility explainer, KFF SHIP page, and others were read via fetch or search extraction). Where a figure comes from a secondary or vendor page, it is flagged "(secondary)". Figures dated before 2022 are flagged "(pre-2022)". Current as of 2026-11-15.

## 1. Medical bills: billing errors and itemized bill requests

### Takeaway
Suspected billing errors are common by survey (43% of adults, 53% of those with debt, KFF 2022), but there is no reliable primary source for the oft-quoted "80% of bills have errors". Roughly half of people who suspect an error do not dispute it, which is the gap software could close (itemized-bill request, line-item checking, dispute letter).

### Cited Findings
- 43% of all adults, and 53% of adults with health care debt, said they had received a medical or dental bill they believed contained an error in the prior five years (suspected, not confirmed, errors; KFF 2022 Health Care Debt Survey) — [ConsumerAffairs summary of KFF survey](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/more-than-half-of-americans-have-owed-medical-debt-in-last-five-years-study-finds-071922.html); related KFF page: [Could Consumer Assistance be Helpful to People Facing Medical Debt?](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/could-consumer-assistance-be-helpful-to-people-facing-medical-debt/)
- Of adults with debt who got a bill they thought was wrong, 51% could not fix it or did not try to dispute it, and 32% said a disputed bill went to collections — same sources as above.
- Commonwealth Fund survey: 45% of insured working-age adults reported a bill or copay in the past year for a service they thought should have been free or covered; fewer than half of those challenged it; 38% of those who disputed had the bill reduced or eliminated — [Commonwealth Fund, Unforeseen Health Care Bills and Coverage Denials (Aug 2024)](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2024/aug/unforeseen-health-care-bills-coverage-denials-by-insurers)
- "80% of medical bills contain an error" and "$1,300 average error on a $10,000+ hospital bill" are repeated across vendor and blog pages with no traceable primary study; treat as unreliable — [Billflash roundup (secondary)](https://billflash.com/healthcare-industry-insights/medical-billing-statistics-in-the-healthcare-industry/), [Modern Healthcare](https://www.modernhealthcare.com/finance/identifying-addressing-common-medical-billing-errors-pre-post-payment/)
- Coding-accuracy evidence is better: the AMA is cited as estimating up to 12% of claims are submitted with inaccurate codes, and a CMS audit is cited for coding errors in 44% of sampled internal-medicine visits (secondary; not traced to primary) — [Medical billing statistics roundup](https://www.dialoghealth.com/post/medical-billing-statistics)
- Paid advocates typically charge 25-35% of savings (market range about 15-50%), or $100-$350/hour, or $300-$1,500 flat; one named service charges a $250 deposit plus 25% success fee on $5,000-$15,000 bills, so $2,000 of savings costs $1,000 (all vendor marketing pages, secondary) — [Pinnie Health](https://www.pinnie.com/articles/how-much-does-a-medical-billing-advocate-cost), [ClaimsMaximizer on Resolve Medical Bills](https://www.claimsmaximizer.com/blog/resolve-medical-bills-review), [medadvocate.net](https://www.medadvocate.net/guides/medical-bill-advocate/)
- Free options named: Patient Advocate Foundation and Dollar For (nonprofits) — [Health Bill Central guide (secondary)](https://healthbillcentral.com/blog/medical-bill-advocates-guide)

### Inferences
- The addressable group is large (tens of millions suspect an error) but the dollar value per case is uncertain because the confirmed error rate is unknown; do not size the market off "80%".
- A contingency advocate's cut (25-35%) is the price to beat; a free tool that gets patients to request an itemized bill and a standard dispute letter competes with the cheapest tier.

### Gaps
- No primary study of confirmed billing-error rates found.
- No source on how often itemized-bill requests are denied or how long they take; no primary data on how many people use medical-bill advocates.

## 2. Hospital charity care (financial assistance) and presumptive eligibility

### Takeaway
Eligibility far exceeds uptake: Dollar For says only 29% of eligible patients get hospital aid, and in Oregon about 80% of those helped in 2025 never applied because the hospital screened them. Six states mandate presumptive eligibility; federal law for nonprofits only requires "reasonable efforts" and a 240-day application window. This is the strongest "complexity gatekeeping" candidate in the health set.

### Cited Findings
- Only 29% of patients eligible for hospital financial assistance actually receive it (Dollar For, Nov 2025, via secondary roundup) — [Houston Public Media on Dollar For, Oct 28 2026](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/) and [Dollar For, The Path to Charity Care (2024)](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For_Path.pdf); the 29% figure was relayed by a search summary of these, not read on the page itself.
- Dollar For: 57% of patients who apply are approved (2024 report); in fully tracked applications 42% approved (June 2025) — [NY Focus, June 2025](https://nysfocus.com/2025/06/12/new-york-charity-care); [Dollar For Path report](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For_Path.pdf). The two rates may be different cohorts; sources do not reconcile them.
- Dollar For totals: about $180 million in medical debt eliminated and more than 8,000 Texans helped (14% of its national total) per Oct 2026 reporting; roughly 49,401 applications as of July 2026 (secondary) — [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/), [Fair Visit Health (secondary)](https://fairvisithealth.com/charity-care-guide/)
- About 80% of Oregon patients who received financial help in 2025 never applied (first 26 reporting hospitals) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- Six states require presumptive eligibility: California, Delaware, Illinois, Maryland, North Carolina, Oregon; Illinois, North Carolina and Oregon require screening before billing, California from 2027 — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- Oregon's screening threshold was $500 from 2024 and raised to $1,500 for a single visit in 2026; legislature weakened the 2023 law so more patients must apply — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/), [OPB, Apr 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/04/11/oregon-democrats-hospital-bills-medicaid-charity-care/)
- At least 11 states require hospitals to wipe out bills for low-income patients (Commonwealth Fund, cited by KFF Health News) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- Federal (ACA, IRC 501(r)): nonprofit hospitals must make reasonable efforts to find eligible patients before extraordinary collection actions; patients have 240 days from first post-discharge bill to apply, and a complete application pauses collection; the rule does not cover for-profit or public hospitals — [Medical Daily explainer](https://www.medicaldaily.com/hospital-bill-charity-care-screening-240-day-guide-479453), [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/health-science/healthcare/2026/10/28/565447/texas-nonprofit-hospitals-must-offer-charity-care-but-many-patients-never-realize-they-can-apply/) (secondary on the 240-day detail)
- Nearly 90% of tax-exempt hospitals used presumptive eligibility as of 2022, up from about 70% in the first year under 2016 rules (RTI International) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- An analysis found hospitals billed patients at least $2 billion in a year they likely did not owe — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- Hospitals spend under 1% to over 7% of expenses on financial assistance (KFF) and nonprofit tax exemption is worth an estimated $24 billion (2020 figure, pre-2022) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)
- 72% of adults with health care debt say it came from a one-time or short-term expense (KFF survey); $194 billion of medical debt in active collection (figure and date not verified here) — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-care-costs/medical-debt-hospital-bills-presumptive-eligibility-explainer-charity-care/)

### Inferences
- A free tool exists in this niche (Dollar For is a nonprofit), so the opening is scale, per-hospital policy lookup (each hospital's financial assistance policy and income cutoffs), and the 240-day deadline tracker rather than a new service category.
- Because hospital policies are published documents, an application builder that pre-fills each hospital's own form is a plausible build; liability is low if the user signs and submits.

### Gaps
- No national count of eligible-but-unapplied patients from a primary source; Dollar For's 29% is an advocate figure relayed by secondary reports.
- 57% versus 42% approval rate not reconciled.

## 3. No Surprises Act: patient-provider dispute resolution (PPDR) and the good faith estimate

### Takeaway
PPDR is open to uninsured and self-pay patients billed at least $400 over their good faith estimate, for a $25 fee. I could not find any actual PPDR filing counts; only HHS's 2021 projection of 26,000 per year. The much larger provider-versus-insurer IDR system is booming, which is not the same process.

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility: uninsured or self-pay patient (including short-term limited-duration plan enrollees) whose bill is at least $400 above the good faith estimate; bill must be within 120 days; $25 administrative fee, refunded if the patient prevails — [CMS NSA GFE and PPDR slides](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/gfe-and-ppdr-requirements-slides.pdf), [CRS IF12338 (Mar 2023)](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12338.3.pdf), [CMS consumer toolkit, June 2026](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-toolkit-combined.pdf)
- HHS's October 2021 rule projected over 26,000 PPDR cases per year (projection, not a count) — search summary of [CMS interim final rule guidance](https://www.cms.gov/CCIIO/Resources/Regulations-and-Guidance/Downloads/Guidance-Good-Faith-Estimate-Patient-Provider-Dispute-Resolution-Process-for-Providers-Facilities-CMS-9908-IFC.pdf); not read directly.
- If a state runs a PPDR-equivalent process, HHS defers to it — [CMS NSA overview slides](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/gfe-and-ppdr-requirements-slides.pdf)
- Contrast, provider-insurer IDR: 1,186,812 disputes initiated Jan-Jun 2025, 39% above the prior half; 1,082,247 determinations; about 20% ineligible — [CMS Federal IDR PUF background](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/federal-idr-supplemental-background-2025-q1-2025-q2.pdf), [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/no-surprises-disputes-idr-2025-cms/810525/)
- GAO found nearly 490,000 IDR disputes April 2022-June 2023, about 61% unresolved at that point (IDR, not PPDR) — [GAO-24-106335](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106335)

### Inferences
- The "how little PPDR is used" premise is unproven by the sources found: no count exists in what I reached. If the writer states it, it needs a CMS data request or a primary count.
- The $400 threshold and 120-day window make this a narrow, deadline-driven task; a good faith estimate checker (compare GFE to final bill, auto-draft PPDR initiation) is software-shaped, but the population is self-pay and uninsured only (insured patients are not covered by PPDR).

### Gaps
- Actual PPDR filing, approval and outcome counts: not found (searches returned only guidance and IDR figures).
- Share of eligible patients who receive a good faith estimate at all: not found.

## 4. Insurance claim denials and appeals (ACA marketplace, Medicare Advantage, employer)

### Takeaway
ACA marketplace insurers deny about 19% of in-network claims, yet under 1% of denials are appealed; in Medicare Advantage 11.5% of prior-auth denials are appealed and about 81% of those are overturned (2024 CMS data). Employer-plan denial and appeal rates were not found.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 plan year, HealthCare.gov: in-network denial rate 19% (about 85 million of 451 million claims); out-of-network 37%; combined 20% — [KFF, Claims Denials and Appeals in ACA Marketplace Plans in 2024](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2024/)
- Denial reasons (2024): administrative 25%, excluded service 13%, lack of prior authorization/referral 9%, medical necessity 5%, other 36% — same KFF brief
- Appeals: under 1% of in-network denials appealed (at least 262,983); 66% of appealed denials upheld by the insurer; external appeals at least 5,881, about 4% of upheld internal appeals — same KFF brief
- Earlier years: 2021 in-network denial rate 16.6%, appeal rate under 0.2% (90,599 of over 48 million; 2021 data) — [KFF, 2021 brief](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans/); 2023: 20% denial rate, 86 million denied — [KFF, 2023 brief](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023/)
- Prior authorization 2025 (KFF, Aug 2026): denial rates MA 12%, Medicaid managed care 14%, ACA marketplace 18%; overturn on appeal MA 67%, Medicaid 47%, ACA FFM 43% — [KFF, Prior Authorization Metrics](https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/prior-authorization-metrics-provide-new-insights-into-insurer-practices-but-gaps-remain/), summarized by [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/insurers-denied-between-12-18-prior-auth-requests-2025-kff)
- Medicare Advantage 2024: 52.8 million prior auth requests, 4.1 million fully or partially denied; 80.7% of denials overturned on appeal while only 11.5% were appealed (secondary summary of KFF/CMS) — [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2026/08/kff-insurers-denied-12-18-of-prior-authorization-requests-in-2025/)
- ERISA: a plan may not bar an authorized representative from pursuing a claim or appeal (29 CFR 2560.503-1(b)(4)); DOL Information Letter 02-27-2019 confirms participants may appoint representatives for claims and appeals; plans may set reasonable verification procedures — [DOL Information Letter 02-27-2019](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/information-letters/02-27-2019), [DOL](https://www.dol.gov/node/180325)
- Fully insured plans use the state external review process; self-funded employer plans fall under ERISA instead — [Hall Benefits Law](https://hallbenefitslaw.com/dol-provides-needed-clarification-on-authorized-representatives-acting-for-erisa-claimants/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Appeal-letter drafting (cite the denial reason code, plan documents, request internal then external review) has a measurable payoff signal: where appeals occur, 43-81% of prior-auth denials are overturned depending on payer type.
- Authorized-representative form generation is the key legal hook for acting on behalf of a patient without becoming a covered entity.

### Gaps
- Employer-plan (largest group) denial and appeal rates: not found.
- KFF 2024 brief does not report internal overturn rates; note 66% upheld implies most appealed claims are not overturned in marketplace plans, which differs from MA prior-auth.

## 5. Price transparency data now public

### Takeaway
Hospital and payer machine-readable files exist but remain hard to use: PatientRightsAdvocate's September 2026 report finds 49.4% of 2,000 hospitals fully compliant and only 18% posting dollar-and-cents prices for at least half of shoppable services.

### Cited Findings
- Compliance at a record 49.4% of 2,000 hospitals reviewed; more than 1,000 noncompliant; 18% post dollar-and-cents prices for at least half of shoppable services — [PatientRightsAdvocate, Eighth Annual Hospital Price Transparency Report (Sept 2026)](https://www.patientrightsadvocate.org/eighth-annual-hospital-price-transparency-compliance-report-september-2026), [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hospital-compliance-price-transparency-regulations-pra-report/830087/)
- CMS began enforcing updated machine-readable file requirements (allowed-amount fields, attestation, organizational NPI) on April 1, 2026 — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hospital-compliance-price-transparency-regulations-pra-report/830087/)
- Earlier PRA report found 34.5% full compliance (sixth report, date not captured) — [PRA statement](https://www.patientrightsadvocate.org/blog/pra-statement-on-turquoise-healths-mrf-tracker)
- Turquoise Health says most hospitals comply with federal rules but consumers still cannot easily compare — via [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hospital-compliance-price-transparency-regulations-pra-report/830087/)

### Inferences
- Raw files are free; value is in normalization and linking to a user's actual bill or good faith estimate (is my charge above the negotiated rate). Existing commercial players (Turquoise) are not free consumer tools for bill checking.

### Gaps
- No usage statistics for price tools; no source on how many MRFs are parseable; payer (Transparency in Coverage) file quality not covered.

## 6. HIPAA right of access: fees, delays and enforcement

### Takeaway
The right exists but enforcement is slow and case-by-case (55 OCR Right of Access actions since 2019). A 2019 audit-style study (pre-2022) found about half of providers not fully compliant. For third-party transmissions the federal fee limit does not apply after Ciox v. Azar.

### Cited Findings
- Access due within 30 days with one 30-day extension — [HHS OCR access guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html)
- OCR Right of Access Initiative began 2019; the Concentra settlement (announced Dec 16, 2025) was the 54th action ($112,500; patient made six requests) and the Azul Vision settlement ($50,000) the 55th (date reported as Aug 27, 2026 by a secondary source; complaint filed Apr 2023, records produced Jan 2025) — [HHS press release on Concentra](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-with-concentra.html), [Caruso Law on Azul Vision (secondary)](https://www.carusolawoffice.com/post/ocr-azul-vision-hipaa-right-of-access-settlement)
- OHSU paid a $200,000 civil monetary penalty in March 2025 for failing to give timely access — [Accountable HQ roundup (secondary)](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/recent-hipaa-violation-cases-latest-enforcement-actions-fines-and-key-takeaways-2026-update)
- The $6.50 flat fee is an optional safe harbor for electronic copies, not a cap — [HHS OCR access guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html)
- Ciox v. Azar (D.D.C., Jan 2020, pre-2022) vacated the extension of the patient-rate fee limit to patient-directed transmissions to third parties; limits still apply to the individual's own requests — [Quarles summary](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/ciox-case-changes-the-hipaa-rules-on-patient-rate-caps-for-access-to-health-records)
- Ciitizen scorecard (2019, pre-2022, not peer reviewed): of 51 providers audited 51% were not fully compliant or needed several attempts; a phone survey of 3,003 providers suggested up to 56% may not be fully compliant; 24% potentially violated fee limits; most common failure was refusing to send by email — [Ciitizen on medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/19004291.full.pdf), [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/most-providers-not-fully-compliant-with-hipaa-access-requirements-research/560954/)
- Yale study in JAMA Network Open of 83 hospitals: only 53% offered the entire medical record (date not captured here) — [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hospitals-failing-comply-hipaa-requirement-copies-medical-records/)

### Inferences
- A request-letter generator plus a 30-day timer and an escalation path to the OCR complaint portal is a small, low-liability build; the user sends the request, so no business-associate relationship is created.

### Gaps
- No current (2024-2026) systematic study of fees or delays; the compliance numbers are 2019.
- A tracker reports a coordinated-care final rule at OMB covering fee transparency; unverified.

## 7. Cures Act information blocking, record aggregation (Blue Button, CMS framework, TEFCA individual access)

### Takeaway
Enforcement of information blocking is ramping (ASTP/ONC letters of nonconformity Feb 2026, 1,600+ complaints reported) but no public OIG penalty was found. Plumbing for patient-side apps is real: CMS Aligned Networks (voluntary, pledged July 2025) and TEFCA Individual Access Services (IAS) with a short list of IAS providers.

### Cited Findings
- HHS announced renewed information blocking enforcement in September 2025; on Feb 11, 2026 ASTP/ONC began sending letters of nonconformity to certified EHR developers; OIG penalties can reach $1 million per violation for developers, HIEs and HINs; providers face Medicare disincentives — [Holland & Knight, Feb 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/the-wait-is-over-information-blocking-enforcement-is-officially-here), [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hhs-active-enforcement-information-blocking-rules/)
- About 1,300 claims via the information blocking portal as of Aug 31, 2025; one source says over 1,600 under review (secondary) — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/health-industry-washington-watch/102lu2u/hhs-announces-crackdown-on-health-data-blocking/), [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/information-blocking-enforcement-2026)
- HTI-5 proposed Dec 2025 to remove certification criteria; one source claims it was finalized Oct 2026 without changing information-blocking exceptions (single secondary source, verify) — [Distilinfo](https://distilinfo.com/2026/06/25/hhs-info-blocking-law-becomes-real-enforcement/) and [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hhs-active-enforcement-information-blocking-rules/)
- CMS Health Tech Ecosystem and Interoperability Framework launched July 2025: voluntary criteria for CMS Aligned Networks; apps may be commercial or non-commercial; 21 networks and more than 60 companies pledged; app categories include Kill the Clipboard and conversational AI; CMS to share Blue Button claims data through aligned networks; apps must follow the CARIN Code of Conduct or be certified through the Digital Medicine Society (per Fierce, secondary) — [CMS Interoperability Framework](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/health-technology-ecosystem/overview/interoperability-framework), [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/cms-trying-speed-tech-innovation-and-ai-patients-major-goalposts-set-2026), [CMS categories](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/health-technology-ecosystem/overview/health-tech-ecosystem-categories)
- Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, ZocDoc and UnitedHealth Group were among companies pledging to build conversational AI connected to CMS aligned networks — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhealthit/news/366628294/Tech-giants-providers-back-CMS-health-tech-ecosystem-initiative), [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/cms-trying-speed-tech-innovation-and-ai-patients-major-goalposts-set-2026)
- TEFCA: 23,000 organizations live and 100,000 unique connections (Aug 2026 RCE call); IAS Implementation SOP v3.0 effective Aug 3, 2026 requires IAS providers to use approved identity-verification vendors; IAS providers listed include Areti Health, Blackacre, Marble IAS, SMART IAS, with earlier lists naming Citizen.health, Fasten Health, HealthEx, Unblock Health and others — [Sequoia RCE Aug 2026 slides](https://rce.sequoiaproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/8.18.26-RCE-Info-Call_final.pdf), [RCE TEFCA for Individuals](https://rce.sequoiaproject.org/rce-tefca-for-individuals/), [RCE IAS SOP](https://rce.sequoiaproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SOP-IAS-XP-v3_June2026_Clean_-5081.pdf)
- HIPAA status of an app: once data reaches an app that is not a covered entity or business associate, at the individual's direction, HIPAA no longer protects it; an app is a business associate only when it acts on behalf of a covered entity (for example a provider-contracted portal); a developer can be a business associate for a health-plan app and not for its direct-to-consumer app — [HHS OCR, The access right, health apps and APIs](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access-right-health-apps-apis/index.html), [OCR health app scenarios](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-health-app-developer-scenarios-2-2016.pdf); the FTC applies instead ([FTC mobile health apps tool](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/mobile-health-apps-interactive-tool))

### Inferences
- A consumer-directed records aggregator is outside HIPAA but inside FTC Act (and FTC Health Breach Notification Rule) and state privacy law (e.g. Washington's My Health My Data Act, not sourced here); that is the regulatory posture for acting "for the patient" via the patient's own access right.
- The IAS list is short and each requires identity verification, so a small team probably integrates through an existing IAS provider rather than becoming one.

### Gaps
- No count of patients using TEFCA IAS or Blue Button 2.0.
- No primary source on API access fees or on EHR vendor patient-app approval delays.
- Whether any information-blocking penalty has actually been imposed: not found.

## 8. Prescription price comparison

### Takeaway
Discount cards often beat insurance for generics (40%+ of 20 studied drugs cheaper through GoodRx, 2023), so comparison is valuable, but GoodRx and Cost Plus Drugs already serve this, leaving little for a new free tool except bundling with plan data.

### Cited Findings
- University of Toledo study in Annals of Internal Medicine (2023): for 20 common generics, more than 40% would have been cheaper through GoodRx cards and about 20% through Amazon; estimated savings $1.8 billion (GoodRx) and $969 million (Amazon) — [UToledo news release](https://news.utoledo.edu/index.php/09_15_2023/utoledo-study-finds-insurance-doesnt-always-make-drugs-cheaper)
- JAAD 2025 study cited by a secondary site: GoodRx lowest price in 67.3% of dermatology prescriptions compared, Cost Plus 32.7%; Cost Plus median 47% below Part D for the same 77 drugs, but Part D cheaper for 9 of 77 (secondary, not verified) — [rxgrab comparison](https://rxgrab.com/articles/goodrx-vs-cost-plus), [Real Cost Report](https://www.realcostreport.com/healthcare/medical-costs/prescription-cost-comparison/)

### Inferences
- Low-priority for a free tool: incumbent coverage, thin differentiation, and cash purchases generally do not count toward insurance deductibles (noted by secondary sites), which an honest tool must warn about.

### Gaps
- No primary data on number of people shopping; no source on pharmacy-benefit-manager transparency rules; GoodRx's own business model not examined.

## 9. Medicare plan choice and SHIP counselor capacity

### Takeaway
SHIP serves about 4.3 million people a year (2022) on about $70 million (2025), roughly $1 per beneficiary, with nearly half the staff volunteers, while about two-thirds of beneficiaries never compare plans. That mismatch is a clear capacity gap, and one federal funding cut proposal makes it more acute.

### Cited Findings
- About 4.3 million beneficiaries, family members and caregivers received SHIP services in 2022, 1.7 million one-on-one; 2025 funding $70 million (up from $60 million in 2015), about $1 per beneficiary; nearly half of SHIP team members are volunteers; 54 SHIPs and about 2,000 local affiliates — [KFF, Role of SHIPs (Sept 24, 2025)](https://www.kff.org/medicare/the-role-of-ships-in-helping-people-with-medicare-navigate-their-coverage/)
- The average beneficiary in 2025 can choose from 42 Medicare Advantage plans and 14 stand-alone Part D plans; open enrollment accounted for one-third of one-on-one counseling sessions (2021 data) — same KFF page
- 2025 SHIP grant cycle covers April 2025 to March 2030; $52 million program funding, 54 expected awards; base funding $75,000 per state — [Grants.gov listing](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/cc681755-03c9-4769-8a9e-aa5a2c0e7114)
- AARP: Medicare enrollment grew 25% over a decade while SHIP funding grew 17% — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/health/coverage-access/state-health-insurance-assistance-program-ship-medicare-assistance/)
- A draft budget would have cut over $55 million in discretionary SHIP funding (about 80% reduction, leaving about $15 million mandatory), with ACL slated for elimination in the March 2025 HHS restructuring; KFF says Congress had not approved the replacement agency's funding (status late 2026 not confirmed) — [KFF SHIP page](https://www.kff.org/medicare/the-role-of-ships-in-helping-people-with-medicare-navigate-their-coverage/), [Georgetown CHIR, May 2025](https://medicare.chir.georgetown.edu/ships-provide-a-critical-service-for-medicare-beneficiaries-may-14-2025/)
- 65% of beneficiaries did not compare coverage during a recent open enrollment; in 2021 open enrollment 82% of MA-PD and 69% of stand-alone PDP enrollees did not compare drug coverage (2021 data, pre-2022 reference year) — [KFF, Nearly 7 in 10 do not compare](https://www.kff.org/medicare/nearly-7-in-10-medicare-beneficiaries-do-not-compare-coverage-options-during-open-enrollment/)
- Medicare Plan Finder is the free federal tool; the existing nonprofit scale of SHIP and these figures show it does not reach everyone (inference, no direct usage figure found).

### Inferences
- Plan-choice help is seasonal (Oct 15-Dec 7) and the consequential decision is drug-list and deductible fit; a tool that works from the user's actual drug list and pharmacy is the underserved piece.
- Medicare Part D plan recommendation tools sit near state insurance-producer licensing (agent/broker rules) and CMS marketing rules; I did not source these (see gaps).

### Gaps
- Per-counselor caseload figure not found; no source on how many people use Plan Finder.
- CMS Medicare marketing and broker compliance rules for an unpaid recommendation tool: not researched.

## 10. Housing: rental assistance, repair demands, voucher search

### Takeaway
Rental assistance is scarce and getting scarcer (ERA ended Sept 2025; only 1 in 4 eligible households gets any help; voucher waits near 2.5 years), voucher search success is about 57% (2022), and I found no national statistics on tenant repair demands or code complaints.

### Cited Findings
- More than 2.3 million households held Housing Choice Vouchers as of Sept 30, 2025; only 1 in 4 eligible households receives any rental assistance — [NLIHC 2026 Advocates' Guide, Housing Choice Vouchers](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2026/CH4/6_Housing_Choice_Vouchers_Chapter_4.pdf), [NLIHC voucher funding 2026](https://nlihc.org/resource/16-2-housing-voucher-funding-needs-2026)
- Households that receive vouchers wait nearly 2.5 years; of the 50 largest PHAs, only two have waits under a year (CBPP via NLIHC; analysis date not captured) — [NLIHC](https://nlihc.org/resource/households-receiving-housing-choice-vouchers-spend-nearly-25-years-waitlist)
- ERA1 provided $25 billion and ERA2 $21.55 billion; ERA2 funds could no longer assist renters after Sept 30, 2025; final reports were due Jan 28, 2026 — [NLIHC voucher funding and Treasury notes via secondary search summary](https://nlihc.org/resource/16-2-housing-voucher-funding-needs-2026); [Benefits USA (secondary)](https://benefitsusa.org/en/blog/emergency-rental-assistance-2026)
- Voucher success rate fell from 66% (2018) to 57% (2022) nationally; rural PHAs trail urban by up to 11 points; success and search time rise with PHA size — [NYU Furman Center, April 2025](https://furmancenter.org/files/Success_Rates_in_the_Housing_Choice_Voucher_Program_508.pdf), [NLIHC summary](https://nlihc.org/resource/success-rates-housing-choice-voucher-program-declined-amid-tightening-rental-housing)
- HUD baseline (pre-2022, year not captured): 63% success when the window extends to 240 days; median successful search 60 days — [HUD, Using Administrative Data to Estimate Success Rates](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/Voucher-Success_Rates.pdf)
- A 2025 study of 1,107,110 Craigslist listings in 77 mid-sized cities found express source-of-income discrimination even where laws prohibit it; Unlock NYC data show 11% of NYC landlords deny voucher holders using income and credit requirements — [Cityscape review, HUD User](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/periodicals/cityscape/vol26num2/ch7.pdf), [Taylor & Francis, Choice denied (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02673037.2025.2498385)
- Urban Institute recommends extending voucher search time from 60 to 120 days — [Urban Institute pilot study](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/pilot-study-landlord-acceptance-housing-choice-vouchers)
- Habitability: slightly more than half of states have repair-and-deduct statutes with caps often one or two months' rent (NHLP memo, undated and appears old); Nicole Summers' single-jurisdiction housing-court study finds meritorious habitability claims rarely won abatements — [NHLP 50-state memo](https://www.nhlp.org/wp-content/uploads/Research-Memo-re-50-State-Survey-of-the-Warranty-of-Habitability.pdf), [Summers, The Limits of Good Law](https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Limits%20of%20Good%20Law-%20A%20Study%20of%20Housing%20Court%20Outcomes.pdf)

### Inferences
- Voucher-holder listing search (filter by payment standard, SOI law, inspection status) is data-heavy and tied to PHA publication of payment standards; the pain is real but the lever is landlord behavior, which software does not fix.
- Repair-demand letter generators are well-suited (state-specific statutory notice forms) but outcome evidence is weak (Summers), and misuse (improper rent withholding) can cause eviction, a liability concern.

### Gaps
- No national counts of tenants filing repair demands or code complaints; AHS tables not queried.
- No data on ERA application burdens or on current state and local rent-assistance program availability late 2026.
- No count of unused vouchers.

## 11. Life admin after a death, and estate administration

### Takeaway
Checklists agree on the steps but I found no survey of hours or percent unprepared; costs are mostly attorney-driven (about 3-7% of estate by vendor estimates) with large state variation. This is a well-understood task with no found quantitative burden evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Survivors are ultimately responsible for making sure SSA is notified even though funeral directors usually report deaths; reporting is by phone (1-800-772-1213) or in person, not online; the month-of-death payment must be returned; a one-time $255 death payment may be available — [AARP checklist](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/when-loved-one-dies-checklist/), [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/checklist-what-to-do-when-loved-one-dies/) (secondary on the phone-only claim; verify against SSA)
- At least 10 death certificate copies recommended; notify one credit bureau and it notifies the others; close sole-name accounts, retitle joint ones; Medicaid may assert estate recovery — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/when-loved-one-dies-checklist/), [Legal Voice](https://legalvoice.org/after-death-occurs-checklist/)
- Total probate cost about 3-7% of gross estate (varies; one source says 3-8%, simple 2-5%); attorney hourly $200-$500, flat fees $3,500-$7,000 for simple estates; executor compensation typically 2-4%; court filing $200-$400; 34% of estates paid under $2,500 in legal fees and 23% over $10,000 (attorney firm survey) — [Greiner Law Corp (vendor/attorney marketing, secondary)](https://greinerlawcorp.com/typical-attorney-fees-for-estate-settlement/), [Swift Probate](https://www.swiftprobate.com/blog/how-much-does-probate-cost)
- A simple $80,000 estate might cost $1,500 in Texas and $7,000 or more in California (vendor estimate) — [Meet Alix](https://www.meetalix.com/resources/how-much-does-probate-cost)

### Inferences
- The consumer's unmet need is sequencing and per-institution paperwork (each bank, insurer, pension demands its own forms); no single federal "Tell Us Once" exists in the US (not directly sourced here).
- Document generation for small-estate affidavits is closer to unauthorized practice of law territory than checklists; state rules vary.

### Gaps
- No primary data on number of deaths per year handled by lay executors, hours spent, or unprepared share.
- Whether a US "tell us once" service exists: not checked.
- Rules on automated small-estate affidavits and UPL not researched.

## 13. Disability paperwork: SSDI initial claims, reconsideration and hearings

### Takeaway
SSA has cut the initial claim backlog (about 884,000 in July 2026 from nearly 1.3 million in FY2024; initial decisions average about 186 days) but pending hearings rose to about 362,000 with a 275-day average wait, and initial approval is reported at 36%. Most figures below come from secondary sites citing SSA's dashboard.

### Cited Findings
- Initial decisions averaged 186 days in July 2026 (220 a year earlier); initial backlog about 884,000 in July 2026 per SSA, versus nearly 1.3 million in FY2024 (another source says about 853,000) — [Disability Exchange, May 2026 update (secondary)](https://disabilityexchange.org/blog/ssdi-backlog-processing-time-may-2026-update/), [Saving to Invest (secondary)](https://savingtoinvest.com/ssdi-wait-times-2026-2/)
- Reconsideration averaged 212 days with about 302,000 pending (Aug 2026) — [Quikaid (secondary)](https://quikaid.com/article/initial-disability-decision)
- Pending hearings rose from about 278,000 (July 2025) to about 362,000 (July 2026), average 275 days to decision against an SSA goal of 270; by hearing office 220 days (Jackson MS) to 347 (Phoenix North) — [NewsBreak (secondary)](https://www.newsbreak.com/the-financial-wire-382710013/4897441487534-about-362-000-people-are-waiting-an-average-of-275-days-for-a-social-security-disability-hearing), [HAIA Foundation (secondary)](https://substack.haia.foundation/p/social-security-cut-the-wait-for)
- Initial approval rate reported at 36% (secondary, SSA source not identified) — [Quikaid](https://quikaid.com/article/initial-disability-decision)

## 14. Vital records and ID: birth certificates and REAL ID

### Takeaway
State fees for a certified birth certificate are about $9-$34 but vary in speed from days to months, and third-party vendors add fees; REAL ID enforcement started May 7, 2025 with 94% traveler compliance by late 2025, and a $45 TSA fee alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Certified copy fees mostly about $9-$34 (Florida $9, Michigan $34 including search; Massachusetts $20 in person but $54 online); processing from same-day in person to 4-12 weeks by mail, with some states reported at 12-24 weeks (sources conflict; secondary) — [Reference Source](https://referencesource.org/birth-certificate-fees-by-state/), [OnlineVitals](https://onlinevitals.com/birth-certificate-cost-by-state/), [VitalRecordsOnline](https://www.vitalrecordsonline.com/state-processing-time-vital-records/birth-certificate)
- Third-party services add their own fees on top of state fees — [Vital Docs](https://www.vital-docs.com/state-fees-vital-records/)
- REAL ID enforcement began May 7, 2025; about 81% of travelers compliant before the deadline, about 93% shortly after and 94% by late 2025 (TSA figures relayed by secondary sites); a birth certificate is an accepted identity proof; the $45 fee for enhanced identity verification (ConfirmID) from 2026 — [TSA REAL ID FAQs](https://www.tsa.gov/realid/realid-faqs), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/05/07/us/real-id-travel-dmv-passport-license), [GovFacts (secondary)](https://govfacts.org/government/federalism/federal-state-relations/real-id-enforcement-what-it-requires-and-the-45-tsa-fee/)
- A 2025 federal rulemaking found less than 60% of licenses in 34 states were REAL ID compliant; Georgia and Florida over 99%, Alabama 33% (secondary; date of data not captured) — [Visapics (low-quality aggregator)](https://visapics.org/news/real-id-enforcement-2025-new-airport-id-requirements-now-in-effect-may-7-update-20251118)

### Inferences
- State vital-records ordering is a pure pass-through with identity verification (often via ID.me or LexisNexis); free tools would be a directory that sends users to the official state office and avoids vendors that add fees, but identity verification and state-by-state eligibility rules limit automation. Vendor marketplaces already exist.

### Gaps
- No primary figure for how many adults lack a birth certificate or REAL ID documents; no data on married-name mismatch rates; fee tables are vendor-compiled and conflict.
- Standard vital-record eligibility rules (who may order) not sourced.

## 15. Cross-cutting: regulatory barriers and the strongest build candidates

### Takeaway
The cleanest regulatory structure is for the user to act and the tool to draft and track: HIPAA does not reach an app that receives data at the patient's direction (FTC and state privacy law do), ERISA lets patients appoint authorized representatives, and no source found requires licensing for a non-attorney billing advocate, though I did not find a source on state unauthorized-practice-of-law limits either.

### Cited Findings
- HIPAA: an app acting solely for the consumer is not a covered entity or business associate; the BA test is whether it creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity — [HHS OCR, The access right, health apps and APIs](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access-right-health-apps-apis/index.html), [OCR scenarios](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-health-app-developer-scenarios-2-2016.pdf)
- ERISA authorized representatives are permitted for claims and appeals but may lack standing to sue; a separate signed designation form is recommended over a general assignment of benefits — [DOL Information Letter 02-27-2019](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/information-letters/02-27-2019), [Word on Benefits (court standing)](https://blog.ifebp.org/legal-legislative-reporter-medical-provider-may-not-bring-claim-on-behalf-of-participants-and-beneficiaries/)
- For Medicare appeals an authorized representative is defined by reference to state or other applicable law — [CMS Medicare Claims Processing Manual ch. 29](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c29.pdf)
- Self-funded employer plans fall under ERISA; fully insured plans under state external review — [Hall Benefits Law](https://hallbenefitslaw.com/dol-provides-needed-clarification-on-authorized-representatives-acting-for-erisa-claimants/) (secondary)

### Inferences
Ranking by (people affected) x (documented uptake gap) x (low regulatory friction), for the writer to challenge:
1. Charity care application and 240-day tracker (strong uptake gap, free incumbent exists but small, low friction).
2. Insurance denial appeal letters with authorized-representative form (large denial volume, under 1% appeal rate in ACA; high overturn where appealed).
3. HIPAA record request plus itemized bill request letters (cheap, feeds #1, #2 and SSDI evidence).
4. Good faith estimate checker and PPDR starter (narrow population, no volume data).
5. Medicare plan-choice with a user's drug list (strong need, but CMS marketing and broker rules unresearched).
Lower: Rx price comparison (incumbents), vital records (identity verification, vendor pass-through), SSDI (waiting is not software-addressable), housing (landlord-side bottleneck), after-death (no burden data).

### Gaps (whole brief, what was not covered or verified)
- Stopped at about 37 tool calls. Not covered: state insurance producer and broker licensing for plan-recommendation tools; state UPL rules and any state licensing of billing advocates; liability/E&O norms; FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer-health privacy laws (Washington My Health My Data Act) beyond the FTC mention; itemized-bill request denial data; PPDR filing counts (none found); employer-plan denial and appeal rates; ERA application burden; code-complaint and repair-demand statistics; US "tell us once" analogue; HIPAA personal representative and proxy access rules for caregivers; REAL ID document-gap populations.
- Many figures are from search-engine extractions of secondary pages (flagged); the primary pages directly read were the KFF 2024 denial brief, KFF Health News presumptive-eligibility explainer and KFF SHIP page. SSDI numbers in particular need a check against SSA's dashboard before publication.
- Possible conflicts: Dollar For approval rate 57% vs 42%; SSDI initial backlog 884,000 vs 853,000; 2023 ACA denials 73 million (secondary) vs 86 million (KFF); information-blocking "penalties imposed" unverified.
