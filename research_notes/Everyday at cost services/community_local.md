# Community and local organizing tools US groups overpay for or patch together (offer-at-cost candidates)

Method note: figures come from WebSearch summaries plus WebFetch of primary pages where they loaded. Many prices come from third-party comparison blogs (some written by competitors); each is flagged. Vendor pricing pages often could not be fetched (SignUpGenius pricing page loaded without prices, Meetup help page returned 403, myTurn pricing 404). Figures older than 2022 are marked **[OLD <year>]**. Today is 2026-11-15.

## 1. What groups use today, and what it costs (Facebook Groups, Nextdoor, SignUpGenius, Meetup, TeamSnap and league apps, Eventbrite, church software, Splitwise, WhatsApp/GroupMe)

### Takeaway
Most of these are free to the group but monetized through ads, fees on money moved, or per-seat pricing that stops fitting a tiny volunteer group. The sharpest recent pain points are Meetup's roughly 3x organizer price rise (June 2024), Buy Nothing's 2025 trademark takedowns of Facebook groups, per-ticket and per-registration fees (Eventbrite about 6.6% + $1.79 per ticket in the US; TeamSnap 3.25% + $1.50 per transaction), and opaque quote-only league pricing.

### Cited Findings
**Facebook Groups**
- Free; problems cited are shrinking organic reach as Meta pushes Reels and recommended content, no feed control, required personal accounts (admin access runs through a personal account that can be disabled), and no member email export. Several vendor blogs claim only 2-5% of members see a given post; this is unverified and comes from competitors. — [Mighty Networks](https://www.mightynetworks.com/resources/disadvantages-of-facebook-groups), [Circle](https://circle.so/blog/facebook-groups-alternatives), [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-getting-rid-of-groups-business-impact/)
- Meta suspended thousands of groups worldwide in a roughly 24 to 48 hour window in June 2025, and announced in October 2025 it was removing Group community chat. These are per a search summary of vendor blogs (competitor sources), not Meta. — [Zelos](https://getzelos.com/best-facebook-groups-alternatives-for-work-teams)

**Buy Nothing (Facebook-born gifting groups; the app dispute)**
- Founded 2013 on Bainbridge Island, WA by Rebecca Rockefeller and Liesl Clark; Wikipedia states 7.5 million community members and over 4,000 volunteers; the app launched 2022 with a paid option; the project is a benefit corporation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project)
- The app charges $54.99/year or $5.49/month (free membership also possible) and "struggled to attract users". — [SF Standard, 2025-11-07](https://sfstandard.com/2025/11/07/buy-nothing-group-facebook-taken-down-trademark-infringment/)
- "Buy Nothing" and "Buy Nothing Project" trademarks were registered in 2022 (USPTO case 90464570). In late Oct 2025 the project had groups taken down that used the name without registering with it; the "Buy Nothing No Rules" San Francisco group (116,000+ members) was removed Oct 30, 2025 via an automated trademark takedown, restored Nov 6 under the name "Buy Not a Thing No Rules San Francisco". The project said about 1% of its Facebook groups were affected. That group's three admins spend roughly 3 hours a day moderating. — [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/11/07/buy-nothing-group-facebook-taken-down-trademark-infringment/)
- Earlier dispute (2021-2023): founders wanted an app, tried fundraising within the community, members objected, they then pursued venture capital (reportedly approaching "upwards of 100 investors"), triggering backlash; many local groups split off and renamed. The source calls the app "not very good". — [Marketplace, 2023-05-16](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2023/05/16/what-happened-to-the-buy-nothing-project)
- Wikipedia also says many affected groups renamed and some were reinstated by Facebook under new names. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project)

**Nextdoor**
- Free for neighbors; revenue is nearly all advertising. FY2025 revenue $257.6M (+4%); Platform WAU fell to 21.0M in Q4 2025 (-5% YoY) after a metric change; claims 105M+ verified neighbors in 350,000+ neighborhoods in 11 countries; net loss $54.2M; restructuring announced August 2025. The "no subscription fees / no transaction fees" claim is from a third-party analysis. — [Nextdoor 10-K FY2025 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1846069/000184606926000023/kind-20251231.htm), [thinkinsights summary](https://thinkinsights.net/leadership/nextdoor-business-model-2026)

**SignUpGenius**
- Free plan (ads shown to sign-up visitors, unlimited sign-ups). Paid: Silver $11.99/mo ($8.99 billed annually), Gold $29.99/mo ($22.49 annually), Platinum $59.99/mo ($44.99 annually); Silver includes 1 ad-free sign-up, Gold 10, Platinum unlimited. Annual cost therefore about $108 / $270 / $540. Prices were reported as checked 2026-10-27 by an aggregator; ITQlick disagrees ($14.99/$34.99/$54.99) and I could not read signupgenius.com/pricing (page loaded without prices). — [Capterra listing](https://www.capterra.com/p/135392/SignUpGenius/), [SignUpKit](https://www.signupkit.co/signupgenius-pricing), [ITQlick](https://www.itqlick.com/sign-up-genius/pricing)
- SignUp.com (the competitor): free Basic with ads; paid annual-only Starter $99.99/yr, Plus $249.99/yr, Max $499.99/yr. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135391/SignUp-com/) and [RallyUp comparison](https://rallyup.com/blog/volunteer-management-software/) (RallyUp is a competitor to VolunteerLocal; treat with caution)
- VolunteerLocal: no free plan; $600/yr Discover or $2,400/yr Grow. — [RallyUp comparison](https://rallyup.com/blog/volunteer-management-software/)

**Meetup (price changes)**
- Bending Spoons completed its acquisition of Meetup on January 24, 2024 (one search summary says "January 2025" in a different place; the 2024 date is the one cited by multiple sources). Organizer price rise took effect June 6, 2024, first since 2019; applied to all Standard organizers (at renewal) and new Pro organizers. — [Meetup help center via search summary](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/28677808413197-Organizer-Subscription-prices-overview), [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40854275)
- Meetup's own help page (as summarized; direct fetch returned 403): Standard from $29.99/mo, $99.99 per 6 months, or $174.99/year, max 3 groups; Pro from $55 per group per month ($47 on 6-month term). Other sources report higher: $44.99/mo, $178.99 per 6 months, $299.99/yr (PriceTimeline, Feb 2025). Sources conflict; "from" prices vary by location and offers. — [PriceTimeline](https://pricetimeline.com/data/price/meetup), [Medium](https://medium.com/@hoffbits/pay-to-skip-why-meetup-risks-undermining-its-own-community-635cb1c25f9c)
- Organizer reports: six-month plan $29 to $99, another $98 to $178 twice a year. — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40854275)
- Meetup's own 2025 progress report: 300,000+ groups in 10,000+ cities; member dues now processed through Stripe. — [Meetup Blog](https://www.meetup.com/blog/2025-meetup-progress-report/)
- One report says organizers who leave cannot export member contact data; single source, unverified. — [nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)

**TeamSnap and league apps**
- TeamSnap per-team plans (listed 2026-09-30): Free under 15 players; Premium $10.83/mo billed annually ($18.99 monthly); Ultra $13.75/mo annual ($25.99 monthly). Clubs & Leagues is quote-only; last published figure was $199/month ($2,388/yr) per a competitor (Clubside); older GetApp listing said from $599/yr. Payment processing 3.25% + $1.50 per transaction. A worked example claims a 42-team club at $19.99 per team per month pays about $10,075/yr (vendor-blog estimate). — [Second Slide](https://go.secondslide.io/teamsnap-pricing/), [Clubside](https://clubside.io/blog/teamsnap-alternative), [GetApp](https://www.getapp.ca/software/102771/teamsnap)
- LeagueApps and SportsEngine do not publish pricing; third-party estimates conflict. LeagueApps: per-registration $2-$5 plus 2-3% processing (RaftLabs, a dev shop) vs $99-$499/mo plus 2.9% + 30c (Gohurdle) vs about $119/mo plus 3.5-4.9% + $0.99 (WifiTalents). One source says PlayMetrics acquired SportsEngine in May 2026. — [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/cost-to-build-app-like-leagueapps), [WifiTalents](https://wifitalents.com/best/sports-league-registration-software/), [Jerseywatch](https://www.jerseywatch.com/blog/best-sports-league-management-software)

**Eventbrite**
- US: 3.7% + $1.79 per ticket service fee plus 2.9% payment processing per order (about 6.6% + $1.79; roughly $5.09 on a $50 ticket, about 10%); no fee cap; free tickets have no fees; Eventbrite Pro from $15/month does not lower ticket fees. These are third-party figures, not Eventbrite's own page. — [Eventcloud](https://www.eventcloud.io/blog/how-much-does-eventbrite-charge-2026), [CheckoutPage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/eventbrite-fees)

**Church management software**
- Breeze (now Tithely Church Management) $72/month flat ($864/yr); Planning Center People free, other modules $15-$239/month each (as of 2026-10-14), full small-church suite estimated about $1,680/yr; Pushpay unpublished, third-party estimates $199-$599+/month for giving, $500-$1,500+/month full suite. Processing (2.9% + $0.30 card, 1% + $0.30 ACH) can exceed the subscription: $100,000 of giving costs about $3,050 in fees. — [Church Stack](https://www.churchstack.io/blog/average-cost-church-management-software), [ChurchPosting](https://churchposting.com/resources/software/planning-center-vs-breeze-vs-churchtrac/), [CharityCharge](https://www.charitycharge.com/nonprofit-resources/church-management-software/)

**Splitwise**
- Free tier limited (commonly cited 4 expenses per day, not officially documented); Pro not priced on Splitwise's site; US App Store $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr; two blogs claim later increases ($49.99/yr, about $60/yr). Sources are mostly competitor blogs; one says the free tier had no daily limit before 2023. — [Split Circle](https://split-circle.com/en/blog/splitwise-free-vs-pro), [Spliteo](https://www.spliteo.app/blog/splitwise-pro-price/), [Splitt](https://splitt-app.com/blog/splitwise-premium-price-worth-it.html)

**WhatsApp/GroupMe chaos**
- No sourced findings. Not researched (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The price cliffs are at "a few volunteers organizing one thing": SignUpGenius paid tiers ($108-$540/yr) mainly buy ad removal and count limits; Meetup is $175-$300/yr per organizer for a small group; TeamSnap-style per-team pricing scales with team count, so a league pays thousands.
- Money-movement fees (about 3% + fixed amount) are the real recurring cost for leagues, churches and event hosts, and a self-hosted tool cannot avoid them; at cost still means passing Stripe fees through.
- The Buy Nothing situation shows the trust failure mode to avoid: a company-controlled brand over volunteer-run local groups. Local-first, admin-owned data is the differentiator.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation of SignUpGenius current prices (pricing page unreadable), Meetup current prices (403), or TeamSnap Clubs & Leagues price (quote-only).
- Nothing researched on WhatsApp/GroupMe limits, GroupMe ownership, or typical "chaos" costs; no source on shared-calendar tools (Google Calendar is free and not priced).
- No Facebook Groups usage count from Meta.

## 2. Unserved or poorly served needs and cost per group per year (sign-up sheets, potlucks, carpools, lending libraries, swap boards, volunteer scheduling, league registration and payments, dues, shared calendars)

### Takeaway
Sign-up sheets, volunteer scheduling and league registration are served but with ads, per-seat tiers or fee drag. Lending libraries have a thin, fragmented software market (Lend Engine is cheap, myTurn pricing not found). I found no sourced evidence on potluck, carpool, dues or shared-calendar tools, so those are untested ideas.

### Cited Findings
- Volunteer/sign-up: free tiers exist but show ads (SignUpGenius, SignUp.com); paid is $100-$600/yr for generic scheduling and $600-$2,400/yr for VolunteerLocal. — sources under section 1 above ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135392/SignUpGenius/), [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/blog/volunteer-management-software/))
- Free volunteer software exists for small nonprofits, e.g. Ember Volunteers free for nonprofits under $50K budget. — [RallyUp](https://rallyup.com/blog/volunteer-management-software/)
- Lend Engine (library-of-things software, lend-engine.com): Free (100 items/100 members, 1 site, 10 live events), Starter $12.50/mo (500/500), Plus $25/mo (2,000/2,000, 10 sites), Business $50/mo (10,000/10,000, 30 sites); 30-day Plus trial; annual prices not listed; each plan can take card payments. That is about $150/$300/$600 per year (my arithmetic). — [Lend Engine pricing](https://www.lend-engine.com/pricing)
- Library-of-things membership fees charged to patrons: $40/yr (LLoTTs on myTurn), UK examples GBP 9.95 and GBP 25. — [search summary of library pages](https://llotts.myturn.com/library/)
- myTurn: no pricing found. A Circular Library Network modified myTurn kiosk license is EUR 2,700 including 16 electronic locks and a one-year subscription. — [Lend Engine search results](https://slot.lend-engine.com/?redirectedFromHTTP=)
- Open-source resource sharing/scheduling tools exist: Leihs, LibreBooking, Karrot. — [Civil Society Technology Foundation](https://civilsociety.dev/articles/open-source-community-tools/)
- Church software and Open Collective already show the dues/payments pattern: Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 card rate is the baseline. — [Church Stack](https://www.churchstack.io/blog/average-cost-church-management-software), [Zeffy comparison (competitor)](https://www.zeffy.com/compare/zeffy-vs-opencollective)
- Open Collective fiscal hosts typically charge 4-15% host fee (Open Source Collective 10%); hosts pay Open Collective a 15% platform share on legacy pricing; new organization customers on activity-based plans from March 2026. — [Open Collective docs](https://documentation.opencollective.com/why-open-collective/pricing), [OSC docs](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/fees)

### Inferences
- A one-group per-year cost range from sourced figures: sign-up sheet $0-$540; league software $0 to several thousand plus about 3% of money collected; tool library software $0-$300; church management $864-$7,200+.
- Gap with weakest competition appears to be small lending libraries needing simple check-out plus free tier; but Lend Engine's free and $150/yr tiers are already cheap, so a new entrant must beat that on ownership/self-host rather than price.
- Potluck, carpool, and dues tools are likely served only by generic apps (sign-up sheets, Splitwise, group chat); this is inference, unsupported by sources.

### Gaps
- No sourced pricing or user complaints for potluck, carpool, swap/give-away board tools, or shared-calendar tools for clubs.
- myTurn standard pricing not found; no Share Starter pricing.
- No data on how many groups actually pay for any of these.

## 3. How many such groups exist in the US (youth sports leagues, Buy Nothing, tool libraries, congregations, Meetup groups)

### Takeaway
Congregations are the best-counted (about 373,000, but from a 2017 estimate). Tool libraries number about 200+ (directory floor) in the US; seed libraries roughly 500-1,000; Meetup 300,000+ groups worldwide; no usable count of youth leagues exists.

### Cited Findings
- Congregations: about 373,000 US religious congregations (335,000 Protestant/other Christian, 23,000 Catholic/Orthodox, 15,000 other faiths) **[2017 estimate, flagged as older than 2022]**; 2025 FACT survey of 7,453 congregations: median 70 regular participants, median income up from $120,000 (2020) to $205,000 (2025); 78% of attendees are in the largest 13% of congregations. 44,000+ nondenominational churches. — [Hartford Institute fast facts](https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/fast-facts-on-american-religion/) (via search summary; page not fetched)
- Youth sports participants: 27.3 million kids aged 6-17, 55.4% in 2023; HHS 2030 target 63.3%. — [Project Play](https://projectplay.org/youth-sports/facts/participation-rates). Project Play State of Play 2025: 55% played organized sports (NSCH), family youth-sports costs up 46% since 2019. — [Project Play](https://projectplay.org/state-of-play-2025/participation-trends)
- NCYS members serve about 60 million registered participants (about 44 million individual kids), with 220+ member organizations; no count of leagues nationally. — [NCYS](https://ncys.org/)
- Named bodies: AYSO claims 800 leagues (regions) and over 630,000 participants (another source says 400,000); US Youth Soccer nearly 2.7 million players and 10,000 clubs and leagues (Soccer America says US Soccer to absorb USYS by 2028); Little League 2.4 million+ kids per a TeamSnap blog (date unclear). Little League chartered 7,444 leagues in 2006, 6,252 in the US **[OLD 2006, forum post]**. — [Wikipedia AYSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Youth_Soccer_Organization), [Soccer America](https://www.socceramerica.com/can-u-s-soccer-tame-the-wild-west-of-american-youth-soccer/), [Baseball Fever](https://www.baseball-fever.com/forum/general-baseball/baseball-101-coaching-fundamentals/32904-little-league-numbers)
- Buy Nothing: 7.5 million members, 4,000+ volunteers (Wikipedia); no group count found. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project)
- Tool libraries: 204+ US libraries in the Tool Library Finder directory (top states CA 24, WA 16, NY 13, MI 13, OR 11; the "+" suggests a floor; counting date not given). Older: about 40 (2013) to 60+ (2015) **[OLD 2013-2015]**; Eartheasy says 80+ in US and Canada (undated). — [Tool Library Finder](https://toollibraryfinder.com/), [USDN](https://sustainableconsumption.usdn.org/initiatives-list/tool-lending-libraries)
- Seed libraries: Seed Library Network map about 1,500 self-reported worldwide (Sept 2026); academic estimate roughly 500-1,000 in the US (about 2024); about 500 around 2018-2019 **[OLD]**. — [Urban Farm Podcast, 2026-09-19](https://www.urbanfarm.org/2026/09/19/1006-seed-chat/)
- Meetup: 300,000+ groups in 10,000+ cities worldwide (Meetup 2025); Wikipedia says 60 million users; a low-quality review site claims about 500,000 active members, conflicting. — [Meetup Blog](https://www.meetup.com/blog/2025-meetup-progress-report/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup)
- Nextdoor: 350,000+ neighborhoods globally. — [Nextdoor 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1846069/000184606926000023/kind-20251231.htm)

### Inferences
- The addressable population for "small team offers at cost" is congregations (about 373,000, median 70 people) and local youth leagues (likely tens of thousands, no sourced count) far more than tool libraries (about 200). Tool libraries are a showcase niche, not volume.
- Because congregations are small (median 70), the Breeze $864/yr and Planning Center price points are material to many of them.

### Gaps
- No count of US youth sports leagues or clubs; no current Pop Warner or Little League numbers; no count of PTAs, HOAs, mutual-aid groups, neighborhood associations or buy-nothing groups (Buy Nothing group count not found).
- Hartford page not fetched directly; the 373,000 figure is a 2017 Brauer estimate.
- No toy-library count.

## 4. Free, open-source or co-op alternatives and why they stall

### Takeaway
Plenty of alternatives exist (Mobilizon, Gancio, Hylo, Loomio, Open Collective, Discourse, Karrot), but I found no direct post-mortem on why they stall; sources only hint at small user bases, rough UX and fragmented stacks. Inference: onboarding and the need to stitch tools together are the main blockers.

### Cited Findings
- Mobilizon: federated events and groups, started by Framasoft in 2019, maintained since 2024 by Kaihuri with NLnet support; 97,210 accounts across 134 servers (FediDB, 2026-10-13). — [Wbcom review and FediDB via search summary](https://wbcomdesigns.com/mobilizon-review/)
- Mobilizon review: small user base, less polish and integrations than Eventbrite/Facebook Events; managed hosting exists. — [Wbcom Designs](https://wbcomdesigns.com/mobilizon-review/)
- Gancio: self-hosted shared agenda with anonymous event publishing, RSS/ICS, embeds, ActivityPub. — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/gancio)
- Open-source Meetup alternatives list marks many tools inactive (attendize, Calendar.social, GetTogether). — [coderbyheart/open-source-meetup-alternatives](https://github.com/coderbyheart/open-source-meetup-alternatives)
- Hylo: free tier with full platform, unlimited members and groups, 1 GB storage, 50 MB attachment cap; Pro est. $60/mo per group, Assistant est. $20/mo (both "coming soon" with waitlist); Whitelabel custom; stewardship support $100/mo (Seed), from $1,000/mo (Blossom), $300 support call, $2,500 onboarding. No payment fees mentioned. — [Hylo pricing](https://www.hylo.com/pricing)
- Heylo (a different product): Base $0, Pro $59/mo per group, Business $199/mo per group. — [Heylo pricing](https://www.heylo.com/pricing)
- Discourse hosting: Starter about $20/mo, Pro $100/mo, Business $500/mo (sources vary; confirm at discourse.org). — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/community-platform/discourse/), [Stackscored](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/community-platforms/discourse/)
- Loomio: 150K+ users in 100+ countries (claim). — [Wbcom search summary](https://wbcomdesigns.com/mobilizon-review/) (loosely sourced)
- Civil Society Technology Foundation catalogs about 100 open-source community tools across 19 categories (including Karrot, Leihs, LibreBooking for resource sharing); it gives no adoption-barrier analysis. — [civilsociety.dev](https://civilsociety.dev/articles/open-source-community-tools/)

### Inferences
- Likely stall reasons (not sourced): a group must adopt several separate tools (events, decisions, money), most groups lack a person who can self-host, no mobile apps or push for most, and network effects favor where members already are (Facebook, group text).
- The Hylo precedent (free forever tier with upsells that are "coming soon" and stewardship services) shows the sustainability question; at-cost hosting with a Stripe pass-through is a cleaner model.

### Gaps
- No post-mortems or interviews on why Mobilizon/Gancio/Loomio adoption stalled; no data on mobile app availability; no co-op platform findings beyond Open Collective; Ourboard not researched.

## 5. Running costs and risks (moderation, abuse and spam, child safety, payment fees, support load)

### Takeaway
Payment processing (about 3% plus a fixed fee) is the dominant variable cost; youth leagues add real legal obligations (amended COPPA in force since April 22, 2026; Safe Sport Act duties on organizations); moderation load on large groups is hours per day.

### Cited Findings
- Moderation: the three admins of a 116,000-member Buy Nothing SF group spend about 3 hours a day moderating; Buy Nothing founders said they worked up to 8 hours a day unpaid. — [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/11/07/buy-nothing-group-facebook-taken-down-trademark-infringment/)
- COPPA: amended rule published in the Federal Register 2025-04-22, effective 2025-06-23, full compliance deadline 2026-04-22. Requires separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing a child's data to third parties, limits retention, adds biometric identifiers to personal information, requires written security and retention programs. FTC issued a policy statement on 2026-02-25 limiting enforcement for age-verification-only data collection. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule), [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html), [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)
- COPPA applies to services directed at children under 13 or with actual knowledge; youth-league registration collecting kid data should be handled as parent-account data (inference below). The cited sources cover the rule's content, not league-specific advice.
- Safe Sport Act (signed Feb 2018): adults authorized to interact with minors must report suspected abuse to law enforcement or the Center for SafeSport within 24 hours; organizations must train adults, adopt a risk management program and policies, limit one-on-one adult-minor interactions, and provide a standardized reporting mechanism. Law-firm and governing-body summaries say it reaches most amateur youth organizations, including YMCAs, church and municipal leagues, even those not tied to a national governing body (this reach claim is from advocacy/law summaries, not statute text). — [Sadler Sports Law](https://www.sadlersports.com/new-safe-sport-act-applies-amateur-sports-organizations/), [Great American Insurance](https://www.greatamericaninsurancegroup.com/content-hub/news-details/protecting-youth-sports-participants), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_Sport_Authorization_Act)
- Payment fees as benchmarks: Eventbrite 6.6% + $1.79 per ticket; TeamSnap 3.25% + $1.50; church card processing 2.9% + $0.30 and ACH 1% + $0.30; Open Collective hosts 4-15% on top of processor fees. — sources under sections 1-2 ([Eventcloud](https://www.eventcloud.io/blog/how-much-does-eventbrite-charge-2026), [Second Slide](https://go.secondslide.io/teamsnap-pricing/), [Church Stack](https://www.churchstack.io/blog/average-cost-church-management-software), [Open Collective docs](https://documentation.opencollective.com/why-open-collective/pricing))
- Meetup moved all member dues payments to Stripe (2025). — [Meetup Blog](https://www.meetup.com/blog/2025-meetup-progress-report/)

### Inferences
- An at-cost operator could pass Stripe's published rates through with no markup; any platform "tip" model (as Open Collective uses) is optional.
- Offering a tool for adult organizers only (parents register kids; no accounts for under-13s; no kid photos or messaging) is the lightest COPPA and SafeSport footprint, but this needs legal review; I am not giving legal advice.
- Support load is dominated by non-technical volunteer admins; no sourced figure.
- A tool that holds sensitive child data and abuse reports raises liability beyond what a hosted sign-up sheet has.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for spam/abuse rates, support hours, or cost per group of hosting.
- No SafeSport statute text or state mandated-reporter specifics fetched; no data on insurance requirements.
- No Stripe nonprofit discount check; Stripe's pricing page not fetched.
