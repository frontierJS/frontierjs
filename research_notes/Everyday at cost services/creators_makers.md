# Creator and maker services a small team could offer at cost (US, as of Nov 2026)

Method note: official pages that loaded are cited as primary (Gumroad, Patreon, Eventbrite, Stripe, Ghost, beehiiv, Big Cartel, Micro.blog, AWS SES, IRS via search snippet). Etsy, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Substack and Patreon help pages returned 403/404, so those rates rest on third-party fee guides that agree with each other; they are marked "secondary". Most fee blogs are written by competitors. Nothing here is older than 2022 unless flagged.

## 1. Real take rates for small sellers and creators (2025-2026), with worked examples

### Takeaway
At small ticket sizes, a platform's headline percentage is only part of the loss. Stripe's fixed 30 cents alone takes 6% of a $5 payment. Etsy costs about 11% on a $25 item, and roughly 26% if an Offsite Ad drove the sale. Gumroad takes 12% to 16% direct, or 30% through Discover. A $5/month supporter nets about 81% on Patreon, Substack and Buy Me a Coffee, and about 91% on Ghost and beehiiv Lite (processor fee only). Handmade, digital, membership, ticketing and newsletter services each sit in a different fee shape, listed below.

### Cited Findings

**Card-processing floor (applies everywhere)**
- Stripe US standard: 2.9% + 30¢ per successful domestic card transaction; +1.5% for international cards; +1% if currency conversion; dispute fee $15 on receipt, $15 to respond manually (refunded if you win), $15 to appeal (not refunded); Billing pay-as-you-go 0.7%; ACH Direct Debit 0.8% capped at $5 — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)
- Worked (my arithmetic from the Stripe rate): processing alone costs $1.025 on $25 (4.1%), $0.445 on $5 (8.9%), $0.387 on $3 (12.9%), $0.329 on $1 (32.9%).

**Etsy (US, secondary sources; Etsy's own fee page returned 403)**
- Listing $0.20 per item, renews every 4 months; transaction fee 6.5% of item price plus shipping and gift wrap charged (US sales tax excluded); payment processing 3% + $0.25 per order, charged on the full total including shipping and tax; Etsy Plus $10/month — [checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees), [craftybase](https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees) (the summary of the nine search results I ran said all agreed on these rates; see the [results list](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees))
- Offsite Ads: 15% for shops under $10k trailing-year sales (can opt out), 12% for shops at $10k+ (mandatory), capped at $100 per order, only on orders from an Etsy-placed ad; launched Feb 2020 — [FeeProofed history](https://www.feeproofed.com/guides/etsy-fee-increase-history-2018-2026/)
- Fee history: transaction fee went 5% to 6.5% on Apr 11, 2022 (still 6.5%); one-time shop setup fee $15 to $29 added Sep 2024 for new shops; Dec 2025 Instant Transfer fee 1% (min $0.25, max $1.00) with standard deposits free; Dec 2025 sales tax on Etsy fees for Texas and Washington sellers; Regulatory Operating Fees changed Jun 22, 2026 in UK/EU/other countries — [FeeProofed](https://www.feeproofed.com/guides/etsy-fee-increase-history-2018-2026/) (a third-party guide; the source itself says it did not verify against Etsy's pages)
- 2022 strike: sellers on "vacation mode" Apr 11-18, 2022 over the fee rise, mandatory Offsite Ads and Star Seller; Etsy said under 1% of sellers took part and made one concession (Star Seller threshold of 5 sales, down from 10); the 6.5% stayed — [CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2022/04/16/etsy-sellers-eye-forming-a-union-after-going-on-strike.html?amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/etsy-seller-strike-fee-increase-222042949.html) (flag: 2022 data)
- Worked $25 item, free shipping, no tax, no ad (my arithmetic): $0.20 + 6.5% ($1.625) + 3% + $0.25 ($1.00) = $2.825, about 11.3%. With a 15% Offsite Ad sale: +$3.75 = $6.575, about 26.3%. Fee-guide figure for the typical sale: about 9.5% + $0.45 before ads, 15-25% with Offsite Ads — [checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees)

**Gumroad**
- 10% + $0.50 on direct sales; flat 30% on sales found through Discover; no monthly fee; since Jan 1, 2025 Gumroad is merchant of record and says it handles all sales tax collection and remittance — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- The page does not itemize card processing. Sources conflict on whether 2.9% + 30¢ is on top of the 10% for direct sales: Dodo Payments, Swell and Checkout Page say it is; another says there is none — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/gumroad-fees-explained), [Swell](https://www.swell.is/content/gumroad-pricing), [Roo/beehiiv summary](https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/gumroad-fees-2026)
- Tiers (as low as 2.9%) were replaced by a flat 10% + $0.50 in 2023; the 2025 change added merchant-of-record service without changing the rate (secondary: same sources, summary of [search results](https://ecomm.design/gumroad-review/)). Direct rate reported to drop to 5% + $0.50 after $20,000 in a month (secondary, single-source-type claim) — [swell.is](https://www.swell.is/content/gumroad-pricing)
- Worked $25 download (my arithmetic): direct $3.00 (12%); $4.03 (16.1%) if processing is separate; Discover $7.50 (30%).

**Patreon**
- 10% platform fee, "plus payment processing, currency conversion, and payout fees, and applicable taxes" — [Patreon pricing](https://www.patreon.com/pricing). The page does not give processing, micropayment (under $3) or payout rates, and the help page returned 403.
- Pages published after Aug 4, 2025 are on one plan with 10% (replacing Pro 8% and Pro+Merch 11%); earlier pages keep their old rate but reportedly lose it on unpublishing; secondary sources disagree on legacy tiers (5/8/12 vs 5/8/11); processing 2.9% + $0.30; total effective 13-15%; currency conversion 2.5% — [Zanfia](https://zanfia.com/blog/patreons-10-plus-apples-cut-the-2026-math-for-moving-off-patreon/), [Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/patreon-review-2026-is-it-worth-it), [Eden](https://eden.so/blog/patreon-alternatives/) (competitor-authored)
- App Store: Apple takes 30% on purchases made through its in-app purchase (15% after a subscription's first year). New purchases in Patreon's iOS app have been subject to it since Nov 2024; the default is an iOS price about 43% higher (1/(1-30%)); US fans can use web checkout and avoid the fee. Apple told Patreon (reported Jan 28, 2026) to move creators on legacy billing (about 4%) to subscription billing by Nov 1, 2026 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/apple-tells-patreon-to-move-creators-to-in-app-purchase-for-subscriptions-by-november/), [Patreon help: iOS IAP for fans](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/27992185537037-How-iOS-in-app-purchases-work-for-fans), [Mac Observer on the earlier delay](https://www.macobserver.com/news/patreon-creators-get-more-time-before-apple-takes-30-percent-of-ios-subscriptions/). I found nothing dated after Nov 1, 2026 on enforcement.
- Worked $5/month on web (my arithmetic): $0.50 + $0.445 = $0.945; creator keeps $4.055 (81.1%). iOS in-app, absorbed: Apple takes 30% first, then Patreon 10% of the rest; one secondary source says a $5 tier nets $3.00 — [Zanfia](https://zanfia.com/blog/patreons-10-plus-apples-cut-the-2026-math-for-moving-off-patreon/)

**Substack**
- 10% of paid subscriptions plus Stripe 2.9% + 30¢; some sources add 0.7% recurring-billing; custom domain a one-time $50 — [Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/blog/what-substack-is-and-how-you-can-use-it-for-subscriptions-or-email-marketing/), [Spark/Substack](https://sparkleon.substack.com/p/substack-fees-explained-what-you-keep-when-you-go-paid-on-substack) (Substack's own help page returned 403)
- Worked $5/month: $0.50 + $0.445 = $0.945; creator keeps about $4.05 (81%), about $4.02 with the 0.7% — Bluehost link above.

**Ko-fi and Buy Me a Coffee (secondary; sources conflict)**
- Ko-fi: tips 0% by default per most sources, but new accounts may default into "Contributor" status taking 5% of tips unless switched off; 5% on shop sales, memberships and commissions, removed by Gold ($12/month per recent sources; $6 per older ones); processor fees on top; Ko-fi's Gold page reportedly says "You no longer need Ko-fi Gold" — [Cartmango](https://cartmango.com/ko-fi-fees/), [Owelet](https://owelet.app/blog/ko-fi-fees); two sources say otherwise on the tip fee — see [Fourthwall](https://fourthwall.com/blog/ko-fi-alternatives)
- Buy Me a Coffee: 5% platform fee on everything, no paid tier to remove it, plus Stripe 2.9% + 30¢, and one source adds 0.5% payout, 1% international, 0.5% on subscription payments — [Donatr](https://donatr.ee/blog/kofi-vs-buymeacoffee-vs-donatr/), [Misar](https://www.misar.blog/compare/ko-fi-vs-buy-me-a-coffee). Worked $5: $0.25 + $0.445 = $0.695 before extras, about 86% kept.

**beehiiv, Ghost, Buttondown, Micro.blog, Big Cartel (subscription model, no cut)**
- beehiiv: Free up to 2,500 subscribers; Lite $49/mo annual ($59 monthly) at up to 1,000 subs; Pro $95/mo annual; at up to 10,000 subs Lite $119 annual / $139 monthly; the page says 0% of paid-subscription revenue on Lite and Pro, Stripe processing still applies — [beehiiv pricing](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing)
- Ghost(Pro): Starter $18/mo (no paid subscriptions), Publisher $29/mo, Business $199/mo, billed yearly; 1,000 members on lower tiers; 0% Ghost fee on paid subscriptions, processor fees apply — [Ghost pricing](https://ghost.org/pricing/)
- Buttondown (aggregators, mixed): free to 100 subscribers; $9/mo at 1,000; $29 at 5,000; $79 at 25,000; paid subscriptions an add-on at $9; sources disagree on which tier includes it — [Capterra listing](https://www.capterra.com/p/10008721/Buttondown/), [Ownletter](https://ownletter.com/reviews/buttondown), [CreatorStackClub](https://www.creatorstackclub.com/software/buttondown)
- Micro.blog: $1 (Micro.one), $5, $10 Premium (adds newsletters), $15 Family (podcast and video hosting), $20 Studio; yearly $50/$100/$150; the page also says Micro.one includes podcast hosting with MP3 up to 75 MB (the page is internally inconsistent on which tier hosts podcasts) — [Micro.blog pricing](https://help.micro.blog/t/pricing/)
- Big Cartel: Platinum $15/mo (7-day trial), Diamond $30/mo, Gold free with limited features; the page does not state transaction fees or product limits — [Big Cartel pricing](https://www.bigcartel.com/pricing)

**Eventbrite (US)**
- 3.7% + $1.79 service fee per paid ticket plus 2.9% payment processing per order; free events carry no fees; buyer pays by default unless the organizer absorbs — [Eventbrite pricing](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/pricing/)
- One source says processing is 2.9% + $0.30 per ticket; the official page lists only 2.9% per order — [whos-in.app](https://whos-in.app/blog/eventbrite-fees-explained-2026) vs. the Eventbrite page. Eventbrite was reported bought by Bending Spoons with the deal closing in March 2026; Flex plan retired — [TickPick summary via search](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/eventbrite-2026-fee-changes), [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/eventbrite-fees)
- Worked $25 ticket (my arithmetic): $0.925 + $1.79 + $0.725 = $3.44, about 13.8%; a source states $3.45 — [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/eventbrite-fees). On a $10 ticket: $0.37 + $1.79 + $0.29 = $2.45, 24.5%.

**Squarespace, Wix, Behance (portfolios)**
- Wix: reported 0% platform transaction fees on paid plans; Free, Light $17, Core $29, Business $39 per month. Squarespace: newer naming Basic $16, Core $23, Plus $39, Advanced $99 per month annually; a 2% transaction fee on Basic (removed on Core) and digital-content/membership fees 7%/5%/1%/0% by tier; older naming sources report 3% on Business — [Hostadvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/website-builders/wix/wix-vs-squarespace-pricing/), [MakingThatWebsite](https://www.makingthatwebsite.com/squarespace-vs-wix/), [Zanfia](https://zanfia.com/blog/squarespace-pricing-vs-wix/). Plan names and prices conflict across sources; I could not load Squarespace's or Wix's own pages.

### Inferences
- The fixed 30¢ and per-ticket dollar fees do more damage than the percentage at small ticket sizes: Eventbrite's roughly $1.79 plus 30¢-equivalent costs make a $10 ticket about 25% fees.
- Flat-subscription services (Ghost, beehiiv, Buttondown, Big Cartel, Micro.blog) are the at-cost pattern that already exists; their remaining cost to the creator is processing, which no one can remove below Stripe's rate except via nonprofit status (which excludes memberships and tickets, see Q4).
- Platform margin above processing: Etsy about 6.7 points on a clean sale plus ads; Gumroad 7.1 (direct, if processing separate) to 30; Patreon, Substack and Buy Me a Coffee 5-10; Eventbrite about 3.7% + $1.79.

### Gaps
- No Etsy 2025 policy-change or seller-backlash coverage found (searches returned the 2022 strike only). Etsy's own fee page was 403.
- No official Substack, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee or Patreon processing/micropayment rate page loaded; Patreon's under-$3 micropayment rate not found.
- Behance fees not checked. Squarespace and Wix primary pages not loaded.
- Etsy Offsite Ads share of orders (how often sellers actually pay it) not found.
- Gumroad: whether processing is on top of 10% is unresolved; check Gumroad's help center.

## 2. How many people this covers

### Takeaway
Etsy alone has about 5.6 million active marketplace sellers (8.76 million across Etsy, Depop, Reverb in the consolidated count). Most part-time creators earn very little: Linktree's 2021-22 survey put 68% of part-timers and 46% of full-timers under $1,000 a year. About one in five US adults did some gig activity in 2024, 13% by selling things.

### Cited Findings
- Etsy FY2025 10-K: consolidated GMS $11,916.9M (down 5.3%), active sellers 8,762 thousand (up 7.7%), active buyers 93,539 thousand; Etsy marketplace alone: 5.6M active sellers (down 1.5%), 86.5M active buyers, GMS about $10.46B; an active seller has a charge or sale in the last 12 months; 74% of GMS from US buyers; Reverb excluded in 2025 so year-on-year is not like-for-like — [Etsy 10-K on SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm) (figures read via a search summary, not opened in full)
- Linktree creator report: 46% of full-time creators earn under $1,000 a year and 12% over $50,000; part-time creators: 68% under $1,000, 3% over $50,000; about 9,500 respondents (survey fielded 2021; self-selected Linktree users) — [TechCrunch, Apr 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/linktree-creator-economy-report-research/) (flag: older than 2022 fieldwork)
- Goldman Sachs: about 4% of roughly 50 million creators are "professional" earning over $100,000; brand deals about 70% of income — reported via search summary of [Bloggingwizard](https://bloggingwizard.com/creator-economy-statistics/), not checked at the primary (older, probably 2023)
- Fed SHED (fielded Oct 2024): 20% of adults did gig activity, 13% made money selling things, 9% short-term tasks; 28% of gig-doers said they were not working for pay — [Federal Reserve 2024 gig chapter](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm). The 2025 report (May 2026) reportedly has no gig questions — [Fed 2025 report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf) (stated by secondary sources; I did not confirm in the Fed's text)

### Inferences
- The target population is large (millions of Etsy sellers alone) but low-earning, so a lump-sum $15-30/month tool is a heavy cost for most, which supports per-sale or usage-based at-cost pricing over flat plans for the long tail.
- Etsy's seller count is not an addressable count for a self-hosted team: many are on a single listing.

### Gaps
- No Etsy distribution of seller earnings found. No Patreon/Substack/Ko-fi active-creator counts found. No 2025-2026 side-hustle survey (Bankrate, Fed) with earnings figures loaded.

## 3. At-cost and co-op precedents, and what limited them

### Takeaway
Co-ops that tried to be a marketplace or a destination (Ampled, Resonate) closed; those that sell a tool (Ghost, Buttondown, Micro.blog) with a plain subscription persist. Reasons named: burnout and lack of staff, payments problems, and the need for audience.

### Cited Findings
- Ampled: founded 2018, artist-owned, over 800 artists; announced Oct 25, 2023 it would wind down by end of 2023; board cited burnout and lack of resources to hire full-time workers, and said it had exhausted all practical options; artists reportedly left for Bandcamp citing card-payment issues — [Billboard Canada](https://ca.billboard.com/ampled-music-platform)
- PCC: Scholz acknowledges Ampled's closure; Scholz and Mannan wrote a "Post-Mortem for Ampled"; co-founder Austin Robey names resource scarcity, market viability and public perception; PCC event "Lessons from Failed Platform Co-ops" May 9, 2024 — [PCC: Lessons from Failed Platform Co-ops](https://platform.coop/blog/lessons-from-failed-platform-co-ops/); I did not open the post-mortem itself
- Resonate: stream-to-own co-op, 45% artists / 35% listeners / 20% employees; operated 2017-2024, officially closed as of 2026; its site does not say why — [Resonate](https://resonate.coop/); 2022 fundraising appeal for EUR 20K to pay staff and developers — [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/resonate); an artist reports a payment rejected by a code bug and a quiet forum — [Alex Rodriguez](https://alexwrodriguez.com/inside-a-music-platform-cooperative-with-ampleds-last-artist-owner/) (single anecdote)
- Open Collective Foundation (fiscal host) dissolved Dec 31, 2024 and over 600 collectives lost their host; reportedly because its 5% host fee did not cover the cost of serving many collectives; Open Source Collective charges 10% — [Open Collective statement](https://blog.opencollective.com/open-collective-official-statement-ocf-dissolution/), [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39533004), [Goodkeep](https://goodkeep.org/blog/open-collective-foundation-alternatives) (the 5% shortfall explanation is "reportedly" in secondary sources)
- Liberapay: listed at 0% platform fee, funded by donations, with processor fees on top; processing details not confirmed; no fiscal-host or expense features — [Schoolmaker](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/open-source-alternatives-to-patreon)
- Ghost: operated by a nonprofit (Ghost Foundation); 0% on paid subscriptions, subscription pricing for hosting; code is open source on GitHub — [Ghost pricing](https://ghost.org/pricing/) (the nonprofit statement is only a badge and footer on that page; Ghost's about page not read)
- Stocksy co-op paid $200,000 in dividends in 2015 and operates in over 80 countries — [PCC/Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_cooperative) (flag: 2015 figure)

### Inferences
- The failures cluster on running a destination with staff, not on infrastructure cost: "burnout, no one to hire," payments, and audience.
- Fiscal hosting at a flat 5% was too thin to carry compliance (Open Collective Foundation), a sign that at-cost pricing must fund the real administrative load.

### Gaps
- Not covered: Buttondown's and Micro.blog's own histories and sizes, Big Cartel's ownership, Resonate's actual cause of closure, Ghost Foundation finances and size, discovery/audience data for any co-op. Platform-co-op movement detail came from search summaries, not opened pages.

## 4. What is unavoidable vs. platform margin

### Takeaway
Card processing (2.9% + 30¢), disputes ($15 per), and sales tax are costs no operator removes; email is cheap per message; marketplace sales-tax duty is what makes running a marketplace heavy. Reporting rules (1099-K) fell back to $20,000 / 200 transactions.

### Cited Findings
- Processing, dispute fees: Stripe numbers above — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing). A $15 dispute on a $25 order is 60% of the sale price (my arithmetic).
- Nonprofit discount: 2.2% + 30¢ for verified US nonprofits; requires over 80% of volume to be tax-deductible donations; memberships, tickets and registrations do not qualify; not retroactive — [Stripe support: nonprofit discount](https://support.stripe.com/questions/fee-discount-for-nonprofit-organizations) (read through a search summary; the page was not opened)
- Micropayments: I found no Stripe-published rate. A single competitor-site claim of 5% + 5¢ is internally contradictory; treat as unverified — [WPForms](https://wpforms.com/stripe-vs-paypal-which-one-is-better/); the Radar 5¢ fee is waived on standard pricing — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees)
- Sales tax: 45 states and DC have marketplace-facilitator laws; most use a $100,000 threshold, but Texas and California are $500,000; the five no-sales-tax states are Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon; sellers are relieved of collection through a facilitator in all states but Hawaii; a facilitator's duty follows its own nexus; some states require notice to sellers (Texas, Connecticut, Wisconsin); the tasks are registering by state, tracking thresholds, and rating by product and local jurisdiction — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/blog/marketplace-facilitator-laws-by-state), [Stripe guide](https://stripe.com/resources/more/marketplace-facilitator-laws). No dollar cost of compliance was found.
- Gumroad's merchant-of-record move (Jan 2025) shows the work a platform absorbs — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- 1099-K: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act restored the $20,000 and over 200 transactions threshold for payment apps and marketplaces (third-party settlement organizations), retroactively replacing the $600 ARPA rule; card transactions have no minimum; states may be lower; income stays taxable below the threshold; Fact Sheet 2025-08 on Oct 23, 2025 (IR-2025-107) — [IRS Form 1099-K FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/form-1099-k-faqs), [IRS FS-2025-08](https://www.irs.gov/pub/taxpros/fs-2025-08.pdf) (IRS content read through search summary)
- Email sending: AWS SES outbound is $0.10 per 1,000 emails à la carte; Essentials $0.16 per 1,000; dedicated IP $24.95 per IP per month — [AWS SES pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)
- Podcast hosting prices: Spotify for Creators free, Buzzsprout about $18/mo ($15 yearly) with 72 upload hours a year, Transistor from $19/mo at 20,000 downloads, Libsyn about $8-12/mo; recent sources newer than older; one warns Spotify means you do not own your feed — [Transistor guide](https://transistor.fm/best-podcast-hosting-platform/), [Ryrob](https://www.ryrob.com/best-podcast-hosting/), [Mediamorpheus](https://mediamorpheuspress.com/blog/best-alternatives-to-buzzsprout) (secondary and conflicting)

### Inferences
- Email: a 10,000-subscriber weekly newsletter is 40,000 sends a month, about $4 at SES à la carte (my arithmetic); delivery reputation and bounce handling, not raw price, is the cost a self-hoster carries.
- A storefront or membership page run per maker in that maker's own Stripe account puts the tax (as seller), disputes and 1099-K on the maker, and spares the operator the marketplace-facilitator duty. I did not find a source that says this directly; it follows from the facilitator rules above, and a tax advisor should confirm.
- Nonprofit rates do not help a membership or ticketing service, since memberships and tickets are excluded.

### Gaps
- Not found: audio storage and bandwidth cost (no sourced figures; Micro.blog's 75 MB per file is the only datum); chargeback rates or fraud loss rates for small sellers; Stripe micropayment pricing; whether a facilitator duty attaches to a software host that does not take custody of funds.

## 5. What a small team could own, and discovery versus owning the audience

### Takeaway
The records point to per-maker, self-hosted tools (storefront in the maker's own Stripe account, newsletter, membership page, podcast host) as the ones with a working at-cost precedent; a marketplace is the part with the worst record (Ampled, Resonate) and the heaviest tax duty. This is judgment for the owner, not a ruling.

### Cited Findings
- Existing flat-price, zero-commission tools show the model works commercially: Ghost, beehiiv Lite/Pro, Big Cartel, Micro.blog — see Q1 citations.
- Discovery is a cost platforms charge for: Gumroad Discover 30% versus 10% + $0.50 direct — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing); Etsy Offsite Ads 12-15% — [FeeProofed](https://www.feeproofed.com/guides/etsy-fee-increase-history-2018-2026/)
- Etsy repeat-buyer data: habitual buyers were about 7% of active buyers and about 40% of 2025 GMS, so audience concentration matters — [Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- Apple's fee on Patreon iOS shows lock-in risk from outside the platform — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/apple-tells-patreon-to-move-creators-to-in-app-purchase-for-subscriptions-by-november/)

### Inferences
- Candidate ranking (my judgment, for the owner to decide): (1) newsletter and membership page, which have the most existing at-cost competitors and the simplest tax story but the slimmest margin over Ghost at $29; (2) a per-maker storefront for digital downloads or handmade goods in the maker's own Stripe account, which beats Etsy's roughly 11% only for makers who bring their own traffic; (3) podcast hosting, whose bandwidth cost is unsourced here; (4) ticketing, where the fixed per-ticket fee is the largest saving ($1.79 per ticket) but chargebacks and event-cancellation risk are unresearched.
- A maker who loses Etsy's search traffic may lose more than 11% in revenue; owning the audience only pays for makers who already have one.
- Fork for the owner: offer a self-hosted tool per maker (at-cost hosting) versus operate a shared multi-tenant service for them. The second touches tax, disputes and 1099-K treatment and needs legal advice before any step.

### Gaps
- No data on conversion from marketplace to own-storefront or on Etsy sellers' share of traffic that is off-platform. Cost of hosting per maker not estimated. Behance and Beehiiv/Ghost self-host economics not compared.
