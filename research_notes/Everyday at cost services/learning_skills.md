# Learning and skill-building services where cost gates access (US, late 2026)

Scope: enrichment and skills, not school accountability or special-education law. Source quality is flagged inline. Most price figures come from vendor and guide blogs, not surveys. Figures older than 2022 are marked **[pre-2022]**. Written 2026-11-15.

## 1. Where cost gates access, and by how much

### Takeaway
Paid private help is priced at roughly $25-$100 per hour for tutoring and $60-$100 per hour for music, and paid tutoring is concentrated in top-income households. Cost is not the only gate. Capacity gates adult ESL (waitlists of 1,000+ in one Maine metro), and a free, low-friction option often goes unused by the students who need it most.

### Cited Findings

**Private tutoring: price**
- Typical US range is $25-$80 per hour. K-12 $25-$50, high school $30-$60, college $40-$80, SAT/ACT $50-$100+, specialists $75-$150+. This is a vendor blog, not a survey. — [Wise](https://www.wise.live/blog/average-tutoring-rate-in-the-usa/)
- Online tutoring is generally cheaper than in-person. One guide puts it at $20-$50 versus $45-$90 in person; sources conflict on size of the gap. — [MyEdSpace](https://myedspace.com/us/blog/private-tutoring-cost) (search-result summary; not opened)
- Cost is far above free peer options. UPchieve says in-person high-dosage tutoring typically costs $3,000+ per student per year. — [UPchieve mission](https://upchieve.org/mission). A secondary source puts Saga Education's high-dosage model at about $3,500-$4,300 per student per year. — [Hiwavemakers](https://hiwavemakers.com/blog/20000-elementary-school-tutoring-what-it-buys-parents/) (low-quality blog; treat with caution).

**Private tutoring: who uses it (the income gap)**
- Consumer Expenditure Survey data for 2009-2019 (**[pre-2022]**): 1.5% of households with school-age children reported tutoring spending in a typical month. 4.6% of top-decile households did, 1.4% of the next 40%, and 0.5% of the bottom half. Top-decile households were 46% of all families reporting tutoring spending. — [EdTech Innovation Hub on a tutoring-chains study](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/private-tutoring-chains-raise-us-test-scores-but-widen-racial-gaps-study-findspei63t6nechguwu48bmhybfykd9vyj) (secondary summary; original paper not opened)
- In 2022 about 6-7% of US families with children 6-17 paid for tutoring in the past year, at an average of about $437 in months they bought it. — [EdWorkingPapers, "The Recent, Rapid Rise of Private Tutoring Centers"](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai21-367_0.pdf) (figure via search summary)
- Counter-finding: a 2026 study combining 10 government surveys found that once children are in school, the lowest-income families receive 72% more tutoring than the highest-income families, and about 17% of school-age children were tutored (2014-2021 data). This almost certainly includes school-provided and publicly funded tutoring, not just paid. — [Fortune/The Conversation, Blazar, 2026-11-04](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/richest-families-outspend-poorest-13-to-1-per-child-schools-equalizer/)
- The same article cites separate research that by 2024 the richest 10% spent more than 13 times as much per child as the poorest quarter on childcare, education and activities combined, up from about 3 to 1 in the early 1980s. It gives no music-specific figure. — [same Fortune article](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/richest-families-outspend-poorest-13-to-1-per-child-schools-equalizer/)

**Music lessons**
- 30-minute lesson: $30-$60 across guides; 60-minute: $50-$100 (some guides $40-$90). Major cities $80-$150 per hour; online $35-$70 per hour. — [Ensemble Schools](https://www.ensembleschools.com/blog/cost-of-private-music-lessons/), [Lessons.com](https://lessons.com/costs/music-lessons-cost), [TakeSessions](https://takesessions.com/tools/lesson-cost-calculator/) (studio and guide pricing, not a survey)
- Weekly 30-minute lessons come to about $170 per month (range $140-$200); weekly 60-minute about $320 per month. — [Ensemble Schools](https://www.ensembleschools.com/blog/cost-of-private-music-lessons/)
- Community music schools subsidize heavily. Community Music School of Springfield (MA): about 70% of its 2,200 annual students get music education free or reduced. — [CMS Springfield](https://www.communitymusicschool.com/impact/). Concord Community Music School: over 15% of budget (nearly $200,000 per year) goes to aid, average onsite scholarship about $1,000. — [Concord CMS](https://www.ccmusicschool.org/supporting-scholarships/)
- Aid is rationed. University of Delaware's community music school gives a 95% discount to households at or below 150% of poverty, limited to the first ten registrants per year. — [UD Community Music School](https://www.udel.edu/academics/colleges/cas/units/departments/school-of-music/community-outreach/community-music-school/)

**Test prep and the GED / trade path**
- SAT $68 and ACT $65 ($90 with Writing; one source lists base ACT at $70). Fee waivers bring SAT to $0 and allow two free ACTs for eligible students. — [Piqosity](https://www.piqosity.com/act-cost-sat-cost-fee-waivers/), [Texas Education Agency](https://tea.texas.gov/academics/college-career-and-military-prep/sat)
- GED: most states charge $30-$40 per subject, about $120-$160 for all four. GED Ready practice test is $7.99 per subject. Some states are free or near-free (New York at a test center). State prices conflict across sources; verify at ged.com. — [Tutoriffic](https://tutoriffic.com/blog/ged-test-cost), [mocktestprep](https://mocktestprep.com/ged/cost/) (guide blogs)
- Trade school: average electrician program tuition $16,021 (2024-25) and about $1,348 books; average welding program $10,695 and 9.7 months. Public community-college certificates are about $3,500-$10,000, while private programs run $10,000-$22,000+ (Tulsa Welding School welding program $21,525). — [CollegeTuitionCompare electrician](https://www.collegetuitioncompare.com/compare/tables/vocational-program/electrician/), [Edvisors](https://www.edvisors.com/plan-for-college/careers/how-much-does-trade-school-cost/), [Trade Schools Review](https://tradeschoolsreview.com/guides/welding-school-cost) (median $5,084 across 474 programs, range $600-$41,250)

**Coding bootcamps: price, outcomes, collapse**
- Average full-time US bootcamp tuition is cited at $14,142 (2025), with other figures at a $13,000 median (Forbes Advisor, July 2025) and $11,874, which traces to a 2017 report **[pre-2022]**. — [Course Report](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/are-coding-bootcamps-worth-it-in-2025), [Wikipedia on bootcamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coding_bootcamp) (search-summary figures; vendor blogs repeat them)
- Outcomes: Course Report's alumni survey reports 79% full-time employment. CIRR-audited programs report about 71% employed in-field within 180 days. Codesmith's audited report for 865 remote 2023 graduates showed 70.1% employed in-field within 360 days. — [Course Report](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/are-coding-bootcamps-worth-it-in-2025), [Metana stats](https://metana.io/blog/coding-bootcamp-statistics-for-2026/) (secondary, marketing-adjacent)
- Closures 2024: Code Fellows, Rithm School, Momentum; Chegg Skills (ex-Thinkful) sunset its online bootcamps for individual career changers; edX shut its university boot camps. — [Course Report 2024 review](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/2024-year-in-review-coding-bootcamp-news) (via search summary; page not opened)
- Closures 2025: non-profit Turing School (Denver) announced closing in June 2025; Lighthouse Labs was acquired by Uvaro in January and Uvaro/Lighthouse Labs had closed by end of 2025. — [Course Report 2025 review](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/2025-year-in-review-coding-bootcamp-news) (fetched)
- That review gives no count of closures and no tuition figure. A "12 major closures 2024-2026" count on Prentus is unverified. — [Prentus](https://prentus.com/blog/future-of-bootcamps)
- Enrollment signal: 2U's alternative credential segment revenue fell 23.3%, largely from a 40% drop in bootcamp enrollment (secondary source, not the 2U filing). — [Jobalert summary](https://jobalert.onl/bootcamp-industry-shakeout-what-closures-and-layoffs-mean-for-students-in-2025/)
- Counterpoint: certificate-program enrollment (all fields, not bootcamps) was 28.5% above 2019 levels. — [Course Report 2025 review](https://www.coursereport.com/blog/2025-year-in-review-coding-bootcamp-news)

**Adult language learning (ESL)**
- ProLiteracy: 51% of member programs keep a waiting list because demand exceeds capacity (2023 fact sheet); less than 10% of adults in need receive services. — [ProLiteracy 2023 fact sheet](https://www.proliteracy.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2023-PL-AdultBasicEducation-FactSheet-2467.pdf) (via search summary; PDF not opened)
- Portland, Maine (Nov 2024): about 1,300 enrolled in English courses and at least 1,000 more on waitlists, where the director said that was only where they stopped counting. Remote classes did not clear it. — [Press Herald](https://www.pressherald.com/2024/11/22/demand-for-english-adult-education-courses-outpaces-class-space-in-portland-region/)
- California library ESL waitlists grew from 255 to an estimated 840 adults in FY2024-25. ESL learners are about 43% of adults enrolled in library literacy programs. — [California State Library](https://www.library.ca.gov/services/to-libraries/clls/esl/)
- Older: a 2006 study of 176 ESL providers found 57% had waitlists ranging from weeks to more than 3 years **[pre-2022]**. — [ERIC "ESL Logjam"](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED527905.pdf)

### Inferences
- Paid tutoring skews high-income, but "tutoring" as a whole does not, because school and public programs fill part of the gap. A new free service competes mainly with those programs for low-income families.
- Capacity, not price, gates ESL. A tool that adds human conversation hours (volunteer conversation partners) addresses the actual bottleneck; a content app does not.
- Community music schools already run a subsidy model of about 15%-70% aided students. Software could help them with waitlists, aid applications and instrument loans rather than compete with them.
- Bootcamps are a poor product to offer at cost: the market is contracting and audited outcomes sit near 70%.

### Gaps
- No national, current, peer-reviewed share of US families paying for music lessons by income. Not found.
- No national count of adult ESL waitlists for WIOA Title II. Only local and state figures, plus ProLiteracy's member-program share.
- Did not open the original paper behind the Consumer Expenditure tutoring-chain figures, the 2U filing, or Course Report's 2024 review.
- Trade and hobby skills beyond welding/electrician (cooking, sewing, woodworking, hobbyist classes) were not researched.
- Test-prep course pricing (Kaplan, Princeton Review) not researched.

## 2. Free options and where they fall short

### Takeaway
Free content is plentiful; free human feedback and sustained completion are not. Drop-in tutoring is used by 1-5% of eligible students, and MOOC-style completion is low. Duolingo, the largest free option, has tightened its free tier.

### Cited Findings

**Khan Academy / Khanmigo**
- Khan Academy core is free with no ads. Khanmigo for Teachers is free to all US teachers, backed by Microsoft infrastructure. Learner/parent Khanmigo is listed at $4 per month or $44 per year; the price comes from reviews, not Khan's page, and may change. — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khanmigo-for-teachers-is-free-for-all-us-teachers-thanks-to-support-from-microsoft/), [KidEdTools review](https://kidedtools.com/blog/khanmigo-khan-academy-ai-tutor-review-2026/)
- Two-year cluster RCT in 18 Tennessee middle schools (Oreopoulos and Low, EdWorkingPaper 26-1551, Aug 2026): Khan Academy plus Khanmigo raised math achievement 1.3 national percentile ranks per term (0.06-0.08 SD per school year), similar to Khan Academy practice without AI. 96% tried Khanmigo, but the median student messaged it on only about a third of practice days, and messaged in only 17% of sessions where they made a mistake. Most messages were bare answers or suggested-prompt clicks. The authors name engagement as the binding constraint. — [EdWorkingPapers 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551)

**Duolingo**
- Free users got hearts (lost only on mistakes); from July-Sept 2025 an "energy" system rolled out in which free users start with 25 energy and it drains on every exercise. Free users typically get 2-3 lessons before running out; they then spend gems, watch ads, or subscribe. Widely criticized as a paywall. Web still used hearts at last report. — [duoplanet](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-energy-system/), [TechIssuesToday](https://techissuestoday.com/duolingo-energy-system-user-backlash/) (blogs)
- Prices: Super $12.99 per month, Super Family $119.99 per year (up to six), Max $29.99 per month. — [MyEngineeringBuddy](https://www.myengineeringbuddy.com/blog/duolingo-reviews-pricing-alternatives-2026/) (review blog; prices vary by country)
- Q3 2025: 11.5M paid subscribers, 50.5M DAUs, 135.3M MAUs, paid penetration 9.0% of LTM MAUs. — [Duolingo 8-K, Q3 FY25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828025049514/q3fy25duolingo9-30x25share.htm) (via search summary). A 2026 source says Explain My Answer became free to all in Jan 2026 and Super Lite is being tested; unverified. — [Mindomax / hellonabu](https://www.hellonabu.com/blog/en/is-duolingo-free/)

**freeCodeCamp and The Odin Project**
- freeCodeCamp is a donor-supported 501(c)(3); one source says it has issued over 1 million free certifications, attributed to its 2024 annual report. Secondhand; report not opened. — [freecodingcourses comparison](https://freecodingcourses.com/guides/freecodecamp-vs-the-odin-project)
- Odin issues no certificates and its Full Stack JavaScript path takes 9-18 months part-time; no completion rate found. — [freecodingcourses comparison](https://freecodingcourses.com/compare/freecodecamp/the-odin-project)
- Completion benchmark across all MOOCs: median 12.6% across 129 MOOCs with data, range 0.7%-52.1% (Jordan 2015, **[pre-2022]**); earlier median 6.5% across 39 MOOCs (Jordan 2014, **[pre-2022]**); Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente (2019, *Science*) found the low rate "has not budged in six years." — [Open Praxis 2024, "Uncovering MOOC Completion"](https://openpraxis.org/articles/10.55982/openpraxis.16.3.606)

**Library programs**
- Mango Languages, LinkedIn Learning and Brainfuse HelpNow are offered free with a library card at large systems (examples: LAPL, Chicago Public Library, LA County Library). Brainfuse has a job-seeker version with resume feedback in 24 hours. — [LAPL Mango](https://www.lapl.org/digital-library/mango-languages), [LAPL LinkedIn Learning](https://www.lapl.org/digital-library/linkedin), [Chicago Public Library learning](https://www.chipublib.org/resources-types/learning/)
- I found no usage data (sessions, users) for these vendors; a claim that over 60% of public libraries offer an online learning platform was unverifiable. — [libraryhours24](https://libraryhours24.com/free-online-courses-library) (low-quality)

**Peer and volunteer tutoring: Schoolhouse.world, UPchieve**
- Schoolhouse.world About page: over 174,000 students in 180+ countries and 460,000+ tutoring sessions, run by student volunteers (page may be dated). A third-party review says about 18,000 volunteer tutors as of 2024 (unofficial). — [Schoolhouse about](https://schoolhouse.world/about), [MyEngineeringBuddy](https://www.myengineeringbuddy.com/blog/schoolhouse-reviews-alternatives-pricing-offerings/)
- UPchieve: foundations cover about $5 per student per year for unlimited on-demand help (earlier $10 in 2021 **[pre-2022 era launch figure, 2021]**). Over 37,000 students and 100,000+ sessions reported by 2023, with about 20,000 volunteer tutors. — [UPchieve mission](https://upchieve.org/mission), [Launch HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=27118794), [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/how-a-free-24-7-tutoring-model-is-disrupting-learning-loss-for-low-income-kids/)
- Usage is thin: some school systems report under 2% usage of online drop-in tutoring (Fairfax County 1.6% using Tutor.com). At UPchieve partner schools 5% logged in at least once in 2022-23 (goal 36%); some schools under 1%. Students needing the most help were least likely to try it (Brown study, ~7,000 students). — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/62572/schools-keep-buying-online-drop-in-tutoring-research-doesnt-support-it)
- UPchieve's promoted 9-percentile-point math gain came from a Mathematica study of 89 self-selected students that really tested a $5-per-week cash incentive; the gain was not statistically significant. — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/62572/schools-keep-buying-online-drop-in-tutoring-research-doesnt-support-it)
- Pittsburgh paid $600,000 for unlimited tutoring for 19,000 students; at Fairfax's usage it would serve at most about 300. — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/62572/schools-keep-buying-online-drop-in-tutoring-research-doesnt-support-it)

**Evidence on tutoring that does work (human, structured)**
- Meta-analysis pooled effect 0.37 SD across tutoring RCTs (Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan, 2020 **[pre-2022]**); teacher and paraprofessional tutors beat nonprofessional and parent tutors; in-school beat after-school. One blog says the published version gives 0.29 SD; unverified. — [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27476/w27476.pdf), [J-PAL review](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/publication/Evidence-Review_The-Transformative-Potential-of-Tutoring.pdf)

**Volunteer matching and retention**
- Mentoring literature (not tutoring-specific): matches ending early average 7.4 months versus 25.1 months across all matches; under 11 months counts as premature (Lyons 2022). — [Am J Community Psychol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajcp.12585)
- School-based matches average just over 5 months in one cited study; the first three months need support. — [Education Northwest fact sheet](https://educationnorthwest.org/sites/default/files/resources/factsheet18.pdf) (age not checked)
- An OJJDP-funded study found 64% of match closures were attributed to the mentor, and re-matching may not make up for an early-ended first match. — [OJJDP](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/ojjdp/grants/251463.pdf) (preliminary evidence)
- Mentor-parent relationship was the most consistent predictor of match longevity in a 2026 study of 120 former Big Brothers Big Sisters mentors. — [Evidence-Based Mentoring summary](https://www.evidencebasedmentoring.org/findings-reveal-that-mentors-who-connect-with-parents-stay-longer-and-that-programs-are-missing-this/)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern: access is solved, uptake is not. Software value lies in nudges, scheduling and a human relationship, not in more content.
- Opt-in drop-in help serves self-motivated students; scheduled, recurring, assigned sessions are what the evidence supports.
- A volunteer-matching tool should treat the first three months and the first 11 months as the retention targets, and track match-ending reasons.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed dropout figures for volunteer tutoring specifically; only mentoring analogues.
- No completion rate for The Odin Project or freeCodeCamp; no usage data for Brainfuse, LinkedIn Learning, Mango.
- Khan Academy annual learner numbers not found. Did not open UPchieve's 2024/2025 report (the 2023 PDF text is garbled).
- Did not research other free options in the brief such as Coursera financial aid or YouTube-based learning.

## 3. Which pieces are software-shaped

### Takeaway
The evidence points toward scheduling, matching and retention tooling around humans, plus tools for organizations that already hold the scarce resource (ESL classes, instruments, teachers). Time banks show the model works but reaches small membership.

### Cited Findings
- hOurworld counter: 27,541 members, 303 communities, 4,180,630 hours of service received. The same site's Time and Talents page also claims 400+ timebanks and 35,000+ members in 39 countries, which conflicts with the live counter. — [hOurworld](https://www.hourworld.org/index.htm), [hOurworld Time and Talents](https://www.hourworld.org/_TimeAndTalents.htm)
- Older hOurworld figures: 44,000+ members in 547 time banks and 2.3 million hours (2019). — [Salon 2019](https://www.salon.com/2019/04/06/time-really-is-money-with-increasingly-popular-cooperative-time-banks_partner/) **[pre-2022]**. So the live membership counter is lower than 2019, a possible decline though counting methods may differ.
- TimeBanks USA says over 3.2 million service hours received. — [TimeBanks USA](https://www.timebanks.org/)
- About 500 US time banks, 37,000+ members, smallest 15 and largest about 3,200; at least 50% of members do not record hours regularly. — [SSIR, Cahn and Grey](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_time_bank_solution) **[pre-2022, date not checked]**
- Individual examples: Kent, Ohio time bank 530+ active members and 101,000+ hours in 15 years; Lehigh Valley Hospital time bank 500 members. — via search summary of [Salon 2019](https://www.salon.com/2019/04/06/time-really-is-money-with-increasingly-popular-cooperative-time-banks_partner/) and related pages
- hOurworld offers free time-banking software (Time and Talents), so a new time-bank platform competes with a free incumbent. — [hOurworld Time and Talents](https://www.hourworld.org/_TimeAndTalents.htm)
- Tutor CoPilot (AI supporting human tutors) RCT: 900 tutors, 1,800 K-12 students; students were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics, 9 points for lower-rated tutors; total API cost for 429 treatment tutors over two months was $1,419.66, about $20 per tutor per year. Sources differ on sample (one summary: 782 tutors, 1,787 students). — [Stanford NSSA PDF](https://nssa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/Tutor_CoPilot.pdf), [arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017)
- Mathematica's UPchieve trial found cash incentives and email reminders raised sessions (about 9 versus under 4; over 10 sessions: 22% versus 14%). — [KQED MindShift](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/62572/schools-keep-buying-online-drop-in-tutoring-research-doesnt-support-it)
- Community music school lending: I found only a sheet-music library at one school; no instrument-lending program data. — [Community Music School, Trappe PA via search](https://www.communitymusicschool.com/about-us/) (weak)

### Inferences
These are design judgments, not sourced facts:
- Candidate pieces: (a) volunteer/learner scheduler with recurring slots, reminders and match-quality check-ins; (b) conversation-partner matching for ESL waitlists (adds the missing capacity); (c) instrument and equipment lending ledger for schools and community music programs; (d) study-group organizer; (e) a time-bank ledger.
- Time banks have modest scale (hundreds to low thousands per bank) and a free incumbent, so a differentiated offering would be integration with tutoring/lending, not another ledger.
- A cheap, high-leverage AI use is assisting human volunteers (Tutor CoPilot model, about $20 per tutor per year) rather than replacing them.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on instrument-lending programs (cost, loss rates), study-group organizer tools, or ESL conversation-partner matching outcomes.
- No data on how time banks perform for skill exchange (as opposed to errands/elder support).
- Did not examine open-source tutor-matching software that could be reused.

## 4. AI tutoring evidence, 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Structured, guardrailed AI tutors help, and unguarded chatbots can hurt unaided performance. Gains in at-scale K-12 are small and limited by student engagement. The strongest results are in controlled or supervised settings.

### Cited Findings
- Harvard physics RCT (Kestin et al., *Scientific Reports* 15, 17458, June 2025): randomized crossover with 194 students in Physical Sciences 2 in autumn 2023; GPT-4-based tutor told not to give answers; median learning gains more than double those in in-class active learning; median AI learner took about 49 minutes versus roughly 60 for class. Caveats: two short lessons, Harvard students, mid-Bloom material. — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6), open copy [PMC12179260](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)
- World Bank Nigeria ("From Chalkboards to Chatbots", De Simone et al., Policy Research Working Paper 11125): six-week after-school program, twelve 90-minute sessions with Microsoft Copilot (GPT-4), teacher-guided, in Benin City public schools. English gain about 0.23-0.24 SD (ITT); overall assessment 0.31 SD; school English exam 0.206 SD. About $48 per pupil per secondary summaries. Caveats: the control group had no alternative program, so extra structured study time is confounded with the AI; persistence unknown; paper test. One secondary source says one school, conflicting with nine; I could not open the World Bank page. — [Fermi News summary](https://news.fermi.ai/learning-ai/nigeria-ai-tutoring-pilot-study), [AskPoppy summary](https://research.askpoppy.ai/papers/de-simone-2025-chalkboards-to-chatbots-nigeria/), [World Bank record](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099548105192529324) (page returned no abstract)
- Bastani et al., *PNAS* 122(26): Turkish high school math, GPT-4 access during practice raised practice performance 48% (GPT Base) and 127% (GPT Tutor); on later unassisted exams GPT Base students scored 17% worse than control, GPT Tutor about the same as control. GPT-4 answered the practice problems correctly only about half the time (steps wrong 42%). Students did not believe it harmed them. — [Wharton summary](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/), [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/kids-chatgpt-worse-on-tests/)
- Khanmigo two-year Tennessee RCT (see section 2): 0.06-0.08 SD per year, same as Khan practice without AI; engagement is the limit. — [EdWorkingPapers 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1551)
- Tutor CoPilot: +4 points mastery; a review notes no significant end-of-year test improvement (secondary). — [Stanford EduNLP](https://edunlp.stanford.edu/projects/tutor-copilot), [arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017)
- A claim that a course-integrated AI tutor "reduced final grades by 0.37 SD" in a 2,379-student US university trial appears only in an aggregator; I did not verify it. — [You.com roundup](https://for.you.com/posts/ai-tutoring-in-2026-what-the-research-really-shows-b6282a98) (unverified; do not cite without finding the paper)

### Inferences
- Common success conditions: tutor told not to give answers, teacher or scheduled structure around it, and a defined time block. Failure condition: free-form chatbot used to get answers.
- Treat the Nigeria effect as "structured study time with an AI" not "AI alone."
- Harvard's result does not transfer cleanly to a free service for general learners; the Tennessee study shows real-world K-12 uptake is weak even when access is free.

### Gaps
- Did not read the Harvard or World Bank full text myself; details are from the Scientific Reports abstract-level summaries and secondary summaries.
- No evidence found on adults, language, music or coding learners with AI tutors specifically.
- Longer-term retention and equity effects (who benefits) not established.

## 5. Running costs and risks

### Takeaway
Inference for text tutoring is cheap on small models, and the real costs are human: volunteer screening, moderation and, if under-13s are involved, COPPA compliance. The least risky design avoids minors or runs through a school/org.

### Cited Findings
**AI inference cost**
- Anthropic's pricing page (fetched 2026-11-15): Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens (prompts up to 100,000 tokens); Sonnet 5.5 $2 input / $10 output; Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5. Cache reads are $0.01 per million for Haiku 5.5 and the Batch API gives 50% off. — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)

World Bank Nigeria about $48 per pupil for the whole program (secondary). — [Fermi News](https://news.fermi.ai/learning-ai/nigeria-ai-tutoring-pilot-study)
- Alternative: self-hosted open models; one comparison page exists. — [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/claude-haiku-4-5-api-pricing-vs-self-hosted-llms-cost-2026/) (not opened; vendor)

**Child safety and law**
- COPPA amended rule: finalized Jan 16, 2025; published April 22, 2025; effective June 23, 2025; compliance deadline April 22, 2026 (already passed). Requires separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties, and bars indefinite retention. The FTC did not finalize its proposed ed-tech/school-authorization provisions. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-long-awaited-child-online-privacy), [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2025/05/coppa-rule-ftc-amended-childrens-privacy), [Privacy & Data Security Insight](https://www.privacyanddatasecurityinsight.com/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-protection-act-amendments-effective-june-23-2025/)
- Volunteer background-check costs: Checkr packages from $29.99 per report ($59.99 and $94.99 higher tiers per a third party); Sterling Volunteers $19 (advanced) to $39 (complete) per screen via VCU's published pricing, plus a reported $250 account setup fee. Budget roughly $19-$95 per volunteer; cheaper vendors advertise $5 but those are vendor comparison pages. — [Checkr](https://checkr.com/use-cases/volunteers), [VCU](https://equity.vcu.edu/protection-of-minors/background-check-platform/), [VolunteerBadge](https://www.volunteerbadge.com/blog/volunteer-background-check-cost-2026)
- UPchieve model: foundation-funded at about $5 per student per year with about 20,000 volunteers, so volunteer screening cost per student is low when volunteers serve many students. — [UPchieve mission](https://upchieve.org/mission), [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/how-a-free-24-7-tutoring-model-is-disrupting-learning-loss-for-low-income-kids/)

### Inferences
- A rough inference, not a sourced figure: at Haiku 5.5 prices, even 100 messages per learner per month at a few thousand tokens each is on the order of cents to under a dollar; verify with real token counts. Prompt design (cached system prompt) matters more than model choice.
- Highest-cost items are not compute: background checks per volunteer, report-handling and moderation of chat/video, and legal review of consent flows.
- Lower-risk options: serve adults only (ESL, trade, coding), have programs or schools register minors, and keep minors off open volunteer chat unless volunteers are screened and sessions are logged.
