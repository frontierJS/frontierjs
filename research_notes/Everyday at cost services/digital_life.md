# Everyday digital-life services US households overpay for or go without, and what an at-cost team could offer

Scope: US individuals and families, late 2026 (today 2026-11-15). Legal, medical, tax and benefits excluded. Figures older than 2022 are flagged "PRE-2022". Many web-search summaries came from low-authority aggregator sites; where I fetched a vendor's own page I say so, and where a number rests on an aggregator I say that too.

## 1. What do people rent monthly for photo/video storage, email on their own domain, personal sites, backup, family password managers and digital legacy, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
The big cloud-storage price points have converged: about $5 per TB per month at the 2 TB to 6 TB tier at Apple and Google, and a family-sharing pool of up to 5 or 6 people is standard. Custom-domain email runs about $5/mo (Fastmail) to $84/yr (Google Workspace), a Squarespace or Wix site about $17-19/mo, and password managers $4-6/mo for a family. Digital-legacy handling is mostly free tooling that few people set up.

### Inferences
- Per-TB retail benchmark: iCloud 2 TB $9.99 = $5.00/TB; iCloud 6 TB $29.99 = $5.00/TB; Google 2 TB $9.99 = $5.00/TB (arithmetic on the cited prices). This is the number a co-op has to beat.
- Family sharing (5-6 people on one pool) is the standard unit; a per-family flat price is more natural than per-seat pricing.
- The "own domain email" market splits into two: $5-6/mo/person full-featured (Fastmail, Proton) versus $10-$90/yr flat-per-account hosts (Purelymail, Migadu) where the buyer does the DNS work. A small team's wedge is probably doing the setup, not the mail transport.
- The cheapest legacy fix (Google IAM, Apple Legacy Contact) is free and already exists; the gap is setup and family-level documentation, not software.

## 2. How many people go without, and how have prices and subscription fatigue moved since 2022?

### Takeaway
Loss and no-backup rates are high, but the best-known numbers are old or vendor-sponsored (Deloitte 51% from about 2019; Pew 2017 password data). Newer data (2023-2026) shows ~1/3 of Americans use password managers, only 12% have set account-level digital-legacy controls, and subscription spend is climbing with price hikes across storage and software.

### Cited Findings
- 51% of Americans had a computer or hard-drive failure that lost photos, movies or other content, from a Deloitte "State of the Media Democracy" survey of about 2,000 consumers, as reported by Backblaze. Publication date not stated on the page; image path hints June 2019. PRE-2022, vendor-reported — [Backblaze blog](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/51-of-americans-have-lost-digital-content/)
- A TechRadar-reported survey found only about a third of Americans use cloud backups and over half have had to delete images because of storage limits (date not confirmed; verify before citing) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/billions-of-cherished-photos-at-risk-only-a-third-of-americans-back-up-their-precious-pics-to-the-cloud)
- Handy Recovery Advisor survey of 1,000 US adults (Oct 2024): ~78% say they back up data, but 63% cannot tell sync from backup. Vendor-run survey of a data-recovery firm — [Handy Recovery](https://www.handyrecovery.com/data-backup-statistics/)
- Older surveys: Toshiba (2010) 70% lost data, Seagate/Harris 25% never back up. PRE-2022 and sponsor-run — [same search; Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2009/10/15/for-many-backup-is-not-an-option/)
- Password managers: Pew (Oct 2023) found 32% of Americans use one, up from 20% in 2019; 41% often write passwords down; about 7 in 10 feel overwhelmed by their number of passwords; 63% of those 65+ write them down — [Pew Research, Oct 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/10/18/how-americans-view-data-privacy/)
- 2017 Pew: 12% used password management software; 41% had shared a password with friend or family (PRE-2022) — [Pew 2017](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2017/01/26/americans-and-cybersecurity/)
- Digital legacy: Trust & Will 2026 Estate Planning Report (5,000 US adults): 48% have no instructions for digital accounts and files; only 12% set account-level controls such as Facebook Legacy Contact or Google IAM. Secondhand via a blog; original not retrieved — [AfterYou](https://blog.afteryou.me/digital-legacy-planning-online-accounts-after-death/)
- Subscription fatigue: C+R Research: people guessed $86/mo, itemized tally $219/mo (1,000 consumers; one report dates it to 2022); 74% say it is easy to forget recurring charges — [GCN](https://gcn.com/most-americans-guess-spend-month-subscriptions/21150). CNET 2026: about $111/mo, up from ~$90 in 2025, ~$21/mo on unused subscriptions. Self Financial (Mar 2026, 1,272 people): $26.79/mo on unused subs. Figures vary $35-$273/mo by survey definition — [Subscription roundup (aggregator)](https://resubs.app/resources/subscription-spending-statistics)
- Deloitte Digital Media Trends 2026: average subscribing US household about $69/mo across ~4 paid streaming services (video only, not storage); 2025 survey: 47% say they pay too much — [Subscription roundup summary](https://getrenewal.us/blog/average-monthly-subscription-spend/) (secondary; Deloitte not fetched).
- Price moves since 2022: Microsoft 365 Family +30% and Personal +43% in Jan 2025 (GeekWire above); Google ended free Photos tier 2021; Google restructured plans around AI in 2025-26 (Pro to 5 TB, AI Plus cut to $4.99); iCloud+ US tiers appear unchanged (low-confidence); Hetzner announced 23 Feb 2026 an increase effective 1 April 2026, covering new orders and existing products, no percentage in the statement — [Hetzner statement](https://www.hetzner.com/pressroom/statement-price-adjustment/); press reports up to 37% — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/hetzner-to-raise-prices-by-up-to-37-percent-from-april-1)
- Backblaze B2 raised storage from $6 to $6.95/TB effective May 1, 2026 per an aggregator (the vendor pricing page lists $6.95 but states no change date) — [Upstash](https://upstash.com/blog/backblaze-b2-pricing-explained-2026), [Backblaze](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)
- Microsoft 365 reported in the CMA/AGCM probes in 2026 (UK/Italy): a low-authority ledger claims family OneDrive storage went from 6 TB to 2 TB shared; unverified — [provenbrief](https://provenbrief.com/story/the-dated-price-storage-and-billing-changes-this-ledger-tracks-the-data)

### Inferences
- The strongest recent evidence of "going without" is the sync-vs-backup confusion (63%) and the digital-legacy gap (48% no instructions), not a single clean "lost photos" statistic; the clean loss number is old.
- Infrastructure costs are rising for small providers too (Hetzner, Backblaze increases in 2026), so an at-cost price must be re-based on current wholesale numbers, not 2022 ones.

### Gaps
- No post-2022, non-vendor, nationally representative figure for share of households that lost photos or have no backup. Pew/Consumer Reports pages with cloud-storage or backup questions were not found.
- Did not locate Consumer Reports data.
- Unverified: original Trust & Will report and the TechRadar survey date/sponsor.

## 3. What free, open-source and co-op alternatives exist, and why do they stay niche?

### Takeaway
Open-source photo and sync tools are popular (Immich ~119k stars) but the barrier is Docker-and-Postgres administration, breaking upgrades and "you are your own backup". Funded niche providers (Proton, Fastmail, Ente, Bitwarden) win by selling hosted convenience; volunteer-run hosts (Disroot, Mastodon instances) stay small and fragile.

### Cited Findings
- Immich GitHub (fetched): 119.3k stars, 7.6k forks, 498 open issues, 189 PRs, AGPL-3.0; README urges 3-2-1 backups — [GitHub: immich-app/immich](https://github.com/immich-app/immich). Star milestones: 90K on 1/26/2026, 100K on 5/8/2026, 110K on 8/8/2026 — [Immich roadmap](https://immich.app/roadmap)
- Immich joined FUTO in 2024 so the core team could work full-time, with a stated "no paywalled features" pledge — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/5/immich-joins-futo-to-enhance-development-of-open-source-photo-and-video-backup-solution); a Contabo comparison says v3.0.0 shipped July 1, 2026 — [Contabo](https://contabo.com/blog/immich-vs-photoprism-vs-ente/)
- Immich upgrade pain (user reports): 1.118 moved port 3001 to 2283 and required compose/reverse-proxy edits; ~1.33 changed the vector database and needed compose edits; a user idle from 1.29x to 1.41x hit three breaking upgrades; one commenter says setups "run fine for a while and then break about six months later" — [HN thread 46172664](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46172664), [HN 46095087](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46095087), [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/10/immich-update-brings-improved-mobile-ui-ux-port-changes-better-jpeg-compression-and-more). Counterpoints: others report one upgrade issue in two years, or "whole household uses it, even the kids."
- Usability barrier quote: one HN commenter would not expect "a non tech savy user to manage their own instance"; another says "You need to be a web dev or a sys admin" — [HN 46172664](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46172664)
- Nextcloud: nextcloud/server about 35-37k stars (aggregator figures disagree; one dated Nov 2026 says ~37.4k); community server is free, paid Enterprise tiers buy support, not features; Enterprise Basic €36/user/year at 100 users; server 35 / Hub 26 Summer released Sep 2026 — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/is-nextcloud-free/), [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/collaboration-software/a/nextcloud/). Aggregator-level sourcing; verify.
- PhotoPrism: self-funded via paid memberships, team plans, services and a PikaPods share; says donations alone don't fund a full-time team; AGPL community edition with member-only features — [PhotoPrism FAQ](https://www.photoprism.app/oss/faq/), [About](https://www.photoprism.app/about/company/)
- Ente (vendor page, fetched): 10 GB free, then 50 GB $2.49/mo, 200 GB $4.99/mo, 1 TB $9.99/mo ($119.88/yr), 2 TB $19.99/mo; family plan of up to 5 shares storage at no extra cost; 3 replicas on paid plans; end-to-end encrypted. Page's structured data lists EUR values, so check live prices — [Ente pricing](https://ente.com/pricing). Server open-sourced March 2024 under AGPL-3.0 (Contabo, above).
- Proton: Swiss non-profit Proton Foundation has been the controlling shareholder of for-profit Proton AG since June 2024; funded by subscriptions, no VC; claims 100M+ accounts and 600+ employees — [Proton Foundation / ownership](https://proton.me/foundation), [Proton team](https://proton.me/about/team), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_AG)
- Fastmail and Bitwarden prices above show the "hosted convenience" model costs $3-11/mo.
- Mastodon / co-op: small instances close often. Examples named: mastodon.technology (admin burnout after 5.5 years), med-mastodon, botsin.space, moth.social, StrangeObject.Space (closing end 2024); moderation is the most time-consuming task; one fediview guide puts a single-user instance at ~€18/mo on a Hetzner VPS and a 5-20 user server at €50-100/mo, with media storage outpacing compute within six months — [Notes on single-person Mastodon (Julia Evans)](https://jvns.ca/blog/2023/08/11/some-notes-on-mastodon/), [GFSC discontinued server](https://gfsc.community/why-we-discontinued-our-mastodon-server/), [fediview guide](https://fediview.com/articles/host-your-own-mastodon-server-2026/) (guide is third-party, cost figures unverified)
- Framasoft (fetched): 35 members, 10 employees, ~10,428 patrons; donations were 94% of 2025 income; spend split 74% employees, 8% servers and domains; runs about 60 services on 100+ servers; created CHATONS network to point people at other hosts rather than scale itself — [Framasoft](https://framasoft.org/en/)

### Inferences
- Immich's growth (≈600-700 stars/week in late 2026 per aggregators) shows demand for "own your photos"; the gap is a non-technical family that will not run Docker, which is exactly what a hosted at-cost operator sells.
- The model that survives at tiny scale pays the operator (Framasoft 74% salaries; May First staff paid from dues; Disroot partial stipends), while the free-and-volunteer model tends toward burnout closures.
- Framasoft's CHATONS stance (don't scale; refer) is itself a design choice a small team should weigh.

- No dependable measure of how many people actually use each alternative (only stars, which are not users).
- No data on user retention/churn for Ente or Proton Family, and no Fastmail financials.
- Disroot's donate page 404'd on the URL I tried; figures come from search results (see Q5).

## 4. What does it really cost to run per user: storage, deliverability, abuse, support, data-loss liability?

### Takeaway
Raw storage is cheap (about €2-3/TB/mo at Hetzner Storage Box, $6.95/TB/mo at Backblaze B2) versus about $5/TB at Apple/Google retail, but redundancy, email reputation, abuse handling and the operator's time dominate real cost. Email is the hardest: Gmail/Yahoo enforce authentication and spam-rate thresholds and a small provider shares one IP's reputation.

### Cited Findings
Storage
- Backblaze B2 (vendor page, fetched): from $6.95/TB/mo, billed per byte-hour; first 10 GB free; egress free up to 3x average monthly stored data then $0.01/GB; free via partner CDNs; Class A/B/C calls free, Class D $0.004 per 10,000 — [Backblaze pricing](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)
- Hetzner Storage Box (vendor page, fetched, prices excl. VAT): BX11 1 TB €3.20/mo; BX21 5 TB €10.90; BX31 10 TB €20.80; BX41 20 TB €40.60; unlimited traffic; setup fee exists but amount not shown — [Hetzner Storage Box](https://www.hetzner.com/storage/storage-box/). I could not confirm whether these are pre- or post-April 2026 prices; Hetzner's statement doesn't name Storage Box or give percentages — [Hetzner statement](https://www.hetzner.com/pressroom/statement-price-adjustment/). Hetzner object storage base rose €4.99 to €6.49 and block volumes €0.044 to €0.0572/GB/mo (secondary, search summary) — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/hetzner-to-raise-prices-by-up-to-37-percent-from-april-1), [bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/08/16/hetzner-2026-price-shocks-owning-hardware-pitch)
- A second Hetzner adjustment was published 27 May 2026, effective 15 June 2026, mainly compute; object storage unchanged then (search summary, secondary) — [Sideship](https://sideship.moritarot.com/blogs/hetzner-price-rise-2026)

Email deliverability
- Google sender guidelines (fetched): all senders to Gmail need SPF or DKIM; senders over 5,000 messages/day need SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, From-alignment (effective Feb 1, 2024); keep Postmaster spam rate under 0.30%, monitoring guidance under 0.10%; sending IP needs PTR that resolves forward to the same IP; TLS required — [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)
- Outlook has rejected non-compliant bulk mail since May 2025 and Gmail/Microsoft now return permanent SMTP rejections rather than quiet filtering (third-party summary) — [Minotaurus](https://www.minotaurus.com/en/insights/deliverability-gmail-yahoo), [CloudNews](https://cloudnews.tech/sending-email-in-2026-is-basically-running-infrastructure-here-are-the-rules/)
- Warm-up for a new IP takes weeks; sporadic dedicated-IP sending can hurt reputation (third-party summary, same sources).
- Migadu's published limits show how a small provider caps risk: Micro 20 outgoing/day, Mini 100, Standard 500, Maxi 2,000 — [Migadu pricing](https://www.migadu.com/pricing/)

Abuse, support, liability, operator time
- Mastodon moderation is "often the most time-consuming part" and does not shrink to zero with few users; neglect leads other admins to defederate (see Q3 sources).
- Disroot (all-donation Dutch foundation): 2023 spending EUR 15,952.81, of which EUR 5,320.63 infrastructure and payment fees (~EUR 440/mo), EUR 7,584 volunteer fees, EUR 2,992 donated to FLOSS; one core member compensated EUR 1,000/mo in the 2024 budget — [Disroot 2023 annual report](https://disroot.org/annual_reports/AnnualReport2023.pdf), via search summary of the report; I did not open the PDF.
- Framasoft: 74% of spend is employees, 8% servers and domains — [Framasoft](https://framasoft.org/en/)
- Immich README: "Always follow 3-2-1 backup plan for your precious photos and videos!" — [GitHub](https://github.com/immich-app/immich); a 2026 comparison warns neither Immich nor Nextcloud is a backup because each is one service on one machine — [Priviy](https://www.priviy.com/en/blog/immich-vs-nextcloud-2026)
- Immich keeps metadata in PostgreSQL, which complicates backups versus a plain file tree (HN/comparison summaries; see Q3).

### Inferences (arithmetic and judgment, not cited facts)
- Hetzner BX21 is about €2.18/TB (10.90/5) and BX41 about €2.03/TB (40.60/20), roughly 40% of the $5/TB iCloud/Google retail price before VAT, redundancy beyond one box, and Backblaze's $6.95/TB (which is itself above the Apple/Google per-TB price; B2 does not undercut on raw price, its value is egress and durability).
- Real storage cost = raw TB x replication. A 3-copy scheme (e.g. one Hetzner box, one B2 bucket, one local) puts the floor near $9-12/TB/mo, i.e. at or above Apple/Google's $5/TB, so at-cost is only competitive for the single-copy or limited-replication case, or when Hetzner-only (the 5 TB BX21 box at €10.90 split across a few families; no 2 TB plan exists).
- For email, the cost is the reputation and abuse work (PTR, DKIM, DMARC, complaint-rate watching, IP warm-up), not the hardware. Relaying outbound through a reputable provider or capping send rates (Migadu's approach) is the lever.
- Support time is the largest unmetered cost; every funded example (Framasoft 74% staff, May First staff-from-dues, Disroot stipends) pays people, which a "free" offer cannot hide.
- Data-loss liability: no source found on legal liability for hobby/at-cost providers; the sensible stance is the 3-2-1 message plus an exportable file layout.

### Gaps
- No source for an operator's actual support burden per user, abuse-handling labor hours or typical data-loss incident rates at small hosts.
- Did not find a Fastmail or Proton description of their per-user cost or deliverability staff.
- Hetzner Storage Box pre/post-April 2026 price delta unconfirmed; setup fee amount not shown by the fetch.
- Whether Backblaze "TB" is 1,000 or 1,024 GB is not defined on its pricing page (per search aggregator).
- No cited cost for Hetzner VPS/dedicated server running a multi-user Immich/Nextcloud instance; only the Mastodon guide's third-party estimates.
- Legal liability for data loss not researched (outside scope, flagged).

## 5. At-cost and co-op providers: how are they funded?

### Takeaway
The working models are: member dues on a sliding scale (May First, Social.coop), small-donor funding with volunteer stipends (Disroot), donations that pay a small staff (Framasoft), and a free tier cross-subsidized by paid plans (Ente) or a corporate parent (Immich/FUTO, Proton Foundation). None is pure "free"; each pays at least one person.

### Cited Findings
- Social.coop (Open Collective page, fetched): members give a sliding scale of £1 to £10/mo; tiers £12, £36 and £120/yr; estimated annual budget £12,590.79; 915 contributors; total raised £78,727.20 (page also shows £85,488.22 across 1,095 contributions, unreconciled); balance £29,469.57; expense tags include community, tech working group (£4,438.04), finance working group (£2,945.00). It co-owns and co-governs a Mastodon instance — [Social.coop on Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/socialcoop)
- Social.coop 2018 governance thread explored $2/mo target with a 150-paying-member minimum for a $3,600/yr budget — [Loomio](https://www.loomio.com/d/cIA3pK4a/looking-at-the-long-term-financial-vitality-of-social-coop)
- May First Movement Technology (search summary): funded almost entirely by member dues on a sliding scale by income or budget plus storage; Basic $50/yr (voting rights, 1 email account, 10 GB); hosting $63/yr (40 GB) to $500/yr (500 GB); VPS $45-$220/mo; three workers (one full-time, two part-time, one volunteer); became a nonprofit membership organization in June 2019; the site says no foundation grants — [May First membership options](https://mayfirst.coop/membership-options/plans-pricing/), [Fortunately Magazine](https://fortunately.us/may-first)
- Disroot: Dutch foundation, founded 2015, donations only; donate page said 220 supporters; 2020: EUR 13,465 from avg 119 supporters/mo; 2023: EUR 25,334.14 from avg 197 supporters/mo; free 1 GB mailbox with extra storage at EUR 0.15/GB/mo up to 10 GB (old ProPrivacy review, may have changed) — [Disroot annual reports](https://disroot.org/annual_reports/AnnualReport2023.pdf), [ProPrivacy](https://proprivacy.com/email/review/disroot), [Liberapay](https://liberapay.com/Disroot/). Summary came via search, PDFs not opened.
- Framasoft: donations 94% of 2025 income, monthly gifts from EUR 2 — [Framasoft](https://framasoft.org/en/)
- PhotoPrism: member-funded; Ente: paid plans over open code; Immich: FUTO-backed; Proton: Foundation-controlled, subscription-funded (Q3 sources).
- Funding-scale benchmark: one blogger suggests mstdn.social's 20,000 users paying EUR 1/mo each would cover all its bills (opinion) — [Julia Evans notes / Mastodon cost discussion](https://jvns.ca/blog/2023/08/11/some-notes-on-mastodon/)

### Inferences
- Rough scale: Disroot spends about EUR 1,300/mo and Social.coop budgets about £12.6k/yr; both run on a few hundred supporters, so a team operating at a few dozen to a couple hundred families is within a proven funding envelope.
- Sliding scale plus voting rights (May First's $50 floor, Social.coop £1-£10) is the pay-what-you-can mechanism that already works in this niche.
- Pay-what-you-can plus a small guaranteed floor appears more durable than purely voluntary donations, because Disroot's active-user-to-donor ratio was under 100 donors in 2018 against thousands of users (2018 figure, PRE-2022).

### Gaps
- No US-based, family-focused at-cost photo/storage co-op found; all examples are activist/tech-community oriented or European. Searches did not turn up one (not exhaustively checked).
- No Riseup, Tilde, or other US collective funding data retrieved.
- Disroot's own donate page 404'd; total user counts for Disroot, Social.coop and May First not found.
