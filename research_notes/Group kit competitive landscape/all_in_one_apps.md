# All-in-one group and community apps for small adult-run US groups, and how organizers stitch tools together

Research date: 2026-11-15. US market. Every figure below came from a search-result summary or a fetched page; the source URL is next to it. Where a vendor page was fetched directly it is marked "(vendor page, fetched)". Where I only had a third party it is marked "(third party)". Reddit could not be searched: the search tool refuses reddit.com ("domains not accessible to our user agent"), so no Reddit thread is quoted anywhere in this file. See Gaps.

## 1. Bundled group apps: pricing, business model, what each does, install/account rules, ownership and complaints

### Takeaway
No bundled app is both free and complete for the kit's four jobs (sign-ups, volunteer scheduling, event pages, paid tickets/dues) with money going to the group's own Stripe account. Heylo is the closest and the only one with a Meetup-switcher story, but it charges 5% + $0.59 on the free tier and its payment flow is not described as direct-to-the-group's-Stripe in anything I fetched. Spond is free and strong for sports/clubs but takes 3.29% + $1.00 per product. The big messaging tools (GroupMe, WhatsApp, Facebook, Discord) are free but have no payments or sign-up depth. Volunteer scheduling is not a headline feature of any bundled app I checked.

### Cited Findings

**Heylo (Meetup-style, in-person groups)**
- Pricing (vendor page, fetched): Base $0/mo, 5% + $0.59 per payment transaction; Plus $19/mo, 4% + $0.49; Pro $59/mo, 2% + $0.29; Business $199/mo, 1% + $0.10; Organization custom. Unlimited members, admins, events and RSVPs on every tier. — [Heylo pricing](https://www.heylo.com/pricing)
- Base includes member directories, group chats, payment collection, Heylo Discovery listing, 500 emails/month, and cash sponsorships from brands. Plus adds analytics and questionnaires; Pro adds waiver collection and embedded events on your website. — [Heylo pricing](https://www.heylo.com/pricing)
- Makes money from payment fees and paid tiers (inferred from the tiered fee table; Heylo says its only fee is a small service fee on payments, versus Meetup's subscription plus "5-10% ticket fees"). — [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up) (vendor claim about a competitor)
- Members/account: "Members can participate via email, web, or the Heylo app", with no mandatory downloads. The page does not say whether an account is needed to RSVP. — [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up). The pricing page is silent on app/account. — [Heylo pricing](https://www.heylo.com/pricing)
- Ownership/funding: San Francisco, founded 2019, co-founders Eric Winters (CEO) and Brandon Pearcy (CTO), ex-Google. Funding sources disagree: $1.5M in one round announced Nov 2022 (Tracxn, VCBacked) vs $2.0M (StartupIntros). A PitchBook "Heylo" profile (founded 2025, $100K) looks like a different company. — [search summary of Tracxn / StartupIntros / VCBacked / PitchBook](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/heylo/___YMc3aSPACXhPb8unXh4zIwH4wodeBZ_zWXXQ2vCfLs), [About Heylo](https://www.heylo.com/about)
- Self-reported rating: 4.9 App Store average. No independent review count found. — [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up)
- Vendor testimonials (marketing, no methodology): Brooklyn Track Club "paid members increased 3x to over 600" and $150k collected in year one; a volleyball group reports 4x participation; an organizer says players in their 70s use it without issue. — [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up)
- Complaint evidence: one third-party reviewer says Heylo's discovery only works in cities and categories where it has density; no alternative replaces Meetup's stranger discovery. — [Kannect comparison, via search summary](https://kannect.co/blog/compare/meetup-alternatives/). Third-party blogs (Group.app) list different Plus/Pro prices ($9/$29), which conflict with Heylo's own page; treat as wrong. — [Group.app](https://www.group.app/blog/alternatives-to-meetup/)
- I found no independent complaint corpus (App Store/Play text, Reddit) for Heylo. Gap.

**Spond (free team/club app; US presence)**
- Pricing: free app, no monthly fee. US payment fee $1.00 + 3.29% per product in a payment request, including Stripe's costs; the group absorbs it by default or can pass it to members. A help-center article mentions a temporary reduced fee in October 2026 (rate not visible to me). — [Spond payment costs (help center)](https://help.spond.com/app/en/articles/118091-payments-costs-in-the-spond-app) (via search summary; page itself not fetched)
- Spond's own US page says no monthly fees and a small per-transaction fee; it lists events, messaging and file storage among app features, and does not mention polls. Payments are built into the app so payers do not need a separate app. Account requirement not stated. — [Spond US payments blog](https://www.spond.com/en-us/news-and-blog/sports-team-payments-usa/) (vendor page, fetched)
- Third-party worked example: $150 season fee x 20 families costs about $5.94 per family (~$119 total) in fees. — [Spond-related comparison via search summary](https://www.spond.com/en-us/news-and-blog/spond-v-teamsnap-v-sportsengine/)
- Other revenue: Spond Club (paid multi-team layer, no public self-serve price) and Superdraw fundraising where the club keeps 65% of ticket sales (third party). — [BeeNet](https://beenet.app/blog/spond-alternatives-for-sports-clubs-2026/); [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/products/spond/)
- Members: App Store listing says the app is not required and members can receive information by email. — [Spond App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id755596884) (search summary)
- Ownership/funding: Oslo, Norway; founder Magnus Kober; $20.59M raised, last round $10.82M on 2021-09-28 (CB Insights). Investors not visible. US App Store rating 4.9 from 2.6K ratings. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/spond), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id755596884)
- Paid-event refunds must be arranged outside the app (third party). — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/products/spond/)
- I found no US launch/market-share data and no complaint text. Gap. Spond is sports-team shaped, not general adult community groups.

**BAND (Naver)**
- Free; in-app purchases $1.46 to $53.09 per item; Google Play lists ads and in-app purchases. — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nhn.android.band&hl=en_US&gl=US) (via search summary)
- Does: board, shared calendar, polls, to-do, private chat, attendance, maps, fee collection, photo sharing, read receipts; reviewers praise sign-ups and polls. — [AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/band-app-for-all-groups-by-naver-corp), [Justuseapp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/542613198/band-app-for-all-groups/reviews)
- Members join through a shared Band URL (SMS, messenger, email). Members use the app. — [AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/band-app-for-all-groups-by-naver-corp)
- Ratings: App Store 4.7 from 74K; Google Play about 4.8 from ~776K. Of 1,399 written US App Store reviews, 69.5% positive, 21.6% negative. — [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/542613198/reviews/3), [AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/band-app-for-all-groups-by-naver-corp)
- Repeated complaints: promo and sticker-shop notifications ("90% of notifications are offers"), "Buggy, full of ads, tracking, and other privacy nightmares", unreliable notifications, tedious sign-up setup, sign-up deadline limits, redesign dislike, phone-only functions. — [AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/band-app-for-all-groups-by-naver-corp)
- Payment fee details for US groups and whether money goes to the group's own account: not found. Gap.

**GroupMe (Microsoft)**
- Free. Microsoft has kept investing: Dec 2025 update (v15.28) added voice messages, Copilot chat summaries, automatic event albums, in-group polls. App includes events and polls, albums, DMs. — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/groupme-lives-on-microsoft-rolls-out-ai-features-and-shared-event-albums/), [Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/microsoft-gives-groupme-a-major-upgrade-with-copilot-chat-summaries-and-voice-messages/)
- Recent change: GroupMe announced 2026-08-17 that it is ending two-way SMS mode; members must use the app or groupme.com. A petition to reverse it was reported and not honored. — [RallyText](https://rallytext.app/groupme-sms-shutdown) (competitor blog; verify against GroupMe's notice), [JTech Forums](https://jtechforums.org/t/groupme-sms-is-over/8316)
- Implication: the "text-only member" path in GroupMe is closed from August 2026 (inference from the above).
- No payments or volunteer scheduling in the cited material. Business model not stated in sources found. Gap.

**WhatsApp Communities (Meta)**
- Group up to 1,024 members; a Community up to 100 groups and 2,000 total members; polls supported. Communities cannot be created from the Business app. — [Watask](https://www.watask.com/guides/whatsapp-group-limits), [Inviter](https://inviter.co/blog/whatsapp-community-member-limit)
- Pricing/revenue model, payments, events: not found in my sources. WhatsApp is not documented here as having RSVP/sign-up. Gap. Requires an account tied to a phone number (general knowledge, uncited).

**Facebook Groups (Meta)**
- Free; ad-funded platform. Reach decline claims are blogger anecdote, not Meta data: admins report 30-60% organic reach drops 2023-2025; one blogger claims 70-80% for text/image posts. Several sources note Meta has not announced Groups are retiring. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/is-facebook-shutting-down-groups/), [Circle's Facebook alternatives post (competitor, biased)](https://circle.so/blog/facebook-groups-alternatives)
- Circle's post says Meta announced removal of Group community chat in Oct 2025; another source disputes this. — [Circle](https://circle.so/blog/facebook-groups-alternatives) vs [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-group-chats-phased-out/)
- June 2025: bot networks reportedly exploited Meta AI moderation to mass-delete thousands of legitimate groups with little recourse. — [Circle](https://circle.so/blog/facebook-groups-alternatives) (single competitor source)
- No built-in payments or real-time chat per a comparison page. — [Nexspark / clubfinder-style comparison via search](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)

**Discord for local groups**
- Free. Reported drawbacks: steep learning curve (channels, roles, bots), empty-channel onboarding, admin burden, search limits, no built-in RSVP or ticketing, and an anecdotal comment that older people do not want to join. — [Siit review](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/discord-review), [Tribechat](https://tribechat.com/blog/why-discord-falls-short-for-community-building), [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives). Tribechat is a competitor.
- 2025 vendor breach exposed government ID photos; ID/selfie age checks tested mid-2026 (one review, not independently confirmed). — [Siit review](https://www.siit.io/tools/trending/discord-review)

**Geneva (status)**
- Bumble announced and closed acquisition of Geneva; Geneva's own page says the acquisition "has officially closed". PitchBook: acquisition 2024-07-01, $17.5M. — [Geneva](https://www.geneva.com/), [Geneva blog](https://www.geneva.com/blog/meant-to-bee), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/433219-60)
- Two secondary sources say Bumble folded Geneva into the relaunched Bumble BFF in September 2025 and shut the standalone app, with Geneva generating zero revenue through June 30, 2025; one review site (updated July 2026) says Geneva still runs. I found no primary Bumble announcement of the shutdown. Treat as "very likely discontinued, unconfirmed". — [Toarn](https://toarn.com/insights/geneva) vs [Creator Stack Club](https://www.creatorstackclub.com/software/geneva)

**Hylo (not Heylo; open-source commons)**
- Free "for now" on the full platform; Hylo Pro $60/mo per group; Hylo Assistant $20/mo; white-label custom; non-profit commons funded by paid products and contributions. No payment processing fees mentioned. — [Hylo pricing](https://www.hylo.com/pricing) (search summary; page not fetched)

**Konfetti**
- I found no "Konfetti" group app matching group events plus payments. Search returned Confetti (team-building event marketplace, from $30/user) and Confetti.events (event ops platform, free plan; paid from 1,585 SEK/mo). Probably not a US adult-group competitor. — [Confetti.events/Software Advice via search](https://www.softwareadvice.com/virtual-event/confetti-profile/). If the brief meant a different product, name it.

**Nextdoor groups**
- Groups support messages, polls and events, scoped to one neighborhood, several or a city; Events Hub rebuilt in 2026; one reviewer calls Groups/Events "empty containers waiting for a motivated organizer". — [Nexspark Nextdoor review](https://nexspark.org/journal/nextdoor-review)
- Ad business: Q3 2026 revenue $78.8M (+14% YoY), self-serve 69% of revenue, 24.5M weekly active users. — [Motley Fool Q3 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/05/nextdoor-nxdr-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- No ticketing, dues or volunteer scheduling found.

**Other notable bundled competitors surfaced**
- Luma (vendor page, fetched): free with 5% platform fee on paid events plus Stripe fees; Plus $59/mo billed annually, 0% platform fee; unlimited events and guests; whether guests need an account not stated. — [Luma pricing](https://luma.com/pricing)
- TinyClub (third party): free plan, up to 400 members, chat, events, payments with a 5% platform fee. — [Tiny Club](https://tiny.club/meetup-alternative)
- Who's In (vendor marketing): RSVP with capacity/waitlist, Stripe payments for dues and events, web push with no install. — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/alternative-to-groupme)
- Invyt: Stripe payments, "no app, ever", better for one-off events. — [Invyt](https://invyt.io/partiful-alternative)
- Partiful: free; its payments feature just opens Venmo/PayPal/Cash App. Meeting Star discontinued. RSVP Street needs no account to RSVP. — [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)
- Meetup: after Bending Spoons' acquisition, organizer prices rose 6 June 2024; members asked to pay about $2/RSVP or a subscription. — [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)

### Inferences
- Of the bundled apps, only Heylo, Who's In and TinyClub combine events, chat, and Stripe payments for adult clubs; all three charge a platform cut (Heylo 5% + $0.59; TinyClub 5%) or are tiny vendors. None describes volunteer scheduling as a feature in the material I found.
- "Free" in this category means "paid by payment fees" (Heylo, Spond, TinyClub, Luma) or "paid by ads/platform" (Facebook, Nextdoor, BAND, GroupMe). A group-owned-Stripe, at-cost model would be a pricing difference, not a feature difference.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (tool block) and no direct App Store/Play review text for Heylo, Spond US, GroupMe, Nextdoor groups.
- Heylo: whether payouts go to the group's own Stripe account or to Heylo as platform is not stated in anything fetched.
- WhatsApp and GroupMe business models and WhatsApp account rules not sourced.
- Konfetti not identified.

## 2. Paid community platforms (the paid ceiling)

### Takeaway
These start at about $79 to $143 per month and charge 0.5% to 10% transaction fees on top of Stripe. They are creator/course-community tools, priced 4 to 10 times above what a hobby club or volunteer group will pay.

### Cited Findings
- Circle (vendor page, fetched): Professional $89/mo (2% transaction fee), Business $199/mo (1%), Scale $419/mo (1%), Circle Plus custom (0.5%); prices assume annual billing, no separate monthly price listed. — [Circle pricing](https://circle.so/pricing)
- Mighty Networks (vendor page, fetched): Launch $79/mo (2%), Scale $179/mo (1%), Growth $354/mo (0.5%), Mighty Pro custom; page's annual figure looked inconsistent ($950/yr vs 10 x $79). Third-party sources quote $95/$215 for Launch/Scale, so figures conflict. — [Mighty Networks pricing](https://www.mightynetworks.com/pricing); [third-party comparison](https://zanfia.com/blog/circle-pricing/)
- Skool (vendor page, fetched): Hobby $9/mo with 10% transaction fee; Pro $99/mo with 2.9% transaction fee. — [Skool pricing](https://www.skool.com/pricing)
- Kajabi (third party only; Kajabi page not fetched): annual billing Basic $143, Growth $199, Pro $399 per month; monthly $179/$249/$499; one community space on Basic and Growth, three on Pro; Kajabi Payments has no transaction fee. — [Ruzuku Kajabi community review](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/kajabi-community-review)
- Positioning in a third-party roundup: Skool cheapest, Circle for brands, Mighty for large communities wanting native apps, Kajabi for creators bundling courses, email and a site. — [Skillscouter](https://skillscouter.com/best-platforms-to-build-a-paid-community/)
- Members need accounts on all of these (community platforms are login-based; stated as general knowledge, no source fetched).

### Inferences
- Skool Hobby at $9/mo + 10% is the only cheap entry, and its fee is higher than Heylo Base's blended fee on typical ticket sizes above about $12 (10% of $20 = $2.00 vs Heylo 5% + $0.59 = $1.59; arithmetic from the cited rates).
- None offers volunteer scheduling or event pages in the Meetup sense; they are not in the same buying decision as a running club or board-game group.

### Gaps
- Kajabi pricing page not fetched. Skool/Circle/Mighty app-install and account requirements not sourced.

## 3. Church all-in-ones (how they bundle only; dues covered by another researcher)

### Takeaway
Church suites bundle people records, groups, calendar, registrations, check-in, giving and a member app, but they are priced per module or flat $72 to $119/mo and are shaped around a church's congregation, not a neutral small group.

### Cited Findings (all third-party; vendor pages not fetched)
- Planning Center is modular: free People/Check-Ins tier, then Services, Giving, Calendar, Groups, Registrations at roughly $10 to $20 each, typical mid-size church $100 to $200/mo; card processing 2.15% + $0.30; Church Center is its member-facing app. — [Pushpay roundup via search](https://pushpay.com/blog/best-church-management-software-for-2026/), [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/tithely-vs-planning-center/)
- Tithe.ly: giving module has no monthly platform charge, 2.9% + $0.30 per card gift; branded church app from about $59/mo; All-Access $119/mo bundles giving, ChMS (Breeze engine), custom app, website builder. Planning Center has no custom branded app or website builder. — [ChurchMemberPro pricing guide](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/tithely-pricing-guide/)
- Breeze is owned by Tithe.ly: $72/mo flat, unlimited people and giving, now sold as Tithely Church Management; sources disagree ($66 to $120). — [ChurchMemberPro Breeze alternatives](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/breeze-alternatives/)
- Small churches (under ~100 people) often fit Planning Center's free tier. — [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/tithely-vs-planning-center/)
- Note: ChurchMemberPro is itself a church-software vendor/reviewer; treat comparative judgments as biased.

### Inferences
- The church market already proves the bundle (people + groups + sign-ups + giving + member app) is what small organizations pay for; modular per-app pricing at ~$10 to $20 is an existing price anchor for a kit.

### Gaps
- No r/churchtech or review text. No evidence on whether congregants must install Church Center (likely yes for full features, uncited).

## 4. Organizer behavior: tool count, app refusal, switching triggers

### Takeaway
There is no credible survey of "tools per volunteer organizer"; the best evidence is the nonprofit stack data and vendor/forum descriptions of a spreadsheet plus group chat plus form pattern. App-install reluctance is well measured for consumers generally and anecdotal for groups. The strongest documented switching trigger is Meetup's June 2024 price rise.

### Cited Findings
- Tool count (nonprofit staff, not volunteers): Omatic 2026 survey of 800+ nonprofit professionals: 90% run 3+ core applications, 80% run 4+, 70% run 5+ (62% the year before). — [Omatic trends report](https://omaticsoftware.com/blog/https-omaticsoftware-com-2026-nonprofit-tech-trends-that-will-change-your-approach-to-technology/) (vendor-published survey)
- Volunteer coordination: typical stacks are paper sign-in sheets, spreadsheets, group chats and email; coordinator time split scheduling 28%, communication 24%, hours 18% (Better Impact 2025 as cited second-hand by a vendor blog). — [ustechautomations (second-hand)](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/nonprofit-volunteer-management-automation-comparison), [CallHub](https://callhub.io/blog/volunteer-management/volunteer-management-software/)
- SignUpGenius reports organizers moving toward narrow tools for sign-ups, schedules and communication, sometimes paired with forms, payments or check-in tools, rather than all-in-one platforms. — [SignUpGenius](https://www.signupgenius.com/blog/volunteer-operations-are-changing) (vendor)
- A guide suggests an availability poll plus a simple spreadsheet for small events, with a group chat for day-of coordination. — [WhenNOT](https://whennot.com/blog/volunteer-scheduling-coordinate-manage-event-volunteers)
- Parent/app fatigue (anecdote): a DC Urban Moms thread asks why every league wants its own messaging app; one parent lists TeamSnap, a parent-chat app, a booster app, a sports-admin app and tournament apps; others say their leagues just use email. Thread is old (around two years). — [DC Urban Mom](https://www.dcurbanmom.com/jforum/posts/list/15/1219579.page)
- App refusal (consumer surveys, not group-specific): HostingAdvice survey of 1,000 US adults, May 2025: 75% prefer a mobile website over downloading an app; 58% find forced app installs more irritating than a poor mobile site. — [HostingAdvice](https://www.hostingadvice.com/studies/survey-mandatory-app-downloads/). Clutch (476 consumers): 18% refuse to download when a browser alternative exists; 80% have been forced to download an app; 54% delete an app after use. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/app-fatigue-what-means-businesses), [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260421358096/en/Clutch-Report-80-of-Users-are-Forced-to-Download-Apps). Adobe: 72% downloaded a retail app for one-time use and later deleted it. — [Mass Market Retailers](https://massmarketretailers.com/app-fatigue-is-real-says-adobe-report/)
- Account friction examples: RSVP Street needs no account to RSVP; Who's In and Invyt market no-install flows; Spond says its app is optional with email delivery. — [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives), [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/alternative-to-groupme), [Spond App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id755596884)
- Switching trigger, Meetup: price rise on 6 June 2024, the first organizer increase since 2019; Hacker News organizers reported six-month plans going $29 to $99 and renewals roughly doubling (e.g., $98 to $178); one 2026 comparison says the annual plan went $108.99 to $214.79; came after Bending Spoons acquired Meetup and features moved behind paywalls. — [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives), [Andy Piper](https://andypiper.co.uk/2024/10/18/meetup-com-is-so-over/), [Medium: Pay to Skip](https://medium.com/@hoffbits/pay-to-skip-why-meetup-risks-undermining-its-own-community-635cb1c25f9c)
- Destination of switchers: Heylo for ongoing groups, Luma for tech/professional events, Eventbrite/Partiful for one-offs, Discord or Facebook for chat. — [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives)
- A homeschool group organizer who moved to Heylo: Meetup "ended up doubling" with about a month's notice, DMs and photos paywalled; she "didn't realize how many tech issues our members were having with Meetup"; moving Discord chat into Heylo raised participation. Vendor-published case study. — [Heylo blog](https://www.heylo.com/blog/meetup-move-homeschool-group)
- A Meetup organizer of 7 years says they are moving to Heylo in January 2026. — [Heylo blog comments](https://www.heylo.com/blog/meetup-move-homeschool-group) (search summary of a comment)
- Meetup will not let organizers remove past events/reviews/ratings, a stickiness/lock-in point noted by a commenter. — [Andy Piper](https://andypiper.co.uk/2024/10/18/meetup-com-is-so-over/)
- Switching cost: moving means re-inviting members. Heylo claims 2-minute onboarding of a whole group (vendor claim). — [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up)
- Facebook trigger: reach decline and fear of sudden group deletion (see Section 1). — [Circle](https://circle.so/blog/facebook-groups-alternatives)
- GroupMe trigger: SMS mode ending Aug 2026 pushes SMS-only members to install the app. — [RallyText](https://rallytext.app/groupme-sms-shutdown)

### Inferences
- The consumer survey numbers are not about group joining; a one-time "view page and RSVP in a browser" flow is consistent with them, but no source tests that for volunteer groups.
- Dues/tickets and RSVP are where organizers already pay (Meetup, Heylo, Luma); the Meetup 2024 hike is the one documented mass switching event for this audience.
- Vendor sources dominate everywhere in this section; no independent survey of volunteer-organizer tool count exists in what I found.

### Gaps
- No survey of "how many tools a typical volunteer organizer juggles". Best proxy is Omatic (nonprofit staff).
- No Reddit evidence (r/organizing, r/Meetup, running clubs, boardgames, r/churchtech) because the tool blocks reddit.com.
- Nothing on switching costs beyond re-inviting members and losing Meetup history.

## 6. Is any all-in-one free and good enough today?

### Takeaway
No. Free options exist (Spond, Heylo Base, GroupMe, BAND, Luma, TinyClub) but each either takes a payment cut (3.29% + $1 to 5% + $0.59), lacks volunteer scheduling, or forces an app. Based on documented evidence, none lets a group take dues and tickets into its own Stripe account at cost while also doing shift sign-ups.

### Cited Findings
- Heylo free tier: 5% + $0.59 per transaction on top of unlimited events and chat. — [Heylo pricing](https://www.heylo.com/pricing)
- Spond: 3.29% + $1.00 per product, which is expensive on small dues (a $20 dues payment loses 3.29% + $1 = $1.66, about 8.3%; arithmetic from cited rate). — [Spond help](https://help.spond.com/app/en/articles/118091-payments-costs-in-the-spond-app)
- Luma free: 5% platform fee plus Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 for paid events; Plus removes the platform fee at $59/mo. — [Luma pricing](https://luma.com/pricing)
- SignUpGenius observes organizers using narrow tools rather than all-in-ones. — [SignUpGenius](https://www.signupgenius.com/blog/volunteer-operations-are-changing)

### Inferences
- The gap is a cost gap on payments (pass-through at Stripe's rate) plus volunteer scheduling, not a messaging gap.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether Heylo, Who's In or TinyClub payouts are direct-to-account (Stripe Connect Standard/Express) or platform-held; this is the main differentiator to confirm before treating the wedge as open.

## 7. Wedge for an at-cost entrant: what to build first so members never install anything

### Takeaway
Build the member-facing flows as install-free, account-optional web pages (event page with RSVP and pay, shift sign-up page, dues link) that email/text a link, with the organizer's own Stripe account receiving funds, and skip chat entirely at first. This is an inference from the evidence, not a finding.

### Cited Findings (evidence the inference leans on)
- 75% of US adults prefer a mobile website over an app (HostingAdvice, May 2025). — [HostingAdvice](https://www.hostingadvice.com/studies/survey-mandatory-app-downloads/)
- Competing positioning already exists around "no install": Who's In (web push), Invyt, RSVP Street, Spond's email fallback, Heylo's "email, web, or app". — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/alternative-to-groupme), [Nexspark](https://nexspark.org/journal/meetup-alternatives), [Heylo vs Meetup](https://www.heylo.com/alternatives/meet-up)
- GroupMe's SMS shutdown (Aug 2026) leaves text-only members without a path. — [RallyText](https://rallytext.app/groupme-sms-shutdown)
- Meetup-hike switchers cited price and member tech trouble. — [Heylo blog](https://www.heylo.com/blog/meetup-move-homeschool-group)
- Church suites show organizations pay for sign-ups + groups + giving in one place at $10 to $20 per module. — [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/tithely-vs-planning-center/)

### Inferences (ordered)
1. First: a shareable event page with RSVP and optional paid ticket, using the group's own Stripe (Stripe fees only), no member account. Competes with Luma's 5% and Heylo's 5% + $0.59 on cost.
2. Second: volunteer shift sign-up pages (slots, caps, reminders by email/SMS, swap links). No bundled app in my sources positions this as a headline feature; SignUpGenius-type vendors own it as a standalone category.
3. Third: dues as a recurring Stripe link with a simple roster, so the same member record serves events, shifts and dues.
4. Defer chat. Members already sit in WhatsApp/GroupMe/Facebook/Discord; the evidence is that organizers stitch chat to everything else, and replacing it is the heaviest app-install ask.
5. Delivery channel: email first (all competitors fall back to it), SMS link second. Do not rely on web push on iOS (uncited, check).

### Gaps
- No evidence on what organizers would pay or accept for "at cost"; no willingness-to-switch survey found.
- Legal/compliance for adult-only organizers and Stripe Connect onboarding burden for hobby groups not researched.
