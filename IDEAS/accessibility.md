# Idea — Accessibility has no declaration site, and that is the whole finding

**Status: IDEA.** Probed against the tree 2026-09-07 (`VERIFYING.md`).

Accessibility is held here **by discipline**. Every component that needs a role
carries one, `<Form>` resolves a label for every control, the kit puts a caller's
spread on the CONTROL rather than the wrapper precisely so `<label for>` and
`aria-describedby` reach it, `packages/css/src/a11y/` owns the focus ring, and
`basecamp`'s drive asserts a11y on real screens. None of that is written down in
one place, nothing fails when a component forgets, and there is no file to add
the next rule to.

That is not a to-do. **It is a fact with N writers and no origin**, which is the
first of the three shapes every defect in this repo takes, and the one this house
answers with a kit rather than a convention. The interesting part is that it is
the only such fact in the UI realm — everything else there has an owner.

---

## Five kinds, four owners, one of them the seed

The reason no single package has claimed this is that a11y is not one kind of
fact. Grading what is actually being held:

| Kind | Example | Where it can live | Today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Static markup | a dialog needs `role=dialog`, `aria-modal`, and a labelled title | `css` `ANATOMY` | per component, by hand |
| Behavioral state | focus trap, roving tabindex, `aria-expanded` toggling | `ui` | per component, by hand |
| Schema-derived | `<label for>`, `aria-describedby` → the server error, `aria-invalid`, `required` | the seed, through `$context.form` | **built**, and the one kind that already derives |
| Tokens | focus ring, contrast, reduced motion | `css` | **built** — `src/a11y/`, `tokens.css` |
| Page-level | landmarks, heading order, skip links, `lang` on `<html>` | `sierra` | nothing |

**Two of the five already derive from a declaration and three do not**, and the
two that do are the two nobody worries about. That is the argument in one line:
where a declaration exists the problem is solved, and the remaining work is not
*write more aria* but *find the declaration site.*

## Why not `css/vocabulary.js`, the obvious answer

`packages/css/vocabulary.js` is 725 lines, exports `VOCAB` and `ANATOMY`, has
four readers, and is checked in **both directions** against the real CSSOM — a
term with no CSS is a documented component that does not render, a class with no
term is a component two people will call two things. It is the only existing
declaration site of the right kind, and it is the wrong home.

It can hold row one. It cannot see rows two, three and five: it grades
**classes**, and a focus trap is not a class, a `<label for>` comes from the
schema, and a landmark belongs to a route. Putting a11y there would declare the
kind nobody was worried about and leave the other three exactly as they are,
while making the register look complete. `css` is a **reader** of this, not its
owner.

## The shape: a toolbelt kit, three readers

**Same admission `/gate` was made on.** Four hand-copied gate ladders across
three packages, drifted, was the case that put the access scale into
`@frontierjs/toolbelt` (`FJS-D197`); four hand-rolled aria conventions across
`css`, `ui`, `sierra` and the form layer is that shape with a different subject.
The substrate is the only package all four may import (`FJS-D26`), which is the
mechanical reason as well as the doctrinal one.

The kit is a table and pure derivations over it — for a term, what role it
carries, which relationships must exist, and which attributes track which state.
That is a pure function of a term and a state, which is the admission test, and
it is not `createStore` (`FJS-D111`): there is no state in it, only a reading of
one handed in.

The readers are the three packages that hold the three unowned kinds:

- **`css`** — `ANATOMY` already gives one canonical markup block per term. The
  kit is where the roles in that block come from, so the anatomy spec grades
  against a declaration instead of against itself.
- **`ui`** — a component asks the kit what it owes and the kit's answer is what
  the browser drive asserts. This is the row `IDEAS/committed-artifacts.md` B
  and `overview.md` 5.17 already want a column for, and it is the column that
  cannot be generated by compiling a component: what a component **should**
  carry is not recoverable from what it does.
- **`sierra`** — the page-level kind, which today nothing holds at all.

## What it costs, stated

**It does not make anything fail.** A kit is a declaration; the check is
separate, and without one this converts *held by discipline* into *held by
discipline, with a table*. The check is the second half and it is where the
value is: `fli check` is the surface for a rule over an app's own files, and a
component that renders a term without the role the kit names is exactly the sort
of claim that has an authority in the tree.

**It cannot cover contrast, order or focus behavior from a table.** Those are
answerable only in a browser, which is why `ui` and `css` have drives. The kit
is the half that is decidable statically; the drives stay the half that is not,
and pretending otherwise would be a check that fails open.

## Open questions

- **Is `sierra` a reader or does the page-level kind go somewhere else?** A skip
  link and a heading order are properties of a route's rendered output, and
  sierra already taps a prerendered route's reads to grade them against `@@gate`.
  The same tap could grade a page's landmarks. That may be a `fli check` rule
  instead, and the two are not equivalent: one runs at build, one at review.
- **What the kit is called and what one entry holds.** Coining a noun is a
  decision-rules matter and is deliberately not made here.
- **Whether `<Form>`'s existing answer is the model for the rest.** It is the one
  kind that derives today and it works: label, constraints and server error all
  resolved from `$context.form`, with `novalidate` and a named case it cannot
  cover. If the other three kinds can be made to look like that, the kit is
  smaller than this record assumes.
- **Where the WCAG level is stated.** A conformance target is a claim a project
  makes, and the framework shipping one for its consumers is a claim it cannot
  keep. Probably a `README` sentence per package and never a config key.
